WHAT THIS IS AND WHY IT IS BEING AUDITED

  A tool builds an ETABS .e2k from structural plan DXFs so the engineer checks a model instead of
  tracing one off fifteen sheets by hand. Andrea Neuviale is the engineer on 31168.

  said was out of scope, and a wall 132 inches thick. Both survived because the person who built it
  verified his own work and did not open the result. Since then two more faults were found the same
  way -- by looking, not by counting:

    - members carried in from the reference model that no drawing produced, which she circled in

      the publish script had been reporting as stale on every run while copying them in anyway

  Every one of those passed every count in the report. THAT is the fault class. Counts agreeing is
  not evidence, and a check that runs after the thing it checks is a note, not a gate.

  I am the person who missed all four. That is why this is being audited by someone else.

WHERE I WOULD LOOK FIRST, HAVING BEEN WRONG FOUR TIMES

  1. LOOK AT EVERY STOREY. tools\Render-E2kModel.ps1 -E2k <file> -OutPng <png> -Storey "<name>".
     Fifteen renders take over ten minutes; run them one at a time or in the background. A model
     that is wrong in a way counts cannot see is the whole reason this brief exists. Ask of each
     one: is that the shape of that floor, is anything floating, is anything missing that the
     drawing shows.
  2. THE INFERRED PLATE. Exactly ONE storey has no closed slab edge on its own drawings and was
     given another storey's: C-LEVEL 3, from C-LEVEL 4. Flood fill recovers the rest. That plate
     was 336x237 ft -- the whole site, out over the ground the towers stand on -- until this
     session; it is now 206x72 ft. Measure it against C-LEVEL 4's own and against C-LEVEL 3's
     drawing. It is the single most likely remaining defect.
  3. THE COUNT THAT LOOKS LIKE A FAULT AND IS NOT. The report says 15 storeys and 14 floors, and
     that reads exactly like a storey that lost its floor. It is a count of plate OBJECTS, and
     C-LEVEL 3's borrowed plate is C-LEVEL 4's object assigned a second time. Check it per storey,
     not by the totals -- I got this wrong once already today. If any storey carrying members has
     no floor assign, that IS a fault.
  4. A-LEVEL 1 carries 6 walls and ZERO columns, while the report says 128 members drawn on a
     sheet placed there were dropped as already-filled. Is its structure genuinely modelled on
     B-LEVEL 1 at the same elevation, or has a storey quietly lost its columns? I believe the
     former and have not proved it.
  5. THE SUMMARY PDF AGAINST THE MODEL. Read the PDF as TEXT, not the HTML it came from, and check
     every number in it against the .e2k. A stale dossier claiming 1,119 walls sat in this folder
     for nine days because the check that caught it ran after the copy.
  6. THE WORKBOOK. Two questions is the target. Anything a reader could answer from the drawings
     is a question that should not be there -- eighteen rows asking about 2-4 inch drafting scratch
     went to her once.
  7. Plate edges recovered by flood fill are traced off a raster and come out slightly stepped. Is
     that acceptable to hand over, or does it read as sloppy?
