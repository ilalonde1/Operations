Audit the DXF → ETABS generator against the product it is supposed to be, and close what you
find. The standard and the current gap inventory are both written down; your job is to test
both, not to inherit either.

START HERE

  docs/KOR-DxfToEtabs-PLAN-AND-GAPS.md

  Part 1 is the product standard: what this tool must be, what it is given, what it returns, and
  the three properties — agnostic, honest, learning — that separate a tool from a rescue written
  around two buildings.

  Part 2 is MY inventory of what does not yet meet that standard, with the measurement behind
  each. TREAT IT AS A CLAIM, NOT A BRIEF. A gap I have stated wrongly, or a gap I have missed
  entirely, is worth more to me than one you confirm. If an item is already closed, say so and
  show the measurement. If my reasoning behind one is faulty, say that too — twice tonight a
  rule I "fixed" turned out to be right for a reason I had recorded falsely, and finding that
  out was worth more than the fix.

RULES OF EVIDENCE

  1. Do not treat this tool's report, its documents, or its test suite as evidence that anything
     is true. They are claims by the thing under audit. Measure instead.

  2. Objects named "K" followed by a letter and digits were written by this tool. Some files in
     the two job folders are its own output round-tripped through ETABS. Establish provenance
     before using any file as ground truth.

  3. Two buildings are not evidence about a third. There are roughly 1,400 engineer-authored
     models under \\Kor-fs01\Projects\Projects. A rule justified from 31168 and 31138 alone is
     unverified however green the suite is — every threshold measured that way tonight was
     either wrong or resting on a false reason.

  4. State the file and the numbers behind every finding. A defect read off code is not a
     finding until you say what it does to a model.

  5. Where you cannot determine something, say so and say what would settle it.

WHAT I MOST WANT

  1. The class of fault that has cost the most here is GEOMETRY THAT LEAVES WITHOUT TELLING
     ANYONE. Three found in the last two days: block INSERTs never read at all; a footprint
     outside the column size rules returning silently; and the same edges drawn twice on two
     layers of one family, building 45 members twice on a job we had shipped. Every one passed a
     green suite. Look for the next one.

  2. Whether anything is still shaped by these two buildings. The engine's geometry carries no
     job numbers, but the publish gate still holds an allowlist of their historical figures and a
     positional check that assumes the summary table's left column is 31168.

  3. Whether the model is right, judged against the DRAWINGS. Both are readable as text on the
     share. A member on the wrong storey, at the wrong size, of the wrong kind, or in a place the
     drawings do not show it, is the class no count can see.

  4. The learning loop under stress. It works — answering a question and importing it moved
     geometry and moved it back. Try to break it: two questions writing the same setting, an
     answer that does not parse, a setting whose units disagree, an import run twice.

CONSTRAINTS

  - The test suite requires KOR_ENGINEERINGTOOLS_STANDARDSDB. Set it process-local; do not setx
    it onto the machine. Connection details are in the file the user gave you.
  - Rules live in KorStandards, not in code. A rule change is a migration in
    C:\VIsual Studio Projects\KOR.Drafter\db\ — never a constant. There is no fallback value by
    design: a missing rule stops the run.
  - Do not modify anything under \\Kor-fs01 except by running the publish script for the two jobs
    it already names.
  - Run the FULL suite before claiming anything about it — about eight minutes — and say plainly
    if you did not. A filtered run says nothing about the models.
  - If you change a rule value or a baseline, the reason and the counts go in the file beside it.
    A number nobody can check is the thing we have spent two days removing.

OUTPUT

  Findings worst first: what is wrong, the numbers you measured, what it costs the engineer, how
  confident you are. Then a second pass trying to knock down your own findings, and say which
  survive. Four that hold beat twenty that read well.

  Then what you changed and what you verified it against. Then, separately: which items in Part 2
  you found to be already closed, wrongly stated, or missing.
