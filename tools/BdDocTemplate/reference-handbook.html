The big idea, in three sentences

Every firm's CRM dies the same way: it becomes a chore nobody does. This one is built to survive that, by asking almost nothing of you.

  • Capture is one decision. You supply the fact only you have — "I called Dave, we lost to Bush Bohlman." The system fills in the rest: when, who Dave is, which pursuit, the source, the region.
  • The context is already there. Open a pursuit and you see the buyer's billing history, our past wins with them, how recently we've emailed them, and who else grabbed the plans — with zero typing.
  • The system chases you. Deadlines and cold pursuits come to you in the morning email you already read. You don't go hunting a dashboard.

✕ What you'll never be asked to do

Fill in a mandatory field. Build a pipeline. Score a lead by hand. BCC the CRM. Do "data hygiene." If a screen ever feels like busywork, you're probably on the wrong one — check the map.

The shape of the work

How a pursuit lives

Everything follows the same three beats. You touch it at the start and the end; the middle mostly runs itself.

1

Grab

Claim a tender from Grab Opportunities. It becomes yours and turns into a Drafting pursuit.

2

Work

Log the odd call, send a fee proposal, watch the context assemble itself.

3

Close

Mark it Won or Lost. Capture the one thing only you know — who beat us, or which project it became.

A pursuit's stage is shown as a coloured pill, the same everywhere in the app:

Drafting Submitted Won Lost

1 · Find work

Grab an opportunity

Go to Grab Opportunities. It's a ranked list of every open tender nobody at KOR has claimed — pulled automatically from BC Bid, CanadaBuys, SAM.gov and 100+ other feeds. The most relevant ones are up top, tagged High or Medium.

Before you claim — look first

Double-click a row to open the buyer's profile: who the key people are, our track record with them, live signals. It stops you claiming blind.

Claim it

Select the row and click Grab selected, confirm, and it's yours. It leaves the list, becomes a Drafting pursuit you own, and the app drops you straight into it.

✓ Good to know

If two of you grab the same tender at once, only one wins and the other is told honestly — nobody ends up with a phantom claim.

2 · Work it

Work the pursuit — what you get for free

Open any pursuit in Pursuits. The left side is the list of everything you own; the right side is the detail. The list opens on your live work by default — click the coloured pills to filter, type in the search box, or hit Mine to see only yours.

▸ On the pursuit, with zero typing, you'll see

  • Deltek history — has this client paid us before, how much, how recently. The gold strip near the top.
  • Email warmth — e.g. "Filed correspondence with @acme.com: last 12d ago · 9 in 90d · mostly j.smith@acme.com." Computed nightly from every filed email.
  • Plan takers — who else pulled the plans on this RFP.
  • History line — how it came to you and how long it's been sitting ("Grabbed by you · In Drafting 12d").

The buttons across the top

Kept deliberately short — the two you'll use most, plus a More ▾ menu for the rest:

  • Edit… — owner, fee, notes, stage.
  • Set stage — move it forward (Drafting → Submitted → Won / Lost).
  • More ▾ — everything else, one click away: Buyer profile (the full dossier on this buyer), Project brief (a one-page PDF for a meeting or a go / no-go), Link to project, and start a Fee Proposal or Brochure pre-named for this pursuit.

The one-minute job

Log a call, meeting, or email

This is the only regular typing the system asks of you, and it's built to take seconds. On the pursuit, in the Activity log row: pick the type (Call, Meeting, Email…), type a subject, press Enter. Done.

✓ Two shortcuts worth knowing

  • Rang someone Friday, logging it Monday? Use the date picker — it records when it actually happened.
  • Point it at the contact it was with from the dropdown. Optional, but it makes the record richer.

Adding a contact

Same detail panel, the Contacts box: name, role, email, phone, and tick primary if they're the main person. Add.

Attach a file, email — anything

Same detail panel, the Files box: drag files straight onto it — a PDF, a saved email, a drawing, a photo, even a video — or click Browse…. They're kept on the shared BD drive with the pursuit, so anyone working it can Open them and nothing important lives only on your laptop.

◇ You don't have to log everything

When you save a fee proposal from a pursuit, it logs itself as an activity. The system captures the big moments so you don't have to remember to.

3 · Close it

Close it — Won or Lost

Click Set stage and pick the outcome. This is the moment that matters most — it's when you know the one fact the system can't get anywhere else.

Won

Mark it Won, then use Link to project… (under More ▾) to tie it to the Deltek project it became. Pick from the client's projects, or just type the WBS1. This is what connects "we chased it" to "we're building it" — so every win is accounted for end to end.

Lost

Mark it Lost and one small box appears: who beat us (start typing the firm), a one-line reason, and a Skip button that's one click. Nothing is mandatory — but the ten seconds it takes is the only time anyone will ever know who won it.

✓ It cleans up after you

Marking a pursuit Won or Lost stamps the close date and files the outcome automatically. Reopen it later and it clears cleanly — no stale "lost to X" hanging around on a live pursuit.

Deliverables

Make a proposal, brochure, or brief

  • Fee proposal / brochure — from a pursuit, open More ▾ and pick Fee Proposal… or Brochure…. It opens pre-named for that pursuit; build it out in the usual builder.
  • Project brief — Project brief… (under More ▾) produces a one-page PDF pulling the RFP, the buyer, and the market context together. Perfect before a call or a bid decision.
  • Org / region / person / project brief — the Make a Brief destination, for when you want a write-up that isn't tied to a single tender.

When it's not in the feed

Enter an opportunity by hand

Heard about something the feeds haven't caught? In RFPs, click New, fill in the name and buyer (the rest is optional), and Save.

▸ The system watches for duplicates

As you type, if it looks like something we already have, you'll see a quiet warning. On Save, it shows you the likely matches — tagged Likely or Possible — with three choices:

  • Open the existing one — it was already there; jump to it.
  • Save as new anyway — genuinely different; create it.
  • Cancel — go back to editing.

It even catches ones that are already closed, so you don't re-enter a job we already won or lost. You're never blocked — it just makes sure you meant to.

If you run the team

For managers — Pursuit Monitor

Go to Pursuit Monitor for one board of every owned, active pursuit — coldest first, so whatever's going stale floats to the top. Each row shows a staleness dot and how long it's sat in its current stage.

Fresh <7d Watch 7–21d Cold 21d+

"Staleness" counts the most recent real touch — a logged call or a filed email — so a pursuit isn't marked cold just because nobody typed a note, when correspondence is actually flowing.

  • Double-click a row to open and manage that pursuit.
  • Reassign hands it to someone else from the firm roster and emails them. It won't let you reassign a pursuit that's already closed.

While you sleep

What happens without you

◇ The morning email

Every morning a report lands in the inbox you already read. It opens with owned pursuits closing soon — anything with a deadline inside two weeks, red if it's under five days — so nothing dies quietly. Mondays it adds open rosters and pre-quals worth chasing.

  • New tenders arrive in Grab Opportunities around the clock, scored and matched to a buyer, with no one entering them.
  • Email warmth refreshes every night from the filed-email record, so "how warm is this client" is always current on the pursuit.
  • Fee proposals log themselves against the pursuit when you save them.

Trust what you see

Reading the numbers honestly

The CRM will tell you it holds 252 engagements, but it won't pretend that's a 100% win rate. 177 of those are historical Deltek wins imported to give you context — clearly labelled as backfill, with no owner and no matching losses.

So the win-rate line reads something like "live: 0W / 0L · live win rate — (no live outcomes yet)" and fills in for real only as pursuits you actually grab reach an outcome. The rule of the house: honest numbers or no numbers. If a figure would be misleading, the app says so instead of showing it.

✓ The one habit that matters

Grab the work you're chasing, and log the moment it closes. That's it. Everything else the system either does for you or can wait. The two events it's really watching for are your first grab and your first logged call — once those feel easy, the rest follows.
