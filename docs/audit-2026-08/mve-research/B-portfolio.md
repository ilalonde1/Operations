## How this was built, and how to read it

**Three primary sources, in order of authority.**

1. **MVE's own live site** (`mve-architects.com`), fetched 2026-08-25. Their 2026 redesign publishes **74
   project pages**, each carrying a structured **Client / Program / Services / Typology / Site Area**
   block. That block is MVE stating its own role in its own words, and it is the strongest evidence in
   this document. Their portfolio map data file (`/data/projects.geojson`) carries **68 records**,
   including projects with no published page.
2. **MVE's own archived site.** The 2026 redesign retired roughly half the firm's published history —
   the pre-2026 site carried **151 project pages** at `/projects/{slug}`, all now returning 404.
   **140 were recovered** from the Internet Archive and are cited here as **ARCHIVED MVE PAGE**. This
   is where the student housing, civic, transit and China work lives. It is still MVE's own copy about
   its own work; it is simply no longer on display.
3. **MVE's own blog** (`mve-architects.com/about/blog`), 12 posts, Dec 2024 – Aug 2026, plus dated trade
   press and city records for anything current.

**Evidence tags.** **VERIFIED** — MVE's own live page, MVE's own blog, or a client's or award body's own
page. **ARCHIVED MVE PAGE** — MVE's own words, recovered from the Internet Archive; treated as near-primary
but flagged because it may be superseded. **REPORTED** — press, aggregator or third-party database.
**INFERRED** — labelled reasoning, nothing more. **UNRESOLVED** — could not be established, and is not
guessed at.

### The attribution rule this document is written under

**Master planner ≠ design architect ≠ architect of record ≠ executive architect ≠ interior designer.**
MVE's own pages distinguish these, and in several cases MVE's page names *another firm* as design
architect. Those cases are reproduced exactly as MVE states them, because getting them wrong in front of
the people who did the work is the one unrecoverable error.

> **The Riverwalk rule.** At Riverwalk San Diego, **MVE is the master-plan and entitlements architect.**
> MVE's own project page lists its services as *"Master Planning, Entitlements"* — not architecture.
> **The 721-unit market-rate Village/Central Village towers are Gensler's, not MVE's.** The only Riverwalk
> *building* this document attributes to MVE is **The Becker**, which MVE's own page and blog claim
> directly. **VERIFIED** — mve-architects.com/portfolio/riverwalk, fetched 2026-08-25.

## 18. What the portfolio says about the firm

**MVE is a housing firm that plans at district scale.** The count is not close: multifamily and mixed-use
residential is roughly three-quarters of everything they have published, and the master-planning practice
is what lets them keep the architecture — Riverwalk, OC Vibe, The Village, The Park, Silo Park, Downtown
Daybreak, Seabridge and Playa Vista II all show the same move, where MVE writes the plan and then designs
buildings inside it.

**The Los Angeles high-rise practice is the newer specialism and it is substantial** — eight towers of 20+
storeys, four of them 36 or taller. It sits alongside a wood-podium and Type III practice that goes back
decades. **Both matter, and they are engineered nothing alike.**

**Geography has moved decisively.** Orange County remains the home base and the Irvine Company relationship
is 45 years deep, but **Salt Lake City is now the second centre of gravity** — twelve projects, roughly
4,100 units, and a single developer relationship (Lowe Property Group with Blaser Ventures) behind most of
it. Add Utah's **Downtown Daybreak** and **Belhaven**, Arizona's **Legacy Park** and **ASU**, and Nevada's
**Park Lane**, and the Intermountain West is no longer an excursion. The **San Diego office opened July
2025** on the back of a 40-year, 30-project regional history.

**The 2026 website redesign is itself a finding.** It cut the published portfolio roughly in half and
removed **two entire sectors** — student housing (about 9,000 beds across thirteen campuses) and
institutional/civic (schools, a police headquarters, theatres, churches). What remains is almost purely
housing, mixed-use, master planning, hospitality and a new automotive line. **That is a firm deliberately
narrowing its public identity to the market it intends to win in.** Do not tell them their student housing
is missing — but know that if the conversation reaches UC, CSU or a campus client, there are 9,000 beds of
history behind it that their website no longer mentions.
