The market with no permit feed, and a better record anyway

Hawaii publishes its pipeline before design, in one statewide bulletin

Hawaii looked like the hardest of the six. Honolulu’s open data stops at 2016 permits, and its live zoning layers record ordinances that have already passed — the outcome, not the pipeline. The answer is not at the county at all. It is a state publication, and it is better than anything the counties hold.

Under Chapter 343, any project using state or county land or money, touching a shoreline or conservation district, or needing a general plan amendment must publish an environmental assessment. The Office of Planning and Sustainable Development gathers these into The Environmental Notice, published on the 8th and 23rd of every month, covering all four counties in one document. Each entry names, in a fixed order: every permit still required, the approving agency with a named officer, the applicant, the planning consultant, the status, and a description that usually gives the unit count.

An environmental assessment is prepared to win entitlements. It comes before construction documents, and the consultant named on it is a planner, not an architect.

✕ What this bulletin does not reach

Chapter 343 is triggered by state or county involvement. A wholly private project, on private land, with no shoreline or conservation trigger, never appears — Ward Village is exactly that kind of project, which is why your own Honolulu work is not in it. The Kakaʻako pipeline runs instead through the Hawaiʻi Community Development Authority, and no new development permit is before its board for the 2 September meeting.

The thing you asked for, and an honest read on it

Phoenix submitted projects — and which layer of the record can actually be acted on

Phoenix publishes a live plan-review service, row-level, reachable without an account. Queried for site plans it returns 373 open cases — 281 preliminary and 92 final. That is the search you asked for and the numbers are exact. It is also, for finding unlet work, close to useless, and the reason is worth a minute of your time.

A site plan is a drawing. To file one, somebody has to have drawn the buildings. So all 373 of those cases have a design team already, whether or not the city names it — and preliminary versus final only tells you how much is still moveable, not whether an architect exists. It is the same reason we do not quote you Houston’s state registrations, which the design firm files, or Miami’s design-review packets, which are the architect’s own drawings.

The best-built record of the six, and an honest read on what is in it

Houston names the developer on every filing — and most of what it names is land

Houston has no zoning, and its state registrations are filed by the design firm, which puts every one of them after the appointment. The Planning Commission’s own plat spreadsheets are the opposite: filed by the developer’s planner or engineer to divide land, before anything is designed. 1,426 applications this year, with the developer company named on 100% of them — along with the filing consultancy, a named applicant, an office phone, acreage, land use and council district. Nothing else in the six markets is built like it.

Now the part that matters more than the record. Of those 1,426: 35 are general plans, which divide land for master-planned communities — Bridgeland’s next village alone is about 7,000 homes going to production builders, so there is no architectural commission in it. 41 are tagged multi-unit or mixed-use, and 31 of those sit under a half-acre — townhouse and duplex infill on single lots. Five are multi-unit at a scale that implies a building somebody has to design.

▲ What a 600-acre general plan is, and is not

Brookfield’s 591-acre Midline, Johnson Development’s 696-acre Amira and Taylor Morrison’s 262-acre Avalon at Cypress are all on this year’s record with no architect named. None is required. A Chapter 42 general plan divides land into streets, blocks and reserves; the homes that follow are production builders’ own designs. These filings tell you where capital is going in Houston. They are not work.
