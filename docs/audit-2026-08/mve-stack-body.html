The product

Mosaic, in depth

A resource and capacity-planning SaaS built for architecture and engineering firms. Not an ERP and not a project-management tool — deliberately narrow. It sits on top of the finance system and integrates with Deltek, Unanet, BST Global and QuickBooks. Pricing is quote-only behind a demo request; no public figure.

What it sells, by its own feature list: project portfolio management, time tracking and timesheets, project and resource planning, project time-budget tracking, workload / utilization / budget / variance reports, hiring and forecasting reports, and custom project data fields.

What it actually does, in its own words:

  • Backlog in real time — “monitor your backlog in real-time, so you know whether you can take on more, have enough, or are overloaded and need to hire — including what role and when.”
  • Hiring by role and date — “Stop guessing when it’s time to hire. Mosaic’s AI forecasts workload and analyzes role demand so you know exactly when to hire.”
  • Utilization without burnout — who is over- and under-loaded, across teams, roles and offices.
  • Plan versus actual — “compares project plans against actuals to improve the accuracy of your estimated project hours, budgets, and schedules.”
  • Scenario modelling — test hiring, pipeline and delivery scenarios before committing.

Resource management is just one small module — and it shows… ERPs were designed for the finance close, not the Monday-morning plan.” They call themselves “the operational planning layer your ERP was never built to be.”

Which is why the “native Deltek reporting is painful” line in the 19 August email was safe. MVE’s chosen vendor makes the same argument far harder, and MVE agreed with it enough to buy. Dan did not defend Deltek in his reply — he routed the question to Ken.

The inference

What buying Mosaic reveals about MVE

Nobody buys a narrow capacity tool when capacity planning is working.

Mosaic is narrow, and what it is narrow about is the diagnosis. MVE bought it and hired someone to run it, which means they were not confident about four things: how much work is genuinely committed, who is over and under loaded, which roles to add and when, and how plans compare to actuals.

They have instrumented their busyness. That is better corroboration of Dan’s “we’re busy” than any published billings figure, and it means Ken can answer “where do you get tight, and in which roles?” precisely — because it is literally his tool’s output.

▲ The commercial consequence — the most useful line in this document

When Jim says “we have resources available to help you,” MVE’s internal answer is decided by Mosaic, which forecasts role demand by date and names the role to hire. So the offer competes with a hire, not with another engineer.

Pitch it as flex capacity that absorbs a peak without adding headcount — that lands against a gap their own system is already showing them, and it is the one thing a hire cannot do quickly. It generalises: any firm running resource-planning software is making a buy-versus-hire decision, not a buy-versus-buy one.

The fair summary: Mosaic is a deep, narrow tool for one job KOR has not built — planning people forward. The KOR suite is broad across finance, portfolio analytics, BD and engineering, where Mosaic does nothing at all. The honest line, if it comes up: “we measure utilization, we don’t plan it forward — that is what you bought Mosaic for.” Saying that costs nothing and buys credibility with the one person who would know.

Restraint

Three things not to say about our own suite

From KOR’s own August audit of the Financials module, whose demo-risk ranking exists for exactly this reason. None of it bites on Thursday because the agreed position is verbal with no screens — but Ken is precisely the person who asks “as of when?”

✕ The three

  • Do not say “real-time.” Most headline figures are as of February 2026 — WIP, cash, backlog, P&L, revenue trends. Only AR, utilization and firm health are genuinely current. If numbers come up, lead with those three.
  • Do not quote a WIP figure. With Revenue Generation off, both branches draw from 238 residual rows — 0.5% of the table. The tile is already hidden. The audit’s own answer is the strong one: “Deltek isn’t running revenue recognition, so we don’t publish a WIP number we can’t stand behind.” That reads as rigour, not as a gap.
  • Do not invite a live P&L comparison. The AI returns a different P&L than the screen does, and two different FX rates sit in one window.

⚠ One more, relevant only if anything is ever shown: the Financials access gate fails open when the AD lookup fails, which force-shows Financials and Compensation — salary data. It is another reason the no-screens position is right.

On the call

How to handle it

  • Ask, don’t assert. “What did you end up putting around Vantagepoint to get the reporting you needed?” He will tell you about Mosaic, and you will be agreeing with him rather than about him.
  • Then the useful one. “Where does your resource picture get tight over the next couple of quarters — which roles?” Easy for him, precise for us, and it opens the door for “we have resources available.”
  • Never characterise Mosaic or his setup. He has spent a year building it and sits on the vendor’s advisory council. Criticising either reads as criticising his work.
  • Keep the Deltek point where it already is — on native reporting, which is what was actually written, and which his own vendor argues harder.
  • Concede the gap deliberately. Admitting KOR does not plan staffing forward is cheap, true, and makes everything else more believable.
