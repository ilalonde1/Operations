Ground truth

What CoStar actually is, and what it costs

CoStar Suite is a commercial-property research and prospecting database covering the US, Canada, UK and France — over 300,000 active users and forty years of history. It is the reference tool for brokers, lenders, appraisers and investment teams. Dan has it for the entire United States, got it as a condition of joining MVE thirteen years ago, and his BD staff use it under him.

Pricing modelCustom and opaque. It does not reduce to a per-seat number — the quote is driven by which markets you take, which modules, and how many users. National coverage sits at the top of the range.

✕ Do not conflate two different products

CoStar Real Estate Manager is a separate lease-administration and accounting product, quoted at $75,000–$200,000/yr mid-market and $200,000–$500,000+ enterprise. That is not what Dan is describing. Quoting those numbers at him would be wrong and he would know immediately.

How CoStar models a project. Its development pipeline runs four phases — Unconfirmed → Planning → Final Planning → In Construction — tracked by construction status, expected delivery date and unit or room counts, sliceable by class, market, submarket and brand. CoStar research staff do talk to architects, developers and planning departments, but they do so to firm up delivery dates. That distinction is the whole opportunity.

The gap

The design team is not a CoStar entity

CoStar’s unit of analysis is the asset and its economics. Ours is the project team.

Everything CoStar is built to return describes a building and its money: who owns it, who leases it, what it sold for, what it will rent for, when it delivers. That is the correct design for its customers — brokers, lenders, appraisers, investors. None of them need to know who the architect is.

So the fields that matter to a firm trying to get hired onto a project — architect, structural engineer, civil, landscape, general contractor, and above all whether a seat has been filled yet — are not what CoStar indexes. Dan can tell you a 400-unit project in Tucson was submitted and who the developer is. He cannot filter for “projects where no architect has been appointed.”

▲ Which is the pitch, and it is not a software pitch

Dan does not need better developer contacts — that is where CoStar is strongest and where he is already good. What he cannot buy is the consultant layer: who is already on a project, who is not, and which developers habitually use which architects. For a BD director at an architecture firm that is a displacement map, and for KOR the same data with the structural row is a target list.

▲ The buy recommendation — BEX, not another scraper

AZBEX is subscription-gated, and testing established that its free pages are stubs. Ten current August 2026 articles were pulled; two carried an architect in the teaser and the rest returned a sign-up form where the body should be. So every “no architect named” from AZBEX is paywall truncation, not absence — and reporting it as absence would be the same error three times over.

The right response is not a better scraper. The same publisher (BEX) covers Arizona and Nevada — AZBEX, NVBEX, and DATABEX, a project database covering every Arizona commercial and infrastructure job over $5M. Two of Dan’s six regions from one subscription, with the design team attached. Pricing is quote-only — bex@azbex.com, 480-709-4190, and they advertise bundle pricing across the three products. Worth a call before another week of research.

⚠ Arizona has no equivalent of San Diego’s permit feed. San Diego republishes its full development-approvals table daily — 834,000 rows with status, valuation, storeys and unit mix. Phoenix publishes aggregate counts only: 23 rows of yearly permit totals. Its proposed-zoning layer gives case numbers and acreage but no project name, developer or unit count. So Arizona cannot be extracted; it has to be researched. Worth knowing before promising a live feed.

✕ The obvious reading of this table is wrong

The two six-project firms look like the competitive pressure. Neither is. Butler Design Group is not a design-build firm at all — six projects, six different contractors (Stevens-Leinweber, Ryan ×3, Willmeng, Layton). And by sector it is four industrial, one healthcare, one civic: zero multifamily. LGE genuinely is design-build, and five of its six are industrial.

Design-build takes 50% of Arizona industrial in this set and 8% of multifamily — one project in twelve — and 0% of civic, healthcare and hospitality. The threat is real and it is not in MVE’s sector.

Read that as a BD director would. Only 12 of the 50 are multifamily, and those twelve went to eleven different architects — Gensler has two and nobody has three. Arizona multifamily has no incumbent. KTGY, Niles Bolton and ESG each hold exactly one: the national multifamily names are present in Arizona and have not taken it.

⭐ Four developers are structurally closed and should not absorb BD time: Creation Equity (6 projects, the most active in the set, LGE on all six, no outside architect ever) · Ryan Companies US (developer and GC on all four, brings Butler on three) · Statesman Group (developer and GC, no architect named) · and in Houston, StreetLights Residential (architect, interiors and GC all in-house). The Butler–Ryan pairing is the only architect-to-contractor relationship that repeats anywhere in the fifty.

Recommendation

What to send, and what to claim

  • Lead with the corrections, not the volume. Dan told us Santa Clara Park is AO’s and that our headcount figure was modelled. Sending those back fixed proves the “it learns and it is never wrong again” claim better than any new document. It costs nothing and it is the most credible thing we can do this week.
  • Send Arizona as a sample, framed as the design-team layer. Fifty projects with forty-nine design teams, plus the architect league table. Say what it is: assembled from public trade sources, not a live feed, and skewed to commercial rather than multifamily.
  • Say the honest thing about his 40-storey test. A filing made that same day is not in any public feed yet; it surfaces when the city posts it or the trade press picks it up, typically within days. The defensible claim is that nothing published gets missed — not that nothing unpublished is known. He is a professional; the honest answer will land better than a dodge.
  • Do not offer a CoStar replacement. He pays for national coverage and forty years of comps. What we have is a different axis, not a cheaper version of his. The line is “this is the layer your CoStar does not carry” — and on that ground we are genuinely ahead.
