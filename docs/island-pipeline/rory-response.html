Correction

Starlight — we had this wrong, and you caught it

You said David Woo works for Starlight and that invest and dev are the same or linked. You are right. The document told you the opposite three versions running, and the cause was our own data.

✕ What we told you

That Starlight Development — our client — and Starlight Investments, the REIT behind Harris Green, were unrelated firms that happened to share a first word. We flagged it as a trap to avoid.

▸ What is actually true

They are one relationship. The evidence was in our own files the whole time:

  • The projects we have billed that client for are 2740 Spencer Rd Langford, 1701 Cedar Hill Rd Saanich, Quadra East and West in Victoria, plus Burnaby and West Vancouver. Urban multi-family — nothing like a retreat fund.
  • Every contact we hold on that client is @starlightinvest.com, including Daniel Drimmer, the CEO of Starlight Investments.
  • Starlight Investments' own announcements name "The District" in Langford. That is our project at 2740 Spencer Rd.
  • David Woo is their VP Development, Western Canada.

So the six-storey heritage-retention building at 350 Douglas St is being filed by our own client's VP of Development, and we described him as an unrelated party. That is the single most costly line in the document and it is now gone.

▸ How it happened, and why the correction is worth more than the error cost

Every firm in our system carries a website, and that website is how the system decides two records are the same company. Our Starlight client record had been given the wrong website — a similarly-named American retreat fund. Everything downstream then reasoned correctly from a bad premise: the system compared two web addresses, saw two different companies, and confidently told us so. Nothing errored, which is exactly why it survived three drafts.

Your correction did more than fix one line. That website is now right, the two duplicate Starlight records are merged into one with 118 contacts on it, and the reasoning that produced the error is written into the system as a permanent warning so it cannot be repeated on this firm. Nine other Island firms were found fragmented the same way in the same pass and merged. That is the return on telling us we are wrong: one flag fixes the instance, and usually a class of them.
