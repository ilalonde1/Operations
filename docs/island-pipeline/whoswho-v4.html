What v4 adds — and the one finding that matters most

v3 read Victoria and Nanaimo. v4 reads Saanich, Langford and Colwood as well — the three municipalities that, with Victoria, account for most of Greater Victoria's housing starts. That took the file from the 997 Victoria and Nanaimo applications v3 could see to 2,335, of which 1,626 are BD-relevant once single-lot house permits are set aside, and 837 were filed in the last twelve months.

▸ The finding

Saanich publishes the applicant's firm name — and our two closest competitors are in it. RJC Engineers is named as the applicant on four Saanich commercial permits. Sense Engineering — a Victoria-headquartered structural firm with 130 staff that v2 missed entirely — is on another. So are WSP, Stantec, Arcadis, Herold, EVOKE, LIA and McCuaig.

This is the first time we have been able to see, from a public record rather than from inference, who is sitting in the engineering seat on a specific Island building. It is also a warning: RJC and Sense are not visiting the Island, they are filing in it.

Four things are new and none of them existed in v3:

  • A price on the work. Langford publishes a construction valuation with every permit. 293 priced applications totalling $326,163,279, including eight at $10M or more. No other Island city publishes this, and it is the first hard dollar figure this programme has produced.
  • Competitors visible as applicants — the section immediately below. Saanich's file names the professional who filed, not just the owner.
  • Saanich is the largest single file on the Island — 960 applications, more than Victoria, Langford and Colwood put together, and current to today.
  • Depth on the firms. 75 firms from the permit file are now decomposed in the BD platform with 1,821 named people attached — 479 across 26 architecture practices, 271 across the three competitor firms, 425 inside eight existing KOR clients.

✕ A v3 claim this supersedes

v3 said dHKarchitects was "our one architect client." With Saanich and Colwood added, the ledger shows three: Mallen Gowing Berzins (CL00257, six KOR projects, last August 2025), dHKarchitects (one job, July 2025) and D'Ambrosio / DAU Studio (in Deltek, no job yet). The correction runs in our favour.

Competitive · new in v4

Caught in the act: competitors filing as the applicant

Saanich requires the applicant of record on a commercial permit, and publishes it. Where the applicant is an engineering firm, that firm is the professional of record on the building. This is the closest thing to a scoreboard this market produces.

  • RJC Engineers — 4 applications, Feb to Jul 2025. Our most direct competitor. 770 staff, 311 of them engineers, employee-owned, founded 1948, with a Nanaimo office. Their own published capability list leads with structural, seismic, structural restoration, heritage restoration and mass timber — that is KOR's list, in KOR's order.
  • Sense Engineering — 1 application, Apr 2025. A second direct structural competitor, headquartered in Victoria, and absent from v2 entirely. Founded 2014 by Brennan Vollering and Ted Denniston. 130 staff, headcount up 29% over 24 months. Structural, envelope, restoration, seismic assessment, capital planning. Ten offices, Victoria listed first.
  • Herold Engineering — 1 application, Dec 2025. Nanaimo civil and structural, the recurring structural partner on Westmark projects — and now filing in Saanich as well.
  • McCuaig & Associates — 1 application, Sep 2022. Building science, concrete restoration and heritage restoration. 42 staff, +39% over 24 months. Overlaps our heritage lane rather than our new-build one.
  • EVOKE Buildings Engineering — 1 application, May 2025. Envelope and building science, employee-owned, founded 2020. Adjacent to us rather than head-on — but in the room early, which is the position we want.
  • WSP Canada (Apr 2025) and Stantec (Dec 2024) — 1 application each. National multi-disciplinaries: present on the Island, not resident on it. Stantec has also teamed with Studio 531 on a transportation terminal.
  • Arcadis Architects Canada — 1 application, Jun 2025. Filed as the architect, not the engineer — worth noting because it means the engineering seat on 2410 Lee Ave was open, and RJC took it three weeks earlier.
  • LIA Engineering — 1 application, Mar 2025. Small local practice, no Apollo record; named from the city's own file.

✕ The honest limit on this section

Langford publishes the price but not the applicant. All 349 Langford rows have an empty applicant field — the city's feed carries no name, agent or contact. So this section tells you what and how much, and never who. Closing that needs the same route as Nanaimo: the council and committee agendas, which name the applicant on rezonings and development permits.

Valuation is the owner's declared construction cost for permit-fee purposes, not a contract value and not a fee base. Use it to rank, not to quote.

The thread

The thread: timber, seismic, and existing buildings

v2 and v3 both identified mass timber as the Island's institutional pattern. That still holds — both flagship post-secondary projects on the Island are timber: VIU Nanaimo's 266-bed 10-storey hybrid CLT (opens summer 2027) and Camosun Lansdowne's 423-bed six-storey mass-timber build, $154.7M, LEED Platinum, ready early 2029.

What v4 adds is that the competition has organised around the same three words we would use. RJC's published capability list leads with structural, seismic, structural restoration, heritage restoration and mass timber. Sense Engineering's leads with structural, envelope, restoration and seismic assessment. McCuaig's leads with heritage restoration and existing-building assessment. Three of the four engineering firms filing on the Island are selling seismic work on existing buildings, which is the same market the Victoria General Hospital energy centre and the Island's heritage stock create.

On the design side the pattern is unchanged and corroborated: Low Hammond Rowe (Saanich, 23 staff, Passive House and hybrid mass timber, headcount +58% over 24 months), Cascadia and Fold as Passive House practices, Studio 531 at +75%, dHKarchitects at +62%. The Island's design bench is being rebuilt around performance and timber, and the firms doing it are growing fast enough to need consultants they have not used before.

✕ Two gaps in the Nanaimo data, unchanged from v3

No applicant contacts. Nanaimo publishes no per-application detail page, so there is no equivalent of Saanich's or Victoria's named agent. The Nanaimo rows tell you what and where, never who.

No application date. All 876 rows are undated — the feed carries no date field, so Nanaimo rows cannot be sorted or aged. File numbers rise over time and are the only ordering available. Nanaimo is therefore excluded from every "last twelve months" figure in this document.

Method

How this document was produced

v2 was assembled by research. v3 and v4 were assembled by a pipeline that runs on its own. This section exists so you can judge how much to trust each number — and so the next person can reproduce it.

1

Read each city's own file

All five publish their development applications as machine-readable services. We read them directly. A rezoning spanning nine parcels is published as nine rows, so rows are collapsed to real applications before anything is counted — 2,335 applications from a considerably larger row count.

2

Filter to buildings

A relevance gate drops sign variances, fences and awnings. It was tightened during this build: plumbing, tree, boulevard, sign and woodstove permits were being admitted because they mentioned a dwelling. 439 such rows were identified and removed. Every count in this document is post-purge.

3

Open every application that has a detail page

Victoria's tracker names the applicant's own agent; Saanich publishes the applicant firm in the feed itself. Langford, Colwood and Nanaimo publish neither. That asymmetry is the coverage table in section 10, and it is stated rather than smoothed over.

Identify the firms

Applicant names and agent email domains were enriched against Apollo for authoritative company names, size, location and headcount growth, then topped up with Hunter for roster depth. 15 firms had no Apollo record and are named from the city's own applicant string — they are marked as such wherever they appear.

Match against our ledger

Each firm was resolved against our client records by exact website domain, then exact name — never by substring, which once matched "Chard" to Richard & Co. That match produced section 03: 15 firms with a Deltek record or KOR job history.

▸ What is now running by itself

Five Island application feeds are live and refresh daily: Saanich (960), Nanaimo (876), Langford (349), Victoria (121) and Colwood (29). Adding another municipality that publishes the same way is a configuration row, not a new build — Saanich, Langford and Colwood were each added in a day. Esquimalt, Oak Bay, Sidney, North Saanich, Central Saanich, View Royal, Sooke and the Cowichan Valley are the obvious next ones.

▸ One thing this exercise found about our own data

The 532 named applicants resolve to 372 distinct strings but only 345 real parties — 50 of those strings sit in a group that is one company spelled more than one way. "READ JONES CHRISTOFFERSEN LTD", "READ JONES CHRISTOFFERSEN LTD." and "READ JONES CHRISTOFFERSEN" are three strings and one firm. So are "NES ARCHITECTURE" and "NES ARCHITECTURE LTD.", and three spellings of Alan Lowe. This is the same duplicate class that had RJC itself split across two canonical records in our platform until it was merged during this build. A separate sweep of the whole database is queued.

Method

Honest labels

  • The permit data is the cities', and it is exact. Application numbers, sites, scope text, applicant names, agent emails and Langford's valuations are published by the municipalities. We copied them; we did not interpret them. Currency: 4 September 2026 for Saanich, 2 September for Victoria, 25 August for Langford and Colwood, undated for Nanaimo.
  • KOR history is ours and reliable — job counts, last-job dates and Deltek client links from our own records.
  • Firm size, revenue, founding dates and growth rates are Apollo's, current as at September 2026, and not individually confirmed. Treat them as scale indicators, not as due diligence. Revenue figures in particular are modelled.
  • Langford's valuation is the owner's declared construction cost for permit-fee purposes — not a contract value, not a fee base, and not adjusted for the 56 unpriced applications.
  • Where a competitor is named as applicant, that means they filed the application — it is strong evidence they are the professional of record, and it is not a signed contract. Nine such firms appear; RJC accounts for four of the rows.
  • Records flagged for verification: Casman Group (Alberta head office against a 250 phone number), and the 15 firms Apollo could not match, whose names come from the applicant string alone — including NES Architecture, the busiest architect on the file.
  • "Live" means an open application, not a funded project. Applications are withdrawn, stalled and refused. Section 03's Deltek history is a much stronger signal than any single row in the addenda.
  • Coverage is Victoria, Saanich, Langford, Colwood and Nanaimo. Esquimalt, Oak Bay, Sidney, the Saanich Peninsula, View Royal, Sooke, the Cowichan Valley and everything north of Nanaimo are not wired. A firm's absence here means it is not filing in these five cities — not that it is inactive.
  • Scope is Vancouver Island. The same platform also reads Coquitlam and Maple Ridge; those are deliberately excluded from this document because they are not this market.
  • v2's corrections stand. The Starlight distinction, the Knappett split, the Omicron self-perform caution and the dropped "$103M RCMP detachment" all carry forward unchanged.
  • Where a tool and our own ledger disagree, the ledger wins. An external tool called this market "completely greenfield — zero worked-with". It is not; it reflects that Apollo's CRM is not populated by KOR.
