What changed in v3 — and why it matters

v2 answered "who are the significant firms on the Island?" from public research. v3 answers a harder and more useful question: who is filing an application in Victoria this month, who is acting for them, and do we already bill that client?

▸ The finding

The most active applicants in Victoria right now are already KOR clients. Not prospects — clients, with Deltek IDs and project history. Northland Properties has 369 KOR projects, the most recent dated 26 August 2026, and is named on two live Victoria applications. Reliance Properties: 82 projects. Intracorp: 46. Townline: 36.

v2 called Townline "the one real door." It was right that it was a door. It was wrong that there was one.

Three things are new and none of them existed in v2:

  • Named agents, not firm switchboards. Every Victoria application names the applicant's own contact — the developer, their architect, or their planning consultant. 47 named people across 42 firms, with direct email. These are not Apollo guesses; the city publishes them.
  • Firms v2 never surfaced. Islandview Group is the single most active applicant on the file and does not appear in v2 at all. Nor do MJM Architect, Wiser Projects, Bayview Place or Fold Architects — all of them filing now.
  • Nanaimo has a file too. 877 applications, including 217 commercial / multi-family / industrial. v2 could only describe Nanaimo from press coverage.

The thread

The Island's institutional pipeline is mass timber

Unchanged from v2 and still the single most actionable pattern in the market. It maps directly onto KOR's CLT and hybrid-timber capability.

  • VIU Nanaimo student housing — 10-storey hybrid CLT, 266 beds, BC Energy Step Code 4, on VIU's district geo-exchange. Opens summer 2027.
  • Camosun Lansdowne student housing — six storeys, 423 beds, mass timber targeting LEED Platinum. $154.7M ($151.7M province, $3M college). Ready early 2029.

Both flagship post-secondary projects on the Island are timber. The permit file now corroborates this from the private side too: Studio 531 Architects lists mass timber as a core capability and has grown headcount 75% in twelve months; Cascadia Architects and Fold Architects both file as Passive House specialists. The Island's design bench is being rebuilt around performance and timber, and that is the conversation to walk in with.

✕ Two honest gaps in the Nanaimo data

No applicant contacts. Nanaimo publishes no per-application detail page, so there is no equivalent of Victoria's named agent. The Nanaimo rows tell you what and where, never who. Closing that gap needs a different route — the planning department's own agendas.

No application date. The feed carries no date field, so Nanaimo rows cannot be sorted or aged. File numbers rise over time and are the only ordering available.

Method

How this document was produced

v2 was assembled by research. v3 was assembled by a pipeline that now runs on its own. This section exists so you can judge how much to trust each number — and so the next person can reproduce it.

1

Read the city's own file

Victoria publishes its development applications as a live map service. We read it directly — 258 rows that resolve to 146 real applications, because a rezoning spanning nine parcels is published as nine rows.

2

Filter to buildings

A relevance gate drops sign variances, fences and awnings. It had to be taught planning vocabulary first — it spoke tender language, and was discarding six-storey hotels because nobody had told it "storey" meant a building.

3

Open every application

Each surviving application links to the city's tracker, which names the applicant's own agent. All 116 were opened and read. That is where every email in this document comes from.

4

Identify the firms

Agent email domains were enriched against Apollo for authoritative company names, size and location — 31 of 42 matched. The remaining 11 are named from the applicant string itself and are marked as such.

5

Match against our ledger

Each firm was resolved against our existing client records by exact website domain, then exact name. 24 of 42 were already in our system; 10 carry Deltek client IDs. That match is what produced section 02.

6

Bank it

47 people, 47 firm affiliations and 42 typed org facts were written into the BD platform. The next pass starts from here, and the pipeline keeps updating whether or not anyone asks it to.

▸ What is now running by itself

Four municipal application feeds are live and refresh daily: Victoria (116), Nanaimo (877), Coquitlam (389) and Maple Ridge (120). Adding another municipality that publishes the same way is a configuration row, not a new build. Coquitlam's feed also publishes the applicant's name directly, which is how we know Ledingham McAllister is filing three 6–7 storey wood-frame buildings there.

Method

Honest labels

  • The permit data is the city's, and it is exact. Application numbers, sites, scope text, agent names and agent emails are published by the City of Victoria. We copied them; we did not interpret them. Currency: 27 August 2026.
  • KOR history is ours and reliable — job counts, last-job dates and Deltek client links from our own records.
  • Firm size, revenue and founding dates are Apollo's, current as at September 2026, and not individually confirmed. Treat them as scale indicators, not as due diligence. Revenue figures in particular are modelled.
  • Two records are explicitly flagged for verification: Casman Group (Alberta head office against a Saanich phone number) and the eleven firms Apollo could not match, whose names come from the applicant string.
  • "Live" means an open application, not a funded project. Applications are withdrawn, stalled and refused. Section 02's Deltek history is a much stronger signal than any single row in Addendum A.
  • Coverage is the City of Victoria and the City of Nanaimo only. Saanich, Langford, Colwood, Esquimalt, Oak Bay, Sidney and the West Shore are separate files and are not wired. A firm's absence here means it is not filing in these two cities — not that it is inactive.
  • v2's corrections stand. The Starlight distinction, the Knappett split, the Omicron self-perform caution and the dropped "$103M RCMP detachment" all carry forward unchanged.
  • Where a tool and our own ledger disagree, the ledger wins. An external tool called this market "completely greenfield — zero worked-with". It is not; it reflects that Apollo's CRM is not populated by KOR.
