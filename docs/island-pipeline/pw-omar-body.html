▸ Harry Jerome is a question, not a credential

Phases 2 and 3 for Darwin Properties carry $430,000 of signed contract between them and have billed nothing at all — no invoices, no time charged. Phase 1 billed $9,840 against a $97,000 fee, about a tenth.

So do not say "we did Harry Jerome". Ask what happened to it. Either it stalled, or it is still ahead of us — and either answer is worth the conversation. It is the single largest piece of unstarted work we hold with them.

✕ Two things not to say

They have never been our paying client. All seven billed jobs were invoiced to the developer. The one record under Perkins&Will's own account — ICBC Head Office, North Vancouver — has no fee, no time and no invoice against it. The line is "we've worked alongside you", never "you've been our client".

It has been a while. Every one of these was opened in 2018–19. If asked what we have done together lately, the honest answer is nothing since.

The opening

The mass timber opening — and who holds that seat

This is the most useful thing on the page, and it points at exactly what KOR does.

In February 2026 Perkins&Will published a study proposing a prefabricated modular mass-timber system for Canadian housing, backed by $250,000 of co-investment from DIGITAL's Housing Growth Innovation programme.

✕ The structural seat went to a competitor

The named partners are ASPECT Structural Engineers, Introba, and SFU's School of Interactive Arts & Technology. A funded Canadian mass-timber housing programme, with someone else in the structural chair.

It is not a side interest for them. When Derek Newby was elevated to the RAIC College of Fellows on 27 May 2026, the citation was for "advancing lower-carbon construction, mass timber innovation, and climate leadership". He is quoted on the housing study itself — "We can do much better. We can start today." — alongside Kathy Wardle. They are also designing BCIT's zero-carbon tall timber student housing, announced in January.

The question to ask: who carries structure on the timber work. Two of the three people quoted on that study are people we already have contacts for, and Peter Busby — still a Principal in the Vancouver studio — is the name most associated with tall wood in this city.

Strategy

Where the firm is betting, outside BC

Three moves in the last twelve months, all from their own announcements.

  • A global retail practice, built from nothing. In January they hired five principals at once, all out of the same Amsterdam design and engineering firm: Matt Billerbeck in Seattle as Practice Leader, Sophie Bramall in Dallas, Erich Dohrer in Chicago, Sarah Holstedt in Seattle, James Mellor in San Diego. CEO Phil Harrison's words: "to build the most sought-after retail practice in the world." Their stated territory is shopping-centre repositioning, stadium neighbourhoods, airports, transit-oriented development.
  • Science and technology, pivoting to applied science. In December they made Jeff Zynda firmwide Science & Technology Practice Leader, explicitly for "robotics and automation, energy systems and storage, artificial intelligence, quantum computing, and machine learning" — working across all 32 studios.
  • They bought into New York. Merged with A+I (Architecture Plus Information), effective September 2025.

Underneath that, a steady leadership build-out: a firmwide Director of Research and Development hired in April from running an MIT accelerator; a new Chief Marketing Officer; and new managing directors in Boston, Miami, Seattle, and Toronto and Ottawa. CEO Phil Harrison has held the job since 2006, from Atlanta.

▸ Read it this way

Retail, applied science and a New York acquisition are all sector and geography expansion, not core-market defence. A firm doing that is hiring capacity and looking for consultants who can move with it. The Canadian leadership sits with Derek Newby, in Vancouver — which is the door we already stand closest to.
