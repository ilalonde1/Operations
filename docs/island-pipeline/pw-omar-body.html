Strategy

Where the firm is placing its bets

Three moves in the last nine months tell you what they are chasing, and none of them are in BC.

  • They are building a global retail practice from scratch. In January they hired five principals in one go, all from the same Amsterdam design and engineering firm — Matt Billerbeck in Seattle as Practice Leader, Sophie Bramall in Dallas, Erich Dohrer in Chicago, Sarah Holstedt in Seattle, James Mellor in San Diego. CEO Phil Harrison's stated goal: "to build the most sought-after retail practice in the world." Their portfolio is shopping-centre repositioning, stadium neighbourhoods, airports, transit-oriented development and vertically integrated commercial hubs.
  • Science and technology is pivoting toward applied science. In December they appointed Jeff Zynda as firmwide Science & Technology Practice Leader, explicitly to respond to a shift toward "robotics and automation, energy systems and storage, artificial intelligence, quantum computing, and machine learning" — working across all 32 studios. He came from Harvard and MIT work plus projects in Ireland and the UK.
  • They bought their way into New York. Perkins&Will merged with A+I (Architecture Plus Information), a New York studio, effective September 2025.

Behind those, a steady leadership build-out through 2026: a firmwide Director of Research and Development hired in April from running an MIT accelerator programme; a new Chief Marketing Officer (Yanel de Angel) in February; new managing directors in Boston (Vickie Alani), Miami (Rosa Maria Colina), Seattle (Todd Buchanan) and Toronto and Ottawa; and new landscape architecture leadership in the Bay Area this month.

The work

What they are building right now

Canada, outside BC

  • University of Calgary — Interdisciplinary Science and Innovation Centre. A new academic building transforming the east campus gateway. Their Calgary studio holds it, and our own records show they took the architectural services contract for it. Worth asking where it has got to.
  • Toronto Metropolitan University — Daphne Cockwell Health Sciences Complex. A 28-storey health education tower in downtown Toronto, which the firm positions as its model for campus design on a dense urban site. It won a Best Tall Building award.
  • Their Calgary studio moved into the historic Glenbow building at 822 11th Ave SW in the Beltline — a deliberately public-facing space, pitched around adaptive reuse and downtown placemaking.

United States

  • University of Louisville — Health Sciences Center, with Champlin | EOP. Public health, medicine, dentistry and nursing brought into one building, linking the academic core to the surrounding medical district.
  • University of Houston — RAD Center, which took the 2026 AIA Design Excellence Award for Education Facilities.
  • LSU Health Shreveport — Emerging Viral Threats Lab, winner of the 2026 Lab Design Excellence Award for small project design.
  • UVA Health emergency department expansion, recognised by IIDA.
  • A mall-to-college-campus conversion honoured by ULI Americas in July — the adaptive-reuse story they are telling loudest right now.

International

  • A world-class science and technology museum in Suzhou, China, unveiled in March.
  • Triple winner at the 2026 RIBA International Awards for Excellence, and their London studio was named Studio of the Year at the 2026 Clerkenwell Design Week Awards.
  • Two AIA National Awards in June.

The opening

The mass timber opening — and who has that seat today

In February they published a study proposing a prefabricated modular mass-timber system for Canadian housing, funded with $250,000 of co-investment from DIGITAL's Housing Growth Innovation programme.

✕ The structural seat went to someone else

The study's named partners are ASPECT Structural Engineers, Introba, and SFU's School of Interactive Arts & Technology. That is a mass-timber housing R&D programme, in Canada, with a competitor in the structural chair — and mass timber is our lane.

This is the most useful thing in the document. Two of the three people quoted on it are people we already hold contacts for:

  • Derek Newby, quoted as Canadian Regional Director: "We can do much better. We can start today."
  • Kathy Wardle, Regional Director of Regenerative Design: "We need to leverage every opportunity to reduce the environmental impact of the built environment."

They are also designing BCIT's zero-carbon tall timber student housing, announced in January. The timber conversation is live, it is funded, and the structural partner is not us.

Caveats

Before you quote anything

  • The nine shared projects are from our own project records and are reliable. They are all British Columbia, and all between 2018 and 2019 — so if someone asks "what have you done with us lately", the honest answer is that the recent work has not been together.
  • ⛔ Perkins Eastman is a different firm. We hold both. Do not mix them up in conversation.
  • The firm does not publish a headcount and neither their website nor their news releases give one. 32 studios is from their own December release and is safe to use; a staff number is not.
  • Ownership was not confirmed for this document. If it comes up, do not assert anything about who owns them.
  • Everything in sections 02 to 04 is from Perkins&Will's own published news, dated December 2025 to September 2026. It is what they are saying publicly about themselves, which is exactly the right register for an event — but it is their framing, not an independent read.
  • The University of Calgary contract is in our own awards data and predates 2026; treat its current status as a question to ask, not a fact to state.
