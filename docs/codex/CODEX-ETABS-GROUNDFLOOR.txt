WHAT CHANGED SINCE THIS WAS WRITTEN -- do not re-derive any of it

  1. IT IS NOT A DASHED LINE. Every slab-edge layer on these sheets carries linetype "Continuous"
     in the LAYER table: JBP_C_SLABEDG, -1 and -2 alike. Only JBP_B_WALL is ___HIDDEN. The whole
     "is this a dashed edge-below" question below is ANSWERED, and the answer is no. The 11-inch
     median gaps are real breaks in the linework, not dashes.

  2. NO TOLERANCE CLOSES IT. Measured on the LEVEL 1 sheet, pooled and per layer, at gaps of
     0.05, 1, 6, 14, 24, 36, 48 and 72 inches, tessellating arcs: the largest region the slab edge
     encloses is 119 SQ FT. JBP_C_SLABEDG spans the full 334x235 ft footprint as 66 to 79 open
     chains that never join into a boundary. There is nothing there to close. Raising a tolerance
     cannot produce that floor and neither can extending -- stop before you try.

  3. THOSE STOREYS ARE NO LONGER BARE. ComposeOptions.InferMissingFloors, off by default, gives a
     storey with members and no floor the plate from the storey below it -- and only where that
     plate actually stands under this storey's own walls and columns, because "nearest below" alone
     handed the mid-rise a floor belonging to the podium under the towers. 31168 ships with it on.
     It is a stand-in, reported as INFERRED, not an answer.

  So the question is no longer "what gap closes it". It is: IS THERE A REAL SLAB EDGE ON THESE
  SHEETS AT ALL, and if not, where should a ground-floor plate come from -- the flood-fill plate
  detection that already exists (see below), the parkade below, or nothing? An engineer trimming an
  inferred plate is a fair outcome; an invented one she cannot tell from a measured one is not.

  What still stands: everything about WHERE the code is, the flood-fill work already in the repo,
  and the constraints at the end. Read those.

MEASURED ALREADY -- do not spend the afternoon re-deriving this

  Slab edges live on a layer family: JBP_C_SLABEDG, JBP_C_SLABEDG-1, JBP_C_SLABEDG-2. The
  loop builder joins ends within dxf.join-tolerance and bridges gaps up to dxf.bridge-tolerance;
  DashedLineJoiner stitches across dxf.dash-join-gap first.

  Rule values actually in force on the run (from KorStandards, printed in the report):

      dxf.join-tolerance   = 0.05 in
      dxf.bridge-tolerance = 6 in
      dxf.dash-join-gap    = 14 in

  `takeoff dxf-inspect <sheet.dxf>` prints, per layer, closed loops / open chains and the
  distribution of loose-end gaps. On the two Level 1 sheets:

  Two shapes of failure, and they are not the same problem:

    - SLABEDG-2 breaks into 187 pieces with a median gap of 11 inches and 373 of 374 loose ends
      inside 24. That LOOKS like a dashed line sitting just outside a 14" joiner. It is not one:
      the layer is Continuous. Eleven-inch breaks in a continuous line are the thing to explain.
    - SLABEDG and SLABEDG-1 have gaps with a median of 46 and 55 inches and a maximum of 762.
      Those are not dashes either, and never looked like it.

  Also measured, so you do not go looking: these DXFs contain LINE and ARC entities only. No
  LWPOLYLINE, no TEXT, no MTEXT -- 0 text entities in the file, so nothing in the drawing names
  what any of this is. Every slab-edge LINE carries linetype ByLayer, so dashedness is decided by
  the LAYER record. That HAS now been read: all three slab layers are "Continuous". Only
  JBP_B_WALL is ___HIDDEN. Nothing on the slab-edge layers is dashed.

THE TRAP -- read this before you invent a plate

  Tolerance is a dead end and is already measured out (see the top). The remaining trap is the
  opposite one: producing a plate that closes cleanly, passes every count, imports without
  complaint, and is not the floor that is there. That is worse than the hole, because an engineer
  cannot see it.

  So the question is "what IS on these layers". Answer it from the drawing -- what the geometry
  encloses, how the three layers sit relative to each other, and whether they behave this way on
  sheets whose floors DO come out right. Every C-LEVEL sheet produces a floor; those are the
  control group and they are in the same folder. If the honest answer is that no slab edge is
  drawn on Level 1 at all, say so -- that is a finding, and drafting can close the outline.

  If the honest answer is that SLABEDG-2 is not this level's edge, then say so and the fix is
  elsewhere -- the floor has to come from SLABEDG and SLABEDG-1 and their 46-inch gaps.

BEFORE YOU WRITE ANY JOINING CODE -- THIS IS ALREADY SOLVED IN THIS REPO

  Do not write a new gap-closer. The same assembly already finds a floor plate by a route that
  does not care whether the edge is dashed or broken every eleven inches:

      Kor.Operations.EngineeringTools.Core/PlanGeometry.cs
          MeasureEnclosedArea(luminance, w, h, darkThreshold, sealHairlineGaps: true)
          -- flood-fills the region enclosed by the heaviest closed linework and seals hairline
             gaps on the way. Built for exactly this failure and used by the takeoff engine.
      Kor.Operations.EngineeringTools.Core/SlabAreaReconciler.cs
      Kor.Operations.EngineeringTools.Core/PlateReliability.cs

  The DXF-to-ETABS path calls none of them. It closes outlines from scratch in PlanLoopBuilder
  and is stuck on tolerances, while the plate detection sitting beside it is insensitive to the
  thing it is stuck on. There is also a `vector-plate` / `vector-plate-auto` verb on the CLI, and
  a PDF ingestion path that feeds the same geometry.

  So the first question is not "how do I close these loops". It is: can the plate for this storey
  come from the existing plate detection, with the vector outline used to place and trim it? If it
  can, that is the fix, and it is smaller than anything in the section below. If it genuinely
  cannot -- say why, with what you tried -- then go on to the vector work.

  A caution that cuts the other way: flood fill will happily enclose a region bounded by whatever
  linework is heaviest, including the dashed edge-below if that is what is there. It removes the
  tolerance problem, not the question of WHICH edge is the floor. That question still has to be
  answered.
