Citations below use filenames relative to `Kor.Operations.EngineeringTools.Core/PdfToSafe/` for `GeometryFilterService.cs` and `DxfExporter.cs`, and `Kor.Operations.EngineeringTools.Core/Intake/` for `DrawingIntake.cs` and `SheetViews.cs`.

1. **What a path must satisfy, and what the witness proves.**

   There are two routes to an exported slab. A closed path can become `BecameSlab` directly: after earlier exclusions and competing column/wall/band decisions, its bounding box must not look column-sized and its diagonal must meet `slabMinDiagonalMm` (`GeometryFilterService.cs:352`, `GeometryFilterService.cs:463`). It need never receive `BecameSlabEdge`.

   For the assembled-line route, open paths first have to survive furniture, grid, underline, match-line, invisible-ink and other filters. They must meet `lineMinLengthMm` or the explicit short-jog exception; frame edges and lines carrying bar marks are excluded before `EmittedAsLine` is recorded (`GeometryFilterService.cs:315`, `GeometryFilterService.cs:325`, `GeometryFilterService.cs:333`, `GeometryFilterService.cs:347`, `GeometryFilterService.cs:515`, `GeometryFilterService.cs:528`, `GeometryFilterService.cs:537`). Short connected strokes also have a curve-assembly route before that length decision (`GeometryFilterService.cs:260`).

   `SlabEdgesFromLoops` first requires at least four entries across lines and grid strokes (`GeometryFilterService.cs:1839`). Eligible lines need at least two points and must not be annotations. A line already claimed as a wall face is eligible only when the length calculation leaves at least 2,000 mm beyond its associated panel. Arrowhead-connected runs are then removed. A second gate requires at least four candidate lines plus two-point grid strokes (`GeometryFilterService.cs:1887`, `GeometryFilterService.cs:1895`, `GeometryFilterService.cs:1908`, `GeometryFilterService.cs:1917`, `GeometryFilterService.cs:3018`). These gates count paths, not all segments in a multipoint path.

   The candidates then have to contribute to recovered rings. Exact joins use 1 mm; open chains at least 2,000 mm long enter the bridging route. The bridge is an option whose compiled default is 152.4 mm, and the extension bound is 1,219.2 mm (`GeometryFilterService.cs:1941`, `GeometryFilterService.cs:1954`, `GeometryFilterService.cs:2040`, `GeometryFilterService.cs:3009`). A conditional planar-arrangement route also uses grid strokes, carried ends, wall outlines and face leftovers, doorway/gap edges and match lines (`GeometryFilterService.cs:2114`, `GeometryFilterService.cs:2201`, `GeometryFilterService.cs:2327`). Thus being claimed as a wall face does not invariably remove that geometry from every slab-recovery route.

   Resulting floor candidates must reach `minSlabAreaMm2` (compiled default 400 sq ft), pass `StandsIn`, and not lie wholly within the recognized X-mark regions. `StandsIn` requires at least one column centre or wall midpoint inside/on the ring, with a strict majority of the counted nearby supports inside/on it; the neighbourhood reach is 10% of the ring's larger bounding-box dimension. Candidates mostly inside a qualifying pre-existing drawn slab or an already selected larger ring are omitted (`GeometryFilterService.cs:2660`, `GeometryFilterService.cs:2693`, `GeometryFilterService.cs:2790`, `GeometryFilterService.cs:2819`, `GeometryFilterService.cs:3040`).

   **Line 2764 is bookkeeping after recovery, not the initial slab-edge classifier.** A selected loop is first added to `result.Slabs`. Candidate lines whose every vertex lies within the 1 mm ring-edge tolerance enter `SlabEdgeLines`. Only paths still recorded as `EmittedAsLine` are then rewritten to `Disposition.Read / BecameSlabEdge` (`GeometryFilterService.cs:2704`, `GeometryFilterService.cs:2745`, `GeometryFilterService.cs:2754`, `GeometryFilterService.cs:2861`). A zero count of that reason alone would miss direct slabs and other contributing geometry.

   The exporter takes `geometry.Slabs`, applies exclusions and point filtering, and writes the surviving polygons closed on the slab layer under the ordinary KOR-layer settings (`DxfExporter.cs:203`, `DxfExporter.cs:376`, `DxfExporter.cs:527`). Generic remaining lines default to open `BEAM` polylines (`DxfExporter.cs:577`). The slab layer is declared even when no slab entities are written (`DxfExporter.cs:488`).

   Consequently, the supplied DXF proves that this view has no entities on that layer; it does not identify which upstream gate failed. Possible stages include initial classification, failed closure or floor qualification, view ownership, and export filtering or layer overrides. The split assigns slabs and lines independently by their mean vertex positions, while walls use their axis midpoints (`SheetViews.cs:259`, `SheetViews.cs:262`); export also permits colour-based role overrides (`DxfExporter.cs:416`). The four source files contain no witness-specific path ledger or slab trace. I cannot honestly name the failed condition on these pages without those records and the original outline's location. A rendering of the resulting DXF cannot establish what the original PDF drew.

2. **Whether another role consumed it, and whether the ledger can tell.**

   Consumption is possible. A filled shape admitted as a wall gets `BecameWall` and leaves the initial loop before the direct slab decision (`GeometryFilterService.cs:435`). Paired face lines explicitly become `Disposition.Read / BecameWallFace`, indexed to their wall (`GeometryFilterService.cs:1523`, `GeometryFilterService.cs:1533`). This can look completely accounted for even if the role is wrong. Whole-face lines are excluded from the ordinary candidate list, subject to the leftover exception above; wall outlines and face leftovers can still reach the arrangement. The exporter suppresses the original wall-face lines rather than emitting them again (`DxfExporter.cs:218`).

   Other routes record `ThinBand`, `Band`, `FurnitureRegion`, `GridAxis`, `Underline`, `FrameEdgeLine`, `BarRun`, `TooShort`, `NoInk` or `PaperFill`; repeated-pattern passes can rewrite earlier column/wall decisions to `PatternCell` (`GeometryFilterService.cs:447`, `GeometryFilterService.cs:459`, `GeometryFilterService.cs:543`, `GeometryFilterService.cs:551`, `GeometryFilterService.cs:582`). There is no basis here to label the witness's missing edge a hatch or an annotation merely from the wall count. Annotation lines are explicitly ineligible for assembled slab edges (`GeometryFilterService.cs:1887`).

   **The ledger survives intake, but is not a complete slab-rejection history.** `DrawingIntake` requests fates, remaps them to the full unthinned path population, and returns both the full page and fates in `SheetRecord` (`DrawingIntake.cs:258`, `DrawingIntake.cs:269`, `DrawingIntake.cs:375`). Remapping rejects duplicate or missing decisions for retained paths and marks paths removed by thinning as `Disposition.Discarded / CollapsedByThinning` (`DrawingIntake.cs:436`, `DrawingIntake.cs:444`, `DrawingIntake.cs:451`). Once an actual PDF edge is identified, this is enough to inspect its recorded role and source geometry.

   It is not enough to explain every failed floor. A line can remain `EmittedAsLine` whether excluded as an arrowhead run, left in an open chain, or associated with a ring rejected by floor qualification. A wall-face path reused by slab recovery need not become `BecameSlabEdge`, because that rewrite indexes only `EmittedAsLine` fates. Grid strokes likewise retain their separate reason. The optional `FaceTrace` contains candidate exclusions and recovery counts; arrangement failure has a separate `SlabEdgeArrangementRefused` field (`GeometryFilterService.cs:1898`, `GeometryFilterService.cs:2651`, `GeometryFilterService.cs:2679`, `GeometryFilterService.cs:2758`).

   `SheetViews.Part` carries a title, filename and geometry, not the page ledger (`SheetViews.cs:33`). The multi-view split remaps geometry indices but does not return page-to-view provenance maps or copy `SlabEdgeArrangementRefused` (`SheetViews.cs:286`, `SheetViews.cs:293`, `SheetViews.cs:330`, `SheetViews.cs:373`). `DxfExporter` receives geometry and export settings, not `PathFate` (`DxfExporter.cs:110`). Thus the parent `SheetRecord` retains evidence, but the split-view/DXF boundary does not carry it. Whether another caller persists that parent record is outside these four files. Nor is an exported entity count a count of source paths: a wall may split into piers, and a DXF polygon emits multiple records (`GeometryFilterService.cs:422`, `DxfExporter.cs:510`).

3. **A safe check, with sibling suppression.**

   Yes, the tool can identify a contradiction worth asking about: an outline-titled structural view produced no exported slab outline, and no sibling view serving the same building/storey accounts for that outline. It cannot infer from the title alone that a particular path was lost or that a particular floor polygon is correct.

   Record the per-view evidence after `SheetViews.Split`, using the view's own title, page identity, geometry ownership and the actual export outcome. Count exported slab polygons, including the direct `BecameSlab` route, rather than counting `BecameSlabEdge` fates or layer declarations. Keep this as a diagnostic candidate until all sibling views are known. The split's return point is an appropriate boundary for collecting it (`SheetViews.cs:373`); the export filtering and emission points establish the outcome (`DxfExporter.cs:203`, `DxfExporter.cs:527`).

   Resolve candidates across the complete set by authoritative building and expanded storey identity, with part-plan coverage considered where applicable. Suppress an empty view when a sibling supplies the relevant floor outline. Do not let a different tower's slab, or merely another view somewhere in the set, suppress the warning. For unresolved cases the statement should be factual: “These CONCRETE OUTLINE views serve this storey, but no exported floor outline was found for it in these or its sibling views; inspect the source outline and its recorded path decisions.” It must add no geometry.

   This decision belongs at the set-level aggregation boundary, not inside `GeometryFilterService` or a single exporter call. `DrawingIntake.Read` gathers all sheets of one PDF and could host a reconciliation helper once view and placement evidence is supplied (`DrawingIntake.cs:25`). It is insufficient on its own when the complete drawing set spans PDFs or final placement is decided later. `SheetViews.Split` itself sees only one sheet (`SheetViews.cs:220`). None of these four methods currently receives every final building/storey assignment and export outcome needed to guarantee sibling suppression.
