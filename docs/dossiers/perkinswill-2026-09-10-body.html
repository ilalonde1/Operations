Summary

Summary and what to do

Perkins&Will is a 2,600-person, $795M firm growing hard — in the United States. Of 128 open roles, two are in Canada. So this is not a grow-with-them conversation; it is a conversation about the Canadian work they already have, and the live piece of it is mass timber, where a competitor already holds the structural seat.

We have billed $536,544 across seven jobs with them, four of which are 98% of it, all opened 2018–19 and all invoiced to the developer rather than to them. They have never been our client. We are re-opening a warm but dormant relationship, and the credible opening is ASPECT holding the structural chair on their funded Canadian mass-timber housing programme.

▸ If you only get five minutes

Say “we’ve worked alongside you” — River District, 600 Robson, 900 Granville. Then ask who carries structure on their mass timber work. Then find Jeff Doble.

▸ The one inference worth carrying into the room

Their growth is American. Dallas alone has 18 open roles, San Francisco 14, Atlanta and Minneapolis 10 each. Vancouver has one and Calgary one. A firm hiring like that is not expanding in Canada — it is consolidating here and pushing hard in the US south and west.

So this is not a "grow with them into new markets" conversation. It is a conversation about the Canadian work that already exists — and the single most active Canadian thing they have going is mass timber housing, where a competitor already holds the structural seat. The opening

The mass timber opening — and who holds that seat

This is the reason to have the conversation.

In February 2026 they published a study proposing a prefabricated modular mass-timber system for Canadian housing, backed by $250,000 from DIGITAL's Housing Growth Innovation programme.

✕ The structural seat went to a competitor

Named partners: ASPECT Structural Engineers, Introba, and SFU's School of Interactive Arts & Technology. A funded Canadian mass-timber housing programme with someone else in the structural chair.

It is not peripheral to them. When Derek Newby was elevated to the RAIC College of Fellows on 27 May 2026, the citation was for "advancing lower-carbon construction, mass timber innovation, and climate leadership." He is quoted on the study — "We can do much better. We can start today." — with Kathy Wardle. They are also designing BCIT's zero-carbon tall timber student housing, announced in January. Mass timber appears three separate times in the firm's own keyword profile.

The question to ask: who carries structure on the timber work, and whether that is settled. Two of the three people quoted are already our contacts. Peter Busby is the name most associated with tall wood in this city — he founded the Canada Green Building Council and holds the Order of Canada — but our records have him in a San Francisco role now, so the people who own this in Vancouver day to day are Derek Newby and Kathy Wardle. Both are on the next page. If Busby is in the room, the Green Building Council is the opening, not our project list.

Federal Canada — a channel worth asking about

They carry Government of Canada contracts, which is a route into public work that has nothing to do with the private developer market either of us usually talks about.

  • Public Services and Procurement Canada — two professional-services contracts awarded 6 March 2025, $200,439 and $42,309.
  • Parks Canada — architectural services at $316,315 (January and March 2017) and a further $364,768 for engineering consultants in December 2017.
  • University of Calgary — the architectural services contract for the Interdisciplinary Science and Innovation Centre, through Alberta Purchasing, 2019.

Worth asking how much of their Canadian book is federal now, and who they team with on it.
