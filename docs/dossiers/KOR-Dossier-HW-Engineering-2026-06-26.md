## What They Do and Where

H+W provides full MEP engineering — mechanical (HVAC, process piping, plumbing systems), electrical (power distribution, lighting, power protection, technology systems), and plumbing design — on projects ranging from ground-up concrete residential towers to complex laboratory and life-science tenant improvements. They supplement core MEP with BIM / Revit coordination, architectural lighting design, energy modeling, sustainability consulting (LEED documentation), and technology systems (low-voltage, AV, security). This breadth is materially valuable in a teaming pitch: a developer writing an RFP can list one MEP entity and get every engineering discipline below structural covered.

**Sectors (confirmed from their website):** Housing and mixed-use (their volume lane), Office and commercial TI, Science and Technology / Life-science, Healthcare and Wellness, Education (including UCSD portfolio), Industrial, Military and Government, Hospitality and Retail, Civic, Central Plant.

**Geography:** Primary market is the San Diego metro; secondary markets include California statewide, and they hold active licenses in Arizona, Texas, Nevada, and Colorado — meaning a teaming relationship with KOR extends beyond SD wherever KOR pursues in the US Southwest.

**Industry associations:** ASHRAE San Diego, IES San Diego Section, ISPE (life-science), I2SL. Tom Harmon and Bryan Wayne met at San Diego State University and started their careers together at the same SD engineering firm before founding H+A in 2009.

## The Teaming Case

**Why KOR + H+W is a complete team.** Most architects and developers in the SD mid-market to institutional range must assemble structural and MEP separately, managing two separate negotiation tracks, two coordination interfaces, and two schedules. A standing KOR + H+W preferred-teaming arrangement eliminates that friction. Together the team covers: structural (KOR), mechanical, electrical, plumbing, BIM coordination, lighting, energy, and technology systems (H+W). That is every hard-engineering discipline on a project — exactly the package a Carrier Johnson, Ware Malcomb, or Miller Hull wants to propose to a Kilroy or HG Fenton.

**Joint-pursuit angles (in priority order):**

1. **Miller Hull joint pitch.** Both firms share Miller Hull as an architect relationship (H+W did MEP on Parco; KOR has SD presence). Miller Hull is active with mid-rise mixed-use in SD and National City. A warm intro from H+W's principals to Miller Hull's SD project managers — framed as "we work with KOR on structural now" — gets KOR in front of Miller Hull without a cold call.

2. **HG Fenton warm-path intro.** HG Fenton (private SD developer, 80+ years, ~7M SF of commercial and residential in SD) is listed directly on H+W's client roster. KOR has no confirmed HG Fenton history. An H+W introduction to the HG Fenton construction/development team is a concrete deliverable Jim can request in the first meeting — it is not a favor, it is a mutually beneficial teaming relationship that gives H+W a structural partner on HG Fenton projects too.

3. **Preferred-teaming agreement.** Once principals have met, propose a one-page preferred-teaming MOU: H+W's standard pursuit process adds KOR as structural; KOR's standard SD proposal process recommends H+W as MEP. No exclusivity required — just first-call status. Both firms win more complete-team RFP responses; neither loses a pursuit they would have won alone.

4. **UCSD / Irvine Company TI pipeline.** H+W has a deep UCSD relationship (30+ active and recent projects). UCSD and similar institutional clients increasingly require a full engineering team on-call. A standing structural + MEP pairing positions both firms for IDIQ-style on-call contracts at UCSD, SDSU, or with Irvine Company Office Properties (shared client).

**Risk — existing SE partner (DCI Engineers):** The one confirmed structural engineer on a named H+W project is DCI Engineers (Parco, 2021, with Miller Hull). DCI is an established Seattle-based firm with a 20-person SD office led by Principal Ryan Slaybaugh. They are KOR's direct displacement target. KOR's pitch should not attack DCI by name — instead, position on project-specific advantages: KOR's SD award-winning concrete high-rise track record (Simone, The Grande), KOR's Vancouver + SD dual-market capability for developers like Pinnacle operating in both markets, and principal-to-principal senior involvement on every project (a stated H+W value).

**Risk — H+W's geographic focus vs. KOR's US footprint:** H+W is primarily San Diego-centric. If KOR pursues LA or other California markets, H+W may not be the natural MEP partner there; however, H+W does hold active licenses statewide and in AZ/TX/NV/CO, so the relationship has runway.

## How Jim Opens the Conversation

Jim will be in San Diego June 29 – July 1. The play is a principals meeting with Tom Harmon and Bryan Wayne, booked via Mandy. Three to four talking points:

1. **Lead with Parco / Miller Hull.** "We've looked at your portfolio and the Parco project with Miller Hull stands out — 130,000 SF, 127 units, mixed-use. That's exactly the typology we do on structural in this market. Who was your structural engineer on that one?" (This is a genuine question — opens the DCI displacement conversation without Jim having to raise it directly, and signals KOR has done its homework.)

2. **Name-drop the shared client book.** "We've been active on Kilroy Realty and Irvine Company TIs on the structural side. Sounds like you're in the same rooms. We should be walking into those pursuits together." This frames the relationship as reciprocally valuable from the first minute.

3. **Propose the preferred-teaming ask concretely.** "What we're interested in is a first-call arrangement — when you're putting together an MEP team for a structural pursuit, KOR is your structural; when we're putting together a team for a developer, H+W is our MEP. We don't need a formal contract. We just want to be in each other's proposals." This is a low-friction ask and consistent with how smaller firms in the SD market operate.

4. **Ask the HG Fenton warm-intro question directly.** "You've done work with HG Fenton. We don't have a direct relationship there yet. If you've got a project manager or VP of construction you work with, an introduction as your structural partner would go a long way." This is the highest-value near-term deliverable from the meeting.
