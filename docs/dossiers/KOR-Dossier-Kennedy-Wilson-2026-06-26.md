## 2. Business & Footprint

**Who they are.** Kennedy Wilson was founded in 1977 as a real estate brokerage and grew into a vertically integrated developer, investor, and asset manager. Core business today is multifamily development and rental housing in the Mountain West, Pacific Northwest, Northern California, and Southern California.

**The Fairfax take-private (June 15–16, 2026).** Kennedy Wilson completed a $1.65B all-cash privatization led by CEO William McMorrow and Fairfax Financial Holdings ($10.90/share), delisting from NYSE effective June 16, 2026. Fairfax holds a majority economic interest; the McMorrow management consortium retains full operational control. Fairfax and KW have a 15+ year partnership ($8B+ in collaborative acquisitions). Strategic rationale: shed public-company compliance costs, focus management on long-term strategy. Financed by a $1.8B senior notes issuance (May 2026). **Implication for KOR:** no quarterly earnings pressure means leadership can pursue larger development bets — and transition to private creates a natural reset point for vendor relationships.

**The Toll Brothers Apartment Living acquisition (Sept 2025, ~$380M).** This is the most important structural fact for KOR's pursuit. Kennedy Wilson paid ~$380M to absorb Toll Brothers' entire multifamily development arm, including:
- GP interests in **18 stabilized apartment/student housing assets** (~$2.2B AUM)
- A **29-site development pipeline** in various stages, projected at ~$3.6B of invested capital on completion
- Management of **20 additional Toll Brothers-retained properties** (~$3B AUM)
- The **entire Toll Brothers Apartment Living executive team**, led by John McCullough (20-year Toll vet) who is now President of Kennedy Wilson Multifamily Development

**Why this matters:** The Lindley, KOR's prior project, was developed by Toll Brothers Apartment Living. McCullough ran that group when KOR was engaged. He now runs development at Kennedy Wilson. KOR's prior relationship follows the org.

## 5. Competitive Reality

**Incumbent SE for Kennedy Wilson:** No SE firm has an established preferred-vendor relationship with KW's *own* development arm — KW grew by acquisition and asset management, not ground-up development. KOR is the only structural engineer publicly identified anywhere in KW's now-managed US portfolio (via The Lindley, which KOR delivered for Toll Brothers/PGIM). That is a credible foot in the door as KW stands up ground-up development under McCullough — an opening to seize, not an incumbency to claim.

**For the new Toll Brothers pipeline sites:** Structural appointments have not been publicly disclosed. Toll Brothers Apartment Living has historically used different SE firms by market. No single national SE is confirmed as a preferred vendor. The transition to Kennedy Wilson resets procurement.

**Competing SE firms in KOR's markets:**
- *Seattle high-rise multifamily:* Cary Kopczynski & Company (CKC) is the dominant firm — 20+ towers of 20 stories+. KOR's Seattle footprint is BC-based; for KW's Seattle acquisitions (existing assets), no new SE work is generated.
- *SoCal:* No dominant incumbent identified for the LA/SD Toll Brothers pipeline. The Lindley (SD) = KOR's proof of delivery.

**Post-privatization transition window (now — Q3 2026).** The Fairfax deal closed two weeks ago. Kennedy Wilson is reorganizing its cost structure, absorbing 85 Toll Brothers employees, and setting up a private-company operating model. Vendor relationships are under review. Incumbent consultants with a track record (KOR) have an advantage — but only if they are visible during the reset.

## 6. KOR Angle & How to Win

**The story Jim should tell:**

KOR engineered The Lindley — Kennedy Wilson's most prominent and architecturally recognized development asset (2025 BIA Icon Award — Best Architectural Design, Multifamily for Rent). KOR delivered a 37-story concrete high-rise in a demanding urban infill site with Swinerton as GC. That relationship is already inside the building.

**Concrete talking points for Jim:**

1. **"We did The Lindley."** Open with it. John McCullough ran that project at Toll Brothers. Michael Eadie is now overseeing it under KW. This is a live credential with people in the room.

2. **"We cover your West Coast geography."** KOR has offices in Vancouver BC (PNW), LA, and San Diego. The 29-site Toll Brothers pipeline includes LA (Warner Center, Culver City, Long Beach) and potentially PNW. One SE firm that knows both markets is an efficiency argument.

3. **"We've worked with Swinerton."** Swinerton built The Lindley. If they're on any LA-pipeline project (common for large GCs), KOR's field relationship is already established.

4. **"Let's talk about the LA pipeline."** Ask McCullough directly which of the 29 sites are in Southern California and whether SE has been appointed. The transition is the opening — don't wait for an RFP that may never be public.

**Four concrete asks for the SD trip:**

1. Request an in-person meeting with **Michael Eadie** (BH or via call) to introduce KOR as KW's SE on The Lindley and discuss the forward pipeline.
2. Ask **John McCullough** (via warm email or intro call) which of the 29 Toll pipeline sites are in SoCal — position KOR as a natural continuation.
3. Identify whether the **Warner Center project** (6464 Canoga Ave, 276 units, AC Martin as architect) has an SE appointed. If not, submit a qualifications letter immediately.
4. Flag KOR's **PNW structural engineering capacity** (BC + potential WA expansion) against Kennedy Wilson's Bellevue office coverage area.
