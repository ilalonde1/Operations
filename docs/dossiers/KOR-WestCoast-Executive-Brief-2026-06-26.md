## The one-page thesis

KOR's West Coast opportunity is **not a cold expansion — it is a relationship-conversion play**, and it runs on a single structural truth that repeats across both markets:

> **Vancouver developers import their Vancouver structural engineer into California — and that engineer (Glotman Simpson) has an LA office but *no San Diego office*. KOR can be in San Diego. That gap is the wedge.**

Everything in the packet resolves into **two motions**:

1. **ELEVATE what KOR already holds — the JWDA channel, anchored by the Greystar incumbency.** KOR is the structural engineer on **multiple JWDA-designed San Diego towers across four developers**, and the engine is the *architect*: **JWDA keeps picking KOR when it has discretion**, carrying KOR from developer to developer. Two pieces are rock-solid: (a) **Greystar** — KOR is SE on **525 Olive** (completed 2022) and **6th & Palm** (under construction to 2028), both Greystar developments; and (b) **The Lindley** (37-story) — though its developer was **Toll Brothers + PGIM**, *not* Kennedy Wilson (KW is a later owner). Convert the JWDA relationship into firm-level preferred-engineer status and elevate Greystar to preferred vendor + a Vancouver door.
2. **DISPLACE the out-of-town incumbent where it has no local feet — Glotman Simpson.** **Pinnacle** and **Bosa** import **Glotman Simpson**, which has an **LA office but no San Diego office** and has served ~50 SD high-rises from Vancouver/LA. *That* is the clean wedge. **KPFF and DCI both have San Diego offices** (since 1994 and 2001), so they are *not* "no-office" targets — displace them on principal attention and the under-served mid-tier, not presence.

San Diego is **near-term revenue**; Los Angeles is a **patient 2027+ build** (owner-direct seismic retrofit + the same Vancouver-relationship lever, via Onni).
