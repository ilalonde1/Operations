CAD vs Revit — what the books say

How much CAD we still do, and whether it makes money. Measured from Deltek, weighted by hours and dollars, not by counting folders.

As of 14 October 2026
Billings posted through October 2026
2,329 jobs with production hours
The short version
  • CAD is 23% of the firm's drafting hours this year, CAD ÷ (CAD + Revit), down from 54% in 2019; counting General and untagged jobs too, 21%. Residential alone is 24%. The shift to Revit happened in 2020–2021 and has held since.
  • Counting jobs overstates CAD. Among tagged residential jobs, CAD is 63% of jobs with hours but 39% of billings. The median CAD job is $50K of fee; the median Revit job $175K.
  • Finished residential jobs started inside the timesheet history lose money on CAD and make it on Revit. Billings-weighted, CAD -18% on 151 jobs and Revit 19% on 83; the typical job -25% against 27%. Counting only jobs billed at least 90% of fee: CAD -22% on 65, Revit 14% on 39. Started 2020 on: CAD -11% on 75, Revit 20% on 69.
  • Year by year says the same. Calendar 2020–2025, residential CAD jobs billed $9.4M against $11.6M of labour (-23%), below zero in 5 of 6 years; Revit billed $21.6M against $19.9M (8%), below zero in 2. In 2025: CAD -24%, Revit 8%.
  • Jobs started before 2019 are left out of every margin here. Deltek's timesheets begin in 2019, so a job billed before then carries lifetime billings against only part of its labour, and its margin reads high: 138 CAD jobs ($12.5M billed) and 73 Revit ($12.1M) would show 46% and 36%, which is not a real number for either.
  • Size and era travel with the tool. Recent CAD jobs are small: 65 of the 75 started 2020 on are under $100K, at -28%. The fee-band tables show the association; nothing here isolates the software from the size of the job or who ran it.
  • Partners are 75% of the labour cost at the imputed $250 an hour, 34% of billable hours. At their posted payroll amount instead, the finished-job margins would read 56% CAD and 62% Revit: the policy moves CAD by 74 points and Revit by 43, because partners put more of their hours on CAD jobs.
  • Coverage: 1,281 of 2,329 jobs with hours carry Deltek's drafting-type tag. Untagged residential jobs billed $3.8M; nothing here can split them.

Where every number comes from
  • Deltek Vantagepoint over the same ODBC route the Financials module uses. Top-level regular projects with engineering or drafting hours.
  • Hours: tkDetail. Engineering = labour codes 10 and 30, Drafting = 20. Rejected lines excluded. Timesheets begin in 2019.
  • Billed: PRSummaryMain, posted through October 2026. Fee: PR.Fee. US jobs converted at 1.36, a flat rate, not the year's.
  • Cost: Deltek's posted labour amounts (tkDetail RegAmt + OvtAmt + SpecialOvtAmt), the rate in effect when the hours were posted, the same figure Firm Health uses. Partner hours at the app's imputed $250/hr. The Historical screen prices hours at today's rate and prices staff who have left at zero; both of those figures are in the workbook beside this one.
  • Drafting type: the Deltek custom field CustDraftingType (RV, CAD, General). Sector: PR.ProjectType. Closed: status not Active, as the screen defines it.
  • Reproduced the app's cost and margin to the dollar on six projects before anything was written. Two independent pulls of drafting hours agree to the hour on every year and type. The same pull, banked in KorTransmittals through a different route, agrees on every sector, type and year cell. The Historical screen, filtered to Has Hours + Fee, shows 1,152 projects and $99.1M of fee; 1,146 of them are this report's population and the rest are 6 hourly-billed jobs with no fixed fee, which the screen counts through their hourly revenue. On the screen's fee measure (fixed fee plus hourly revenue) those 1,146 jobs are $99.0M; this report counts the fixed fee alone, $98.2M.
What this does not say
  • Only 1,281 of 2,329 jobs with hours carry a drafting-type tag. The untagged line is real work that cannot be split.
  • Jobs started before 2019 have no margin here. Their labour before 2019 is not in Deltek, and nothing in these files can recover it.
  • A "CAD job" is the tag on the job, not every hour. A job that moved to Revit mid-way counts where Deltek put it.
  • Margin is before subconsultants and before anything not in Deltek's cost rate. 4,038 of 457,898 billable hours (1%) carry no posted amount and cost nothing here, all of them 2019–2021.
  • Partner hours carry a policy rate, not a payroll rate, and it is most of the labour cost. The overview gives both readings.
  • These are associations between the tag on a job and its margin. Job size, era, client and who ran the job travel with the tag; the fee-band and cohort tables show that, they do not remove it.
  • Counting folders with a .dwg or .rvt in them counts jobs, not work. Of 31 jobs on one such sheet, Deltek tagged 18 and the sheet agreed with 15 of them; on 13 Deltek had no tag, and the folder check found CAD files on 5.
