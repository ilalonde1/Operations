## 3. Commissioning vs peer review — they are not the same thing
The lunch raised it, so here is the distinction precisely:

| | **Peer review** | **Commissioning** |
|---|---|---|
| When | During design | End of construction → occupancy |
| What | An independent engineer reviews *another firm's* structural design and reports on its adequacy | Verifying the *built* facility actually performs to design intent before it opens |
| Governed by | EGBC structural peer-review practice guideline (BC); owner-driven on post-disaster and health projects | **CSA Z8001 — Commissioning of Health Care Facilities** (companion to the Z8000 design standard); mandated by health authorities |
| Who leads | A senior structural engineer | Mechanical/electrical/controls specialists — most of what gets functionally tested is HVAC, med-gas, power |
| Deliverable | Review report (you never stamp the design) | Verification reports, functional test records, deficiency closure |
| Fee shape | Design-phase consulting fee | Field-heavy, smaller, recurring |

**The structural slice of healthcare commissioning — the lane KRA pointed at:**
- **Seismic restraint and anchorage verification** of equipment and non-structural components (CSA S832 territory) — every generator, rack, ceiling, and imaging machine needs restraint review and field verification.
- **Equipment-replacement structural assessments** — MRI/CT/imaging swaps in *operating* hospitals: small fees, tight logistics, infection-control protocols, night work — and they recur forever.
- **Floor vibration verification** for imaging suites and operating rooms; equipment pads, supports, and openings.

This work is fussy, low-glamour, and continuous — which is precisely why established firms avoid it, and why the seat is open.
