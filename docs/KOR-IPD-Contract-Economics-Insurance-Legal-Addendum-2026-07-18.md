## 1. CCDC 30-2025 — what actually changed versus CCDC 30-2018

**CCDC 30-2025 is real and confirmed released 2025-06-30**, superseding the 2018 edition, alongside updates to CCDC 5A, 5B and 17 (the construction-management family) and a new companion guide **CCDC 30-G–2025**. [V] — ccdc.org product page; corroborated independently by BLG (2025-07-29), Miller Thomson (2025-09-10), Gowling WLG (2025-07-31), Clark Wilson (2025-11-17), and Pallett Valo (2026-01-30). The base dossier (§2, §8 action 2) already knew a 2025 revision existed and was in use (Penticton's 2026 fire hall) but did not detail its content — that gap is closed below. Full contract text remains paywalled ($15–$140 depending on outlet/format: ccdc.org, OCA, RAIC, VRCA, OGCA, NLCA); everything below rests on 2+ corroborating law-firm bulletins per claim, same evidentiary standard as the base dossier.

**Structure.** The contract is reorganized around project lifecycle: **Validation Phase** (now formally defined as occurring after contract execution) → **Project Execution Phase** (design, procurement, construction, warranty) → General Articles/Conditions. A new **"Ready-for-Takeover"** milestone (imported from CCDC 2–2020) replaces the fuzzier "substantial performance" as the marker into the Warranty Phase. [V] — Gowling WLG, Clark Wilson, Miller Thomson, Pallett Valo all corroborate.

**Money mechanics — directly relevant to a consultant's fee-at-risk position:**
- **"Risk Pool" is formally renamed "Profit Pool."** CCDC's own stated rationale: the new term "more accurately reflects the intent and nature of the funds." This is a naming change, not (on the evidence found) a mechanical change to how the pool grows/shrinks. [V] — Gowling WLG (direct quote), corroborated by Clark Wilson and Pallett Valo.
- **Added Value Incentive Items (AVIIs)** — owner-selected upgrade/scope items funded from savings — can now be added **during the Construction Phase**, not only the Design Phase as in 2018. For a structural consultant this means scope-creep-via-incentive can now arrive later in the job, after your design effort is largely sunk. [V] — Miller Thomson, corroborated by Pallett Valo.
- Reimbursable-cost schedules were expanded and made more customizable, with overhead categories more clearly specified and audit procedures around reimbursables strengthened. [V] — Clark Wilson (2025-11-17); general corroboration (no line-item detail) from Gowling and Pallett Valo.
- **UNVERIFIED / flagged discrepancy:** one research stream surfaced a claim — attributed to cwilson.com/Pallett Valo/a Lexology-syndicated "2025 CCDC Contract Updates" summary — of a specific new liability cap ("greater of fee/contract price or $2M, capped at $20M"). A second, independent stream that read the Clark Wilson and Pallett Valo bulletins directly for CCDC 30 content specifically did **not** surface this figure. It is plausible this cap applies to the broader CCDC 5A/5B/17 design-build/CM family rather than CCDC 30 itself, or that it is accurate but wasn't captured on the direct read. **Do not cite a specific dollar liability cap for CCDC 30-2025 without pulling the actual contract text or a bulletin excerpt naming CCDC 30 by number.**

**Collaboration/governance additions:**
- **"Big Room"** co-location practice is now formalized in the contract text (not just informal IPD practice). [V] — Miller Thomson, Pallett Valo, Clark Wilson.
- New **optional "IPD Advisor" role**: an independent individual/organization "qualified to provide guidance on IPD and Lean principles," jointly appointed by the Project Management Team, chiefly to coach the team through Validation. [V] — Gowling WLG (direct quote), corroborated by Miller Thomson, Pallett Valo, Clark Wilson.
- Lean construction principles are now explicitly referenced in the contract text. [V] — Clark Wilson.

**Liability waiver — the clause that matters most to a signing consultant:**
- The core no-fault, mutual-waiver-of-claims structure among IPD Team members is **preserved unchanged** in 2025 — parties still waive claims against one another. [V] — Gowling WLG direct quote.
- **New: a mutual waiver of *consequential damages* was added across the CCDC 5A/5B/17/30 family** — but the term "consequential damages" is left contractually undefined, mirroring the (also criticized) approach in CCDC 2-2020. Two independent research streams corroborate this applies to CCDC 30 specifically (BLG 2025-07-29, read directly by two separate agents). Law-firm commentary flags the undefined term as a likely future dispute driver — exactly the kind of clause worth a principal's own read before signing. [V]
- Clark Wilson's direct caution (2025-11-17), consultant-relevant: once the Profit Pool is exhausted by an overrun, "the owner is still liable for the cost of the project to completion and is precluded from legal action" — i.e. the waiver still shields the consultant/contractor from owner claims over cost overruns even after profit is gone to zero. Clark Wilson explicitly says CCDC 30 (any edition) needs "careful review as to its suitability" before use. [V]
- A carve-out for **wilful misconduct, wilful default, fraud, criminal acts, and abandonment** from the liability limitation is confirmed as a 2025 feature of the CCDC suite generally, but the sources fetched directly named it only in the context of **CCDC 5A/5B/17** (Beale & Co, Mondaq/Pallett Valo). No fetched source's exact text ties this carve-out to CCDC 30 by name. **Flag as UNVERIFIED specifically for CCDC 30** — recommend confirming against the actual CCDC 30-2025 text (base dossier action 2: buy CCDC 30, ~$100) before repeating this as fact in front of a client or counterparty.
- No bullet found describing any change to **who** is covered by the waiver (e.g., extension to sub-consultants/trades) — no evidence of change either way.

**Joining-agreement mechanics for consultants.** No bulletin found describes a change to how a consultant executes onto the multi-party agreement (a standalone joining-agreement schedule, etc.). CCDC 30 remains a single multi-party contract signed from inception by owner/consultant/contractor (and key trades where applicable); smaller consultants can still flow through under a prime party. This appears **unchanged from 2018** on the evidence available.

**Validation Phase.** Now formally a distinct, defined phase occurring after contract execution, producing a Validation Report that sets Base Target Cost, Milestone Schedule and risk/contingency profile — after which the owner may proceed, adjust scope, or withdraw. Pallett Valo adds that termination/compensation procedures for a *rejected* Validation Report were clarified and enhanced in 2025. [V] — Gowling WLG, Clark Wilson, Pallett Valo all corroborate the framing.

**Insurance requirements.** No bulletin found gives CCDC 30-2025-specific insurance-clause changes; ccdc.org's own product description lists "insurance and contract security" as a covered topic but gives no detail behind the paywall. Treat as **unchanged pending direct document read** — see §3 below for the separate (broker-side, not contract-text-side) insurance picture.

**Termination/payment alignment.** A new cure-period/default-curing clause was added to termination provisions, and the contract was updated to align with provincial prompt-payment/adjudication legislation — explicitly noted by Clark Wilson as **excluding BC**, which still has no prompt-payment regime. [V] — BLG, Clark Wilson.

**Bottom line for §8 action 2 of the base dossier:** buying and reading the actual CCDC 30-2025 text remains the right move before any signature — this addendum resolves most of the "what changed" question from secondary sources, but two items (the specific liability-cap figure, and whether the wilful-misconduct carve-out text names CCDC 30) need the primary document to close out.

## 2. Structural-engineer-specific IPD economics — still does not exist, and now confirmed against the best possible source

**No public source isolates a Canadian structural or engineering consultant's own financial outcome (profit-pool share won or lost, fee-at-risk realized) on an IPD project.** This confirms and hardens the base dossier's §7 finding ("no published data isolates a structural engineer's profit outcome") rather than changing it — but it is now confirmed against the single best dataset that could plausibly contain it, which the base dossier had not located.

**The core source:** *Investigating Factors Leading to IPD Project Success in Canada* (Erik A. Poirier & Ahmad Arar, ÉTS/GRIDD; Sheryl Staub-French, Puyan Zadeh & Devarsh Bhonde, UBC BIM TOPiCS Lab; commissioned by the Integrated Project Delivery Alliance, published 2022-05). [V] — full PDF read directly: `ipda.ca/site/assets/files/4374/ipda_canadian_ipd_case_studies_final_28.pdf`.
- Methodology replicates the 2016 US "Motivation and Means" study (Cheng & Johnson, University of Minnesota/LCI/IPDA); interviews with virtually every signatory — **including the structural engineer, named, on all three projects** — plus document review and survey.
- **Barrie-Simcoe Emergency Services Campus** (Barrie, ON, $85.8M): structural engineer **LEA Consulting Ltd.**, interviewee John Ford.
- **Canada Games Aquatic Centre** (Kamloops, BC, $14.1M): structural engineer **WSP Canada Inc.**, interviewee Patrick Taylor (also IPDA Treasurer — see base dossier §5).
- **Thelma Chalifoux / Soraya Hafez Schools** (Edmonton, AB): structural engineer **RJC**, interviewee Jeffrey Rabinovitch.
- **The "Profit & Payout" finding, verbatim in substance**: all three projects paid out their full Incentive Compensation Layer — every signatory received full profit. Team-level savings and owner/team split ratios are disclosed (e.g., CGAC: $1M savings, split 70/30 owner/team). **At no point does the report break out what LEA, WSP, or RJC individually received, lost, or risked.** The only discipline-adjacent financial nuance anywhere in the document is an owner-vs-owner split on BSESC (one co-owner reported no meaningful savings, the other reported "millions") — not a consultant-level figure. [V]
- One non-financial but risk-relevant data point: on the Edmonton schools project, a team member "unexpectedly shut down their structural division" mid-project; the report says only that the team "recovered and promptly solved the issue," with no dollar figures attached and the affected firm not named. [V]

**Why this is the finding, not a gap in the search:** this is a well-resourced, methodologically rigorous study that specifically interviewed the structural engineers by name on three Canadian IPD projects, and it *still* reports only project-level ICL payout, never a discipline-level breakdown. That is a structural feature of how IPD financial reporting works — both contractually (the Profit Pool is a single team-wide fund) and in research practice — not a research-effort shortfall. [V]

## 3. Insurance — no doctrinal shift; one fresh Canadian citation, otherwise settled ground

**The substantive picture has not changed since well before the base dossier** — the load-bearing technical content (project-specific professional liability, or "PSPL," as the standard fix; rectification/mitigation coverage as the IPD-specific feature; the insured-vs-insured and contractual-liability-exclusion problems the mutual waiver creates for ordinary E&O) dates to 2023 and earlier (NFP, 2023-11-30, is the most technically detailed source found anywhere in this search, US-authored but broadly applicable). This addendum adds one genuinely new, genuinely Canadian citation and closes the loop on the "who runs Canada's engineer PL program" question.

**New (2025):** Navacord (formerly Lloyd Sadd), "Why Project-Specific Professional Liability Insurance is Essential in IPD Projects," ~2025-09-04. [V] — `lloydsadd.com/insights/why-project-specific-professional-liability-insurance-is-essential-in-ipd-projects/`. Direct quote: without PSPL in place, the CCDC 30 GC 10.1 mutual-claims waiver "falls apart" — because a claim under a PSPL policy doesn't require a lawsuit, so it doesn't collide with the contract's no-suit structure. **This is the current Canadian broker-consensus answer to the base dossier's open insurance question:** the recommended structure is **(a) a single project-specific professional liability policy covering the whole IPD team**, not per-firm endorsements to individual practice policies, and not a mandatory OCIP/CCIP wrap (though one secondary, unattributable-to-a-dated-source snippet suggested OCIP+PSPL combined as a "more sophisticated" option for larger projects — flagged UNVERIFIED for authorship/date).

**Rectification/mitigation coverage** remains the key IPD-specific policy feature: it pays to fix a design or construction defect *before* it becomes a claim, sidestepping the no-blame structure entirely. Confirmed by NFP (2023) and Navacord (2025) independently; concept traces back at least to a 2014 US broker article (Fenner-Esler) with only a maintenance-level 2025 touch, not new substance. [V]

**Who runs the standard Canadian engineer/architect PL program:** **Victor Canada** (formerly ENCON Group Inc., a subsidiary of Marsh & McLennan / Victor O. Schinnerer & Co.), jointly endorsed by **Engineers Canada and ACEC Canada since 1969**. Victor Canada publishes a "Single Project Coverage" product for architects, engineers, land surveyors, landscape architects and PMs on a named project — claims-made, full prior-acts coverage from first services on that project, limits to $25M, no insured-vs-insured exclusion. [V] — `victorinsurance.com/ca/en/pl/solutions/ae/single-project.html`. **The product page itself does not name IPD or mutual waivers explicitly** — this is a general single-project wrap that is a plausible fit for IPD use, not a confirmed IPD-branded product. A March 2024 Engineers Canada article on this program covers deductible mechanics only, no IPD content.

**Wider adoption context (relevant to how urgent this is for KOR):** the most current Canadian legal survey found — Chambers Practice Guide, "Construction Law 2026 – Canada" (BLG authors, published 2026-06-04) — confirms IPD/alliance adoption is "growing but limited" and flags **Quebec's Bill 62 (2024), effective 2026-04-01, formally opening municipal collaborative contracting** (IPD, progressive design-build, alliance) — new geography to watch, outside KOR's current AB/BC footprint but a signal of national momentum. This guide has **zero substantive coverage of PL insurance or mutual-waiver mechanics** — itself informative: insurance treatment of IPD is still a specialist/broker topic, not yet mainstream legal-practice doctrine, even in the country's most current construction-law survey. [V]

**No IPD-specific published content found** from Aon Canada, Marsh Canada, BFL Canada (as a dated, attributable byline), Gallagher/AJG Canada, HUB International Canada, Jones Brown, or Novex Insurance — despite targeted searches. This does not mean these brokers lack a view; it means they haven't published one publicly. The base dossier's §7 reference to "an Aon executive" flagging collaborative-contracting insurance risk is a 2021 quote (David Bowcott) — old, not 2025-2026 new; worth noting the base dossier's citation predates this addendum's window.

**Net assessment for KOR:** nothing here changes the base dossier's action item (§8.6: "broker conversation on E&O vs project-specific cover... before any signature"). What's new is a name to bring to that conversation — **Victor Canada / ENCON**, the ACEC/Engineers Canada-endorsed program administrator, plus the Navacord PSPL framing as talking points — not a change in what to actually do.

## 4. Legal / disputes — no new cases; one existing case has escalated to appeal

**No new Canadian IPD or alliance-contract court decision or arbitration award, beyond the two the base dossier already knew, was found** after a direct CanLII search and a law-firm-bulletin sweep — but one of the two known cases has moved stages, and the other's vintage needs correcting in the base dossier's framing.

**Onec Construction v. NWT Energy — now under appeal.** [V] The trial decision the base dossier cites (*Onec Construction Inc. v. NWT Energy Corp. Ltd. et al*, 2025 NWTSC 56 — Inuvik High Point Wind Project access road, NWT; contract termed "Integrated Project Delivery Contract," signed 2022-03-15) is confirmed unchanged in substance. **New:** the NWT Court of Appeal's official pending-appeals list (`nwtcourts.ca`, PDF, "as of 2026-07-18," pulled directly — primary source) shows docket **A-1-AP-2025-000011, "ONEC CONSTRUCTION INC. et al v. NORTHLAND BUILDERS LTD."** — judgment 2025-08-06, appeal filed 2025-09-03, Respondent Factum filed 2026-06-12, no hearing date yet scheduled. **The legal interpretation of "IPD contract" the base dossier draws from this case is therefore not yet final law** — flag this caveat wherever the case is cited going forward.

**The "Saskatchewan IPD arbitration" the base dossier references — identified, and it is old, not recent.** [V] This is almost certainly *Graham Design Builders LP v. Black & McDonald Ltd.* (Dr. F.H. Wigmore Regional Hospital, Moose Jaw, SK). Graham (CM/GC) vs. Black & McDonald (subcontractor); arbitrator awarded BML ~$12.0M cost-to-complete + ~$1.44M profit + interest; Graham's leave to appeal on interest/profit questions was **denied by the Saskatchewan Court of Queen's Bench in 2019 SKQB 161** (mixed fact/law, not pure law). Source: Glaholt Bowles, "IPD Gone Wrong: Graham Design Builders LP v. Black & McDonald Ltd.," 2021-02-21. **This is a 2018–2019 case, not a 2024+ development** — if the base dossier's phrasing implied recency, that should be corrected: it remains the field's original landmark Canadian IPD dispute, still cited as such as recently as Glaholt Bowles's 2025-12-05 provincial construction-law survey, with no successor Saskatchewan case since.

**Systematic negative result.** CanLII direct searches ("integrated project delivery," "CCDC 30," "alliance contract... dispute 2025 2026") and a sweep of 2025-2026 law-firm bulletins (Glaholt Bowles 2025-12-05, DWF Canada Winter 2026 brief 2026-02-13, Chambers "Construction Law 2026 – Canada" 2026-06-04) surfaced no additional IPD/alliance case law. [I] Two years of searching CanLII and law-firm bulletins turns up **zero instances of a design consultant or engineer being named as a party, or discussed as a target of liability, in any Canadian IPD/alliance dispute** — both known cases are owner/contractor/subcontractor payment-and-performance disputes with no consultant involvement. This absence is itself the risk-relevant finding for a structural engineering firm.

**Context, not a dispute:** Burnaby Hospital redevelopment is proceeding via BC's "Alliance model" (ReNew Canada/infrastructure coverage, 2026) — a live BC alliance project worth watching for future disputes given KOR's West Coast footprint (consistent with base dossier §6's BC alliance tracking).

**EGBC: no change since the base dossier (2026-07-03).** [V] Direct site searches and an indexed-crawl check of EGBC's news and practice-resources pages surfaced no EGBC-authored publication, bulletin, or guideline on IPD, alliance contracting, or CCDC 30. **APEGA: confirmed zero.** [V] `site:apega.ca` searches for IPD/alliance/CCDC 30 returned nothing; APEGA's full Practice Standards, Bulletins and Guidelines index (4 standards, 2 bulletins, 16 guidelines, 4 joint publications — most recent items a 2025-11-01 CPD standard and a 2025-04-01 authentication-requirements revision) was pulled directly and contains nothing on delivery models. APEGA has never published anything on this topic — a new, previously-unconfirmed data point the base dossier didn't have (it focused on EGBC).

**ACEC (national): nothing dedicated found.** [V] `acec.ca` searches and direct-fetch attempts (403'd, so search-index-reliant) surfaced no national position paper or bulletin on IPD/CCDC 30. One older award-profile mention: ACEC content cites the Drayton Valley Raw Water Treatment project (ISL Engineering) as the first Canadian municipal-infrastructure project delivered under CCDC 30, and the Kingston Third Crossing/Waaban Crossing as the first North American IPD bridge — pre-existing award content, not new guidance.

**ACEC-BC (formerly Consulting Engineers of BC, renamed 2012):** one adjacent document worth logging. [V] "Budget Guidelines for Engineering Services, Document 1 – Transportation & Infrastructure," Version 2, 2025-08 (`acec-bc.ca`), §7.2 "Risk Shared Consulting Assignments," verbatim: *"Risk-shared consulting assignments like Alliance or Integrated Project Delivery utilise alternate delivery arrangements that differentially share financial risk between project partners. Undertaking the necessary design work to assist with proposal preparation within a risk-shared consulting assignment involves considerable effort and risk on the part of the consulting engineer. For that reason, the consultant should be provided full remuneration for proposal development services."* **Caveat:** this exact language also appears in ACEC-BC's 2022-11 "Document 2 – Buildings" guidelines — it is carried-over boilerplate, not new 2025 substance; the August 2025 update appears aimed at general regulatory-alignment language, not an IPD-specific refresh. A separate ACEC-BC position paper, "Partnering Considerations for Small Firms" (2026-06-10), addresses subconsultant protections in **traditional** procurement only — explicitly not IPD-relevant, checked and ruled out.

**Upstream trigger, not yet acted on by any body:** CCDC ran a 30-session seminar roadshow (Apr–May 2025) introducing CCDC 30-2025, then a 24-session 2026 roadshow (Apr–May 2026, per a 2026-07-16 Daily Commercial News piece by Don Wall, quoting Geza Banfai of McMillan LLP) focused on the newer CCDC 32-2026 Progressive Design-Build documents. **None of these seminars were found to be hosted, co-branded, or promoted by EGBC, APEGA, or ACEC.** The CCDC 30-2025 release is the logical trigger for eventual professional-body guidance, but as of 2026-07-18, eighteen days after the base dossier, none has followed.
