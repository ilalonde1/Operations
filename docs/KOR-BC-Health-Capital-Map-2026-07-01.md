## 1. Executive summary
1. **The market is real but newly volatile.** BC Budget 2026 (Feb 17) cut the health capital plan **$15.4B → $11.1B**: Burnaby Ph2 ($1.7–1.8B) "re-paced," the Squamish/West Van LTC RFP re-paced, and **three Fraser Valley LTC design contracts cancelled outright (Apr 2026)**. Funding gates now matter as much as procurement gates — every target below carries both. [V]
2. **How the seat is won depends on the delivery model, and we verified the mechanics:** on **Alliance and Design-Build** work (the big hospitals), Infrastructure BC procures a *pre-assembled team* — the structural engineer joins a **GC/architect pursuit team before the RFQ closes**; the health authority never hires the SE directly. On **Construction-Management** work, the **owner procures the design team directly** — the only true direct-to-owner channel. On **Foundation-led private** projects (VGH+), the architect/Foundation picks. [V 0.8–0.85]
3. **The incumbency is a two-horse race:** **Glotman Simpson** (~10 documented BC health structural seats, incl. the new Kamloops Cancer Centre) and **Bush Bohlman** (the two largest single builds — Cowichan/Quw'utsun ~$1.5B and Surrey Memorial CCT — plus RCH Ph2), with RJC third (+ New St. Paul's shared with MKA). Common misattributions we killed: Entuitive's BC hospital roles are **envelope-only**; WSP holds **no** BC health structural-EOR credit; BC Cancer North was **RJC, not Fast+Epp**. [V]
4. **Three seats are decidable in the next ~6 months** (Section 3), five more inside 6–24 months — and the premier future gate is **NRGH Nanaimo (design-team RFP likely 2027–28)**, on Island geography where the Lower-Mainland incumbents have no local presence.
5. **KOR's wedge is geographic + seismic:** 3 of the 5 biggest upcoming projects sit in **high-seismic Metro Vancouver** (VGH campus, VGH+, Burnaby Ph2; Richmond adds liquefaction), and the Island/North pipeline (NRGH, Port Hardy, Kitimat) sits where Glotman/Bush Bohlman have no offices. Post-disaster hospital structural is KOR's core claim — aimed at the right subset.
