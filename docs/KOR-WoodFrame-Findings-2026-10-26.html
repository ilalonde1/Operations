A · The model and the drawings disagree

FindingWhat was measuredWhat it means
1 · 177 beams are the wrong product (Edgar, 31165-01)607 beam marks on Edgar's framing plans paired with the beam under each. 564 sit on a beam of the right size; 197 sit on a different product. 177 of those are marked B23 — 3½" × 11⅞" 1.55E Timberstrand on the schedule — on beams modelled as 3½" × 11⅞" 2.2E Parallam.The marks followed a substitution (Jason's B20 drafted as B23); the model did not. Anything taken off the model — quantities, schedules, a supplier list — carries Parallam where the drawings say Timberstrand. Where the substitution came from was not asked.
2 · Levels measured from two places (Edgar)2 of 12 level types are measured from a different base point, 93 ft from the other. 2,366 of 7,544 framing members are attached to Level 1 and raised to their floor by whole-storey offsets.The sheets look right. Anything that reads a level's height or a member's level from the model — an ETABS export, a schedule by level — gets it wrong.

B · No two wood jobs are drafted alike

FindingWhat was measuredWhat it means
3 · The framing sheet is made three ways"LEVEL N SHOWING LEVEL N+1 FRAMING OVER", read off each model:
Office template (v2): sheet S2.13 holds 6 schedule legends and no plan view.
Edgar: a ceiling plan of level N (view type "002 - WD STRUCT FRAMING OVER") stacked on a plan of N+1 ("002 - WD FLOORS AND COL OVER"), 1/8" scale, the schedule split into 7 legends.
Sherwood: two plans of type "001 - CONCRETE FOUNDATIONS PLAN", 3/16" scale, one combined legend.		Same sheet title, three different drawings. The office template has the legends but not the views, so a drafter starting from it has to invent the rest — which is how the two jobs diverged.
4 · The written view standard is not in the templateNelson's folder holds "Wood Framing View Setup" (July 2025). It names a view type "## - WD_STRUCTURAL WITH FRAMING OVER" and a view template per level, "## - WD_Structural with Framing Over Level N". The office template v2 carries 28 view templates and none of those. Edgar uses one template for every level; Sherwood uses 21 per-level templates of its own ("KOR - Wood frame - …"), none sharing a name with Edgar's.There is a written standard for the view — it never reached the template. Edgar's drafter followed it most closely.
5 · View templates multiplyEdgar's model holds 107 view templates, 38 of them for wood or framing, under three naming prefixes ("## -", "KOR_", "KOR -") — e.g. both "## - WD_STRUCTURAL WITH FRAMING OVER" and "KOR_WD_Structural with Framing Over".Duplicates come from loading again from another job. Two templates with one purpose drift apart, and a sheet can look different depending on which one a drafter picked.
6 · No standard wood model familyThe office template carries no wood beam or joist family to load (searched by name for beam, joist, lumber, TJI, Parallam, Timberstrand); it does carry a solid-wood post and a hold-down family. The office library (009_REVIT CUSTOMED) holds only older BMZ_ wood families, which neither job uses. Edgar's 2,085 beams use 7 families and 15 types; Sherwood's 911 beams are one placeholder type with the size in the mark. The same post family is "## - Solid Wood Columns" on Sherwood and "KOR_Solid Wood Columns" on Edgar; Edgar carries two hold-down families ("## - HD" and "KOR_HD").With nothing standard to load, every job builds its own, and schedules and tags split across the copies.
7 · Joists modelled on one job, drawn on the otherSherwood: 0 joist members, 212 span symbols. Edgar: 5,459 joist members plus span symbols. The office wood master: drawn in 2D as detail items.Only one of the three can be quantified or exported from the model.
8 · The office wood master predates modellingThe office wood master (00 Templates) is a concrete-only model with the wood drawn in 2D on the plan; the 3D sits in linked files. The catalogue's 148 wood-frame details are linework and arrows.The firm was told wood frame could not be done in Revit; the master reflects that. Wood framing is now ruled modelled, not drawn (KorStandards 112, your decision).

D · What exists now

Decisions in KorStandards

36 wood drafting rulings (migrations 109, 111, 112) — each a choice with its alternatives. Both jobs recorded against them, 20 of 21 groups each. The ETABS convention for wood floors is migrations 110 and 114.

The families have files

The four wood families that existed only inside Edgar's model — engineered-wood beam, TJI joist, solid-wood post, hold-down — are saved out as Revit 2024 files (the template's version), beside KOR_Beam Lumber. Every member kind now has a candidate .rfa.

The checks that found these

Every mark against its beam and the schedule (found finding 1 in seconds); each job's drafting choices measured; each framing sheet read off the model; the PDF route checked on all 57 projects.

Not yet built
