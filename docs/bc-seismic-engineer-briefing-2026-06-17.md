## 1. How the school program actually works (verified)

- **Program:** Seismic Mitigation Program (SMP). Provincially funded (now Min. of Infrastructure), delivered **through the school districts**. EGBC is the Ministry's technical advisor. **$1.9B+ spent; 233 done, 247 still on the list, 498 total** (May-2026 Progress Report — exact figures).
- **Entry point is the SPIR** — the Seismic Project Identification Report. *"The Ministry of Education requires that a School District submit a SPIR as the first step in requesting seismic upgrading funding."* It defines scope/schedule/risk and a Class C cost estimate; it feeds the PDR, then the Capital Project Funding Agreement, then design/construction.
- **The structural engineer is the PRIME on the SPIR.** Verbatim: *"The prime consultant for an SPIR shall meet the following requirements: (a) structural engineering consultant."* The SE *"shall retain and coordinate"* the A/M/E, geotechnical and cost-consultant team. This is unusual and it's in our favour — at the study stage **we lead, the architect subs to us.** (At detailed design/construction the prime typically reverts to the architect.)
- **SRG is mandatory.** *"The technical reference for all SPIR studies is the current edition of the Seismic Retrofit Guidelines."* Risk ratings (H1/H2/H3 priority blocks) come out of the SRG performance-based methodology.
- **TRB gate.** Every SPIR and PDR is reviewed by the **Technical Review Board** (APEGBC/EGBC-administered — structural + geotech engineers + cost consultants) **before the Ministry accepts it**; a SPIR is "formally complete" only after TRB cost-reviewer and TRB Panel-Lead sign-off. *(Operational note: SPIR submissions are administered through a Bush Bohlman desk — they staff the TRB review.)*

## 2. The gate — what a firm must have to do this work

This is the part that matters for "how do we get registered":

- **EGBC SRG training is the hard prerequisite.** Two independent confirmations:
  - SPIR Guidelines: the prime's project manager / project engineer must have completed the SRG training, *"as verified by a signed attestation."*
  - EGBC: *"Only firms who have had one or more registrants trained on the current SRG are granted access to the guidelines and seismic performance analyzer."*
- Training also unlocks the **Seismic Performance Analyzer** and, with a signed Confidentiality Agreement, the **BC Seismic Retrofit Program Database** (every school's risk + status — the prime *must* be a registered database user).
- EGBC publishes a **"Seismic Retrofit Engineering Firms" list** — effectively the prequalification roster. The incumbents on it include **RJC and Fast + Epp**.
- **Training confirmed (EGBC Knowledge Centre):** the course is **"Seismic Retrofit Guidelines 2023 Edition (SRG2023)"** — online video, **7 CE hours, $689** per registrant, tagged *Seismic School Program / Structural*. At that price it's a same-day decision. *(To confirm on enrolment: whether completion auto-adds KOR to the firm-access list + the Seismic Retrofit Companies roster, and any recertification when the SRG edition updates.)*

## 3. Our honest position

- **We have deep seismic capability** — heritage and high-importance/post-disaster work, Markulin on the CSA A23.3 Cl. 21 seismic committee, performance-based design experience. That's real and it's a strength.
- **But we have no K-12 school-seismic track record**, and districts shortlist on demonstrated school experience. RJC and Fast + Epp are the entrenched incumbents. So on schools we are a **credible challenger, not a shoo-in.**
- **The realistic path:** get SRG-trained, then go after **first SPIR (study) assignments** — lower stakes, SE-led, and they build the reference line we need — rather than expecting the large replacement projects out of the gate. Teaming with an established firm for a first credit is also on the table.

## 4. The other four doors (so we're complete)

- **Health / hospitals** — **PHSA maintains the central pre-qualified consultant list on behalf of all the health authorities**; we join via the annual Notice of Intent on BC Bid. Major hospital builds go P3/design-build through Infrastructure BC (Bush Bohlman is the structural incumbent there). High-importance/post-disaster category work — squarely our strength.
- **Provincial / municipal / post-disaster (fire halls, EOCs)** — **our best capability match.** No dedicated funding program (CEPF is flood/emergency-prep, grants go to governments), but there ARE rosters: **City of Vancouver's pre-qualified engineering-consultant roster** (refreshed Feb 2024, ~57 firms) and the **provincial Multi-Use List RFQ**. We get on those.
- **Bridges (MOTI)** — real, funded program via the **e-RISP roster** (roster selection under $1M, BC Bid over $1M), design to CSA S6:19 + the MOTI Vol. 1 §4 supplement, Lifeline/Major-Route/Other importance categories, peer review for Lifeline. **Not our lane — buildings, not bridges. We consciously skip it.**
- **Post-secondary** — institution-led; register on UBC's Bonfire portal, monitor BC Bid. Relationship-driven.
