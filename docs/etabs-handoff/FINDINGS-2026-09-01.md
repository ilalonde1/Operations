## B. Walls

### B1 — A wall of ours duplicates one of hers · OPEN
`KW164` is an **18-inch** wall standing **7.5 in** from her `W20` on `LEVEL 2`, parallel. They
overlap in plan: hers is a centreline, ours comes off an outline, and the two differ by up to half
the wall's width while being the same wall. `dxf.already-modelled-tolerance` is a flat **6 in**, so
it cannot see this.

Raising the test to `max(6, thickness/2)` was tried and **does work** — the report gained
"1 wall(s) and 0 column(s) were already modelled" — but `KW164` itself survived, so it reaches the
model by a second code path that does not run this check. **Find that path.**

### B2 — Every diagonal wall in the job is missing · OPEN
The drawings carry **105 diagonal `JBP_V-WALL` segments** (23 of them on sheets that are placed).
Both published models contain **zero** diagonal wall members — every wall in both files is exactly
0° or 90°. An entire geometry class is dropped or squared off, and no count can see it: it only
appears when the drawing and the model are put side by side in plan.

### B3 — Wall lengths that match no drawn segment · OPEN
On `LEVEL 2`, 11 of 15 generated walls match a drawn `V-WALL` segment length exactly. The other four
are off by **9 to 21 inches**:

    KW164  293.5 in   nearest drawn 272.5   (off 21.0)
    KW16   187.5 in   nearest drawn 198.8   (off 11.3)
    KW250  186.5 in   nearest drawn 176.0   (off 10.5)
    KW244  185.5 in   nearest drawn 176.0   (off  9.5)

Length is invariant under the drawing→model rotation, so this is a real discrepancy in the
decomposition, not a coordinate artefact.

### B4 — Drawn wall rings that produce nothing · OPEN

**63 rings resolved to zero panels**. Worst offenders:

### B5 — Walls standing off every plate · OPEN · low

## C. Reconciliation between drawing and model

### C1 — The comparison is one-directional and the baseline is wrong · OPEN
A sheet-by-sheet join of drawn rings against the report's ledger reported "78 walls with no drawn
ring behind them" on 31168. **That number is not trustworthy**: the baseline counted only CLOSED
rings, and open chains also make walls. It must be rebuilt before it is used for anything.

The sound instrument is length-matching (B3), because length survives the transform. Position
matching needs the drawing→model transform, which the pipeline knows and the checks do not.

### C2 — The two 31168 models cannot be compared by storey name · OPEN · 2 tests red
`C-LEVEL 3` sits 5.5 in above `LEVEL 3` — inside `dxf.storeys-at-one-level-gap` of 12 in, so they
are one physical level drawn twice. The site file keeps both; the cut drops `LEVEL 3`, re-homes the
members standing inside building C's footprint onto `C-LEVEL 3`, and cuts the towers' away.

So `TheTwoPublished31168ModelsAgreeOnEveryStoreyTheyShare` and `...PriceBuildingCTheSame` compare
C's share in one file against C's share plus what was re-homed in the other, and report three
storeys of a correct pair as wrong. **The models are not at fault.** Folding the whole twin in was
tried and is worse — `LEVEL 3` carries the towers' 40-odd walls. The comparison needs to know which
members were re-homed, which is the cut's business and is not recoverable from the finished files.

## D. Intake

### D1 — Bridge 1.0.35 is not deployed · OPEN
`BridgeExec.SafeName` maps every invalid filename character to `_`, so two Revit views differing
only in such a character produce one filename. Before v1.0.35 (commit `95bf6e0`, 1 Sep) the loser
was **deleted and the winner moved over it** — a lost drawing, reported as a skip, in a run
reporting success. The shipped 31168 set was exported 26 Aug from a build without it.

Checked: every storey carrying members traces to a sheet, and the only storey with no sheet
(`B-LEVEL 41`) is explained — building B's top sheets are LEVEL 39 (ROOF) and LEVEL 40 (UPPER ROOF),
and the storey-shift rule puts that sheet's 2 walls on storey 41. **That is evidence, not proof:
the collision leaves no trace in the output folder.** There is no manifest, so a collision cannot be
detected after the fact. The definitive test is a re-export under 1.0.35 diffed against the 139.

### D2 — Wall strengths per type are not read · OPEN
They are in the schedules; nothing reads them into the DXF→ETABS path.

### D3 — Bluebeam markups · OPEN · re-test
A previous pass concluded they are rebar detailing and therefore not useful here. That conclusion
was reached quickly and it decides a whole input.

### D4 — Slab strength · CLOSED, dead end
It is on no drawing. Page 30 of the stick file prints `14" SLAB` — thickness only.

## F. Do not repeat these

### F1 — "Carry a member down until something supports it" · WITHDRAWN
Written for A1 and **withdrawn after measuring the second building**. Uncapped it took 31138 from
224 walls and 307 columns to **455 and 587**; capped at one storey it still reached **362 and 379**.
31138 is a gap-fill model where the engineer has already drawn the walls, so few positions carry a
plate of ours and members march downward. Inventing storeys of structure is the one thing this tool
must not do.

Stashed as `stash@{0}`, with the `max(6, thickness/2)` change from B1 in the same stash.

**The lesson, and it is rule 9 in `CLAUDE.md`:** one command must measure EVERY deliverable. This
looked correct on 31168 (+17 columns, exactly the hanging ones) and was only caught because 31138
was measured too.

### F2 — Filtering generated members on the `K` prefix
It also catches `KF` floor plates and `KS` headers, and filling a stack of those puts a SLAB on the
storey between two floors. Walls and columns are `KW` and `KC`.
