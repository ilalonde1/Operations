An inch is an inch in every unit: the shell's material, the storey reader's limits, and a feet model's precision

Briefs CODEX-ENGINEERING-FIX-1 and -1B, by Codex; verified on the dev box 2026-10-19.

The fault was one class: inch literals applied to values measured in the model's own unit. The PDF route writes
millimetre models, and the corpus holds 159 reference models in feet and 29 in millimetres.
- E2kShellBuilder wrote a 4000 psi mix as lb-in numbers under UNITS LB MM, which reads as 4000 lb/mm2. Ground truth:
  an ETABS export (30783-01, KIP/IN) stores the same mix as FC 4, E 3604.997.
- E2kDocument compared eight inch literals (480, 60, 12, 36) with model-unit elevations and distances.
- E2kGeometryComposer wrote lengths to 3-4 decimals in every unit; in feet an 8 in wall became 8.004 in.

The fix:
- One unit table: E2kDocument.InchesPerUnit.
- Materials in the declared unit.
- Each literal scaled by the model's inch.
- Decimals that follow the unit.

The existing unit differential (AModelIsTheSameInInchesAndMillimetresTests) gains a feet arm and compares materials,
storey elevations and floor-below. Inch and millimetre output formatting is unchanged.
