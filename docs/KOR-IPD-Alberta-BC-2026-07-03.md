## 1. Executive summary
1. **IPD is a contract, not a philosophy.** In Canada it means CCDC 30 (published 2018, revised 2025): one multi-party agreement signed together by the owner, architect, key engineers, the contractor, and major trades. Costs are reimbursed open-book; **each firm's entire profit goes into a shared pool** that grows or shrinks with the whole team's performance against a jointly-set target cost; parties waive claims against each other except for wilful default. [V]
2. **It is a different seat from the one KOR sits in today.** In our normal Prime Consultant work, KOR is the architect's sub-consultant: the architect holds the owner contract, picks us, and our fee and liability are conventional. In IPD, KOR would **co-sign with the owner and the builder**, put its profit at risk on the team's collective result, and join before design starts. Section 3 has the full comparison. [V]
3. **Alberta is one of Canada's two IPD centres of gravity — but it's a small, repeat-owner club, not a wave.** Confirmed owners: Edmonton Public Schools (the flagship program), Red Deer Catholic, City of Edmonton, Strathcona County, EPCOR, Red Deer Polytechnic, plus private one-offs. **There is no IPD in Alberta health capital and no Alberta Infrastructure IPD framework** (its alternative-delivery play is P3). The structural seats belong overwhelmingly to **RJC** (at least five corroborated projects, seven on its own roster, with named regional IPD leads), with single projects held by Entuitive, Eng-Spire, and Fast+Epp. [V]
4. **The precedent that matters: a Vancouver structural firm has already won an Alberta IPD seat** — Fast+Epp held it on the Mosaic Centre (Edmonton, Alberta's first IPD). Entry is documented and relationship-shaped: the owner picks the contractor and architect first, and that core hand-picks the consultants on collaborative track record and culture fit — **prior IPD experience is scored, never mandatory, and training happens after selection**. A cold RFQ from an unknown out-of-province firm has no precedent. [V]
5. **In BC, the ramification is a fork.** True IPD is rare (~3–5 projects ever) and lives in **municipal work — where owners procure the structural engineer directly** (Penticton posted a standalone "Structural Consultant: IPD" RFP in 2026). The big BC collaborative wave is the **health-capital Alliance model** — IPD's sibling, now the default for hospital megaprojects — where **engineers do not sign the alliance agreement** and the seat is reached through the contractor/architect pursuit team, exactly like our Prime Consultant playbook. [V]
6. **The money and risk, honestly:** every published Canadian IPD study we found paid the team full profit (Mosaic finished 12% under market), but no structural-engineer-specific outcome is published anywhere; standard professional-liability insurance has named IPD gaps that need a broker's work before any signature; and IPD disputes still reach courtrooms despite the no-suit clause. [V]

## 2. What IPD actually is — at the contract level [V unless noted]
- **The document:** CCDC 30 — a single poly-party contract (real Canadian teams have had 24+ signatories). Smaller consultants can alternatively join via a flow-through "joining agreement" under a prime party. The 2025 revision is already in use (Penticton's 2026 fire hall is CCDC 30-2025).
- **The money:** audited, open-book **burdened costs are reimbursed** regardless of outcome; **profit is fixed as a lump sum and pooled**. Underrun: full profit plus a share of savings (typical published structures: an owner hurdle of ~2%, then up to 80% to the team, capped at 50–100% of target profit). Overrun: the pool is consumed proportionally — down to zero. The floor (costs always reimbursed) is the market norm but **negotiated, not automatic**.
- **The liability deal:** parties waive claims against each other except wilful default, and defend third-party claims jointly. This is the clause that makes conventional E&O insurance imperfect for IPD (Section 7).
- **The process:** a paid **validation phase** first — the integrated team tests whether scope fits budget and can honestly kill the project there. Then design with **target value design** (designing to the cost, continuously estimated with the trades in the room), big-room/co-location sessions, Last Planner scheduling, and heavy BIM. For the structural engineer this front-loads effort: the fabricator is commenting on your framing scheme while it's still a sketch.
- **What it is not:** "integrated" or "collaborative" in a press release does not mean IPD. Design-build, construction management, alliances, and progressive design-build are all different contracts with different seats for the structural engineer (Section 3). Several famous "IPD" examples failed verification — see Sections 4 and 6.

## 6. BC — what IPD means at home [V unless noted]
- **True IPD in BC is rare: roughly 3–5 projects ever.** Kamloops Canada Games Aquatic Centre (2019 — "BC's first IPD"; WSP co-signed as an engineer party), 1st & Clark Vancouver (BC Housing/City/VCH — reported as BC's first CCDC 30, lower confidence), Burnaby Fire Halls #4 and #8 (**RJC structural**), and Penticton's fire hall (**CCDC 30-2025, signed June 2026** — Chandos again). Famous "BC IPD" claims that failed verification: UBC Brock Commons (construction management), Cowichan Hospital (Alliance, not IPD).
- **The important BC fork for a structural engineer:** in the **municipal IPD lane, owners procure the SE directly** — Penticton posted a standalone "Structural Consultant: IPD" RFP, and Kamloops co-evaluated engineer proposals at the owner table. That is a door KOR can walk through without a sponsor. In the **health-capital Alliance lane** (Cowichan, Richmond, Burnaby — now the Province's default for hospital megaprojects), the alliance agreement is bespoke (Australian-inspired, not CCDC 30), signatories stop at owner/contractor/architect, and **engineers do not sign** — the structural seat is reached through the GC/architect pursuit team before the RFQ, which is exactly the Prime-Consultant-shaped path in KOR's existing playbook. A real 2024 alliance RFQ (Richmond Hospital) scored prior collaborative experience at only 20/100 — and key individuals' résumés at 50/100. **No structural firm has been confirmed as a direct BC alliance signatory** (architects have been).
- **Trajectory:** growing, under the "Alliance" name — Infrastructure BC's own published alliance project count rose across its 2025 publications to roughly nine, spanning hospitals and highways [I — count assembled from IBC publications; the direction is solid, the exact tally less so], and it states it will continue using the model. Tempered by Budget 2026 capital re-pacing: Burnaby Hospital Phase 2 paused, the Fraser River Tunnel collaborative contract terminated. Next likely collaborative owners: VCH (Richmond Phases 2–3, construction 2026–29), Northern Health (UHNBC), municipal fire halls (Vancouver Firehalls 2 and 9 — rumoured, UNVERIFIED), BC Housing. No standing contractor–architect alliances exist in BC, but repeat pursuit pairings are visible: **EllisDon+DIALOG**, EllisDon+Parkin/ZGF, and Chandos+Johnston Davidson.
- **Regulatory reality:** EGBC has published nothing on IPD, alliances, or CCDC 30 (verified absence) — the only firm-level constant is the mandatory individual secondary-liability program (~$250K). There is no BC-specific legal commentary on the IPD insurance question at all; the closest is a Vancouver construction lawyer's 2013 observation that alliance risks can be "difficult to insure." KOR would be working from first principles with its broker — like everyone else.

## 7. The money and the risk — what KOR would actually sign up for [V unless noted]
- **Downside is capped but real:** costs reimbursed, profit at risk. On an overrun — anyone's overrun, fault-irrelevant — the shared profit pool drains toward zero. You can do excellent structural work and still earn nothing above cost because a trade partner blew its budget. That is the deal; it is also why teams pick partners so carefully.
- **The published record is good:** every Canadian IPD study found paid the team its full profit; Mosaic Centre finished 12% under comparable market cost. **No published data isolates a structural engineer's profit outcome on any Canadian IPD project** — the claim "consultants do well in IPD" rests on team-level results and testimonials. [V for studies; the gap is the point]
- **Insurance needs real work before any signature:** standard E&O has named IPD gaps (insured-vs-insured exclusions when parties share one contract; contractual-liability exclusions against the waiver regime; no cover for rectifying your own work). The documented fixes are project-specific professional liability policies or written carrier endorsements. An Aon executive has publicly flagged collaborative contracting "without the right insurance design" as an emerging industry risk. This is a broker conversation, not a form to sign.
- **"Aligned incentives" is not "dispute-proof":** an IPD project reached the NWT Supreme Court in 2025 (*Onec Construction v. NWT Energy*), and a Saskatchewan IPD dispute went through arbitration and appeal. Rare, but the no-suit clause has edges.
