# The BD Radar — how to use it in five minutes

**For:** regional managers — Vancouver, Vancouver Island, Okanagan, Alberta, US.
**Date:** 9 October 2026.
**Companion to:** your region's *field guide* and *playbook* (June 2026) — those say where the
market is. **This says what the system is holding for you today**, and what to click.

---

## The one thing to understand

The system watches two completely different things, and they need opposite reactions.

| | **SIGNALS** | **BIDS** |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Somebody applied to build something | Somebody is asking for prices |
| How early | Months to years before a tender | Now |
| Is the engineer chosen? | **Usually not** | **Almost always yes** |
| What you do | Find out who the developer and architect are. **Phone them.** | Decide whether to bid, by the deadline |
| Why it matters | This is where a relationship gets made | This is where you compete on price |

**Signals is the point of the whole thing.** By the time something is a Bid, somebody else has
usually already been chosen as the structural engineer. A Signal is the chance to be that
somebody on the *next* one.

---

## Opening it

**Home → Business Development → Opportunities.** You get three tabs:

- **Signals** — applications and rezonings. Sorted **newest first**.
- **Bids** — RFPs and tenders. Sorted **closing soonest first**.
- **Mine** — whatever you have claimed.

Under those, two controls that matter:

- **Work size: "Smaller work collapsed."** Leave it. It hides sheds, decks, signs, single
  houses and tenant improvements. It tells you how many it hid, and **"Show"** brings them
  back if you want them.
- **"To grab"** — tick it to see only what nobody has claimed yet.

---

## Doing something about one

1. **Click a row.** Read the **Name** and the **Buyer**.
2. **Look at who filed it.** The applicant is often an architect or a developer — that is the
   person worth knowing.
3. **Press `Grab`** (the green button on the row). That claims it under your name so two of us
   don't phone the same person. It moves into **My Pursuits**.
4. **Open My Pursuits** and work it there: contacts, calls, stages, files.
5. Need something to send? **Brief** builds a one-page PDF on the opportunity. **TOOLS →
   Make a Brief…** does a fuller one on an organisation.

That is the whole loop. **Find it → Grab it → Phone them → Log it in My Pursuits.**

## Three things that will otherwise confuse you

**1. A Signal has no deadline, but it goes cold.** A rezoning filed in 2017 is history; one
filed last week is a phone call. That is why Signals is sorted newest first — **work from the
top.**

**2. The score is a hint, not a verdict.** It reads the words in the application. A project
described badly scores badly. **Open things that look interesting even if the number is low.**

**3. If a screen is empty it will say so.** A blank screen with no message used to mean
"broken". Now it tells you whether there is genuinely nothing, or you need to search first.

## If something looks wrong

Tell Ian. Specifically useful: *"this row should not be here"*, *"this one is ranked far too
low"*, *"my region is missing X council"*. Each of those is a fixable rule, and the system was
built to be corrected.

⚠ **Known gaps, so you don't waste time looking:** Burnaby, Richmond, Langley Township, Delta,
Abbotsford and Chilliwack publish applications we cannot yet read — roughly 1.9 million people
of the BC footprint. Kelowna publishes nothing usable at all. We hold **16 of 56** BC
municipalities, covering 52% of the footprint's population. Your region's page says exactly
what that means for you.
