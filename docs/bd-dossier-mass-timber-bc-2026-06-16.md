## 1. Market & policy drivers
- **Wood First Act (2009):** wood preferred for provincially funded buildings; raised wood-frame to 6 storeys — seeded the market. ([gov.bc.ca/mass-timber](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/industry/construction-industry/mass-timber))
- **BC Building Code — Encapsulated Mass Timber (EMTC):** 2018 → 12 storeys; **BCBC 2024 (eff. Mar 8 2024) → 18 storeys** for residential/office, **exposed mass timber to 8 storeys**, and crucially **new occupancies: schools, libraries, retail, light/medium industrial, care facilities**. This 2024 expansion is the single biggest near-term demand driver — it unlocks school boards, health authorities, industrial and retail owners as first-time mass-timber clients. ([gov.bc.ca news](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2024HOUS0055-000522))
- **BC Mass Timber Action Plan (2022)** + Office of Mass Timber Implementation; sector projected to ~$403M by 2035. Includes the **Mass Timber Demonstration Program (MTDP)** — up to $500k/project, 4 intakes to date, 20+ demo buildings. ([masstimberbc.ca/funded-projects](https://www.masstimberbc.ca/funded-projects/))
- **GCWood (federal, NRCan):** $92k–$500k/project; BC recipients incl. Fast+Epp, Vienna House, Nexus Penticton, Ronald McDonald House. ([NRCan](https://natural-resources.canada.ca/corporate/planning-reporting/evaluation-green-construction-through-wood-gcwood-program))
- **BC Builds / BC Housing** financing affordable mass-timber rental (e.g. North Vancouver 225 E 2nd, 18 storeys). **CleanBC** embodied-carbon targets reinforce demand.
- **Scale:** ~450 mass-timber buildings complete or under construction in BC + 60 in planning; BC has ~20× the per-capita mass-timber supply of the rest of North America. ([BIV](https://www.biv.com/news/real-estate/mass-timber-going-mainstream-in-bc-despite-growing-pains-11206596))

## 3. Structural-engineer competitive landscape
- **Fast + Epp — the dominant incumbent.** Brock Commons, Grandview Heights, Richmond Oval, BCIT, The Hive, Marpole, Cameron, River District, the VSB schools. Offices Vancouver/Edmonton/Calgary/Seattle/NY/Germany/Toronto. Moat = Paul Fast's research/code influence + repeat architect ties (hcma, Diamond Schmitt, DIALOG, Francl, GBL, Perkins+Will). **Primary displacement target, but near-impregnable on trophy towers.** (Verified email on file: pfast@fastepp.com.)
- **Aspect Structural Engineers — strong #2**, mid-market/institutional: Tallwood 1 (first 12-storey EMTC in Canada), Lauwelnew School, Malahat Skywalk. *Not currently a canonical in KOR's graph at the timber tier — worth adding/tracking.*
- **Equilibrium Consulting** — pioneer (WIDC, UBC ESB); shifting to larger/international (Google MT1). Less visible in current BC pipeline.
- **StructureCraft (Abbotsford)** — engineer-led specialty SE + fabricator (DowelLam/DLT). Comes in via GC/developer, not architect selection.
- **Glotman Simpson** — upper-end hybrids (M5 25-storey, Earth Tower concept, UBC Pharma Sciences). Less active in mid-market civic/school.
- **RJC Engineers** — broke in on UBC Gateway (CREE hybrid) — proof a national firm can win large institutional mass timber; also delivered **The Exchange** mass-timber office in **Kelowna** (2024) from its Interior office — RJC is active in *interior* mass timber too.
- **ROV Engineering (Kelowna)** — the local Interior high-rise + mass-timber specialist; SE of record on **Capstone** (9-storey mass-timber affordable housing, BC Housing, 2025). The Okanagan-local timber competitor to know — and a different firm from the coastal incumbents.
- **DIALOG** — self-engineers its own timber buildings (FNHA HQ).
- **WHM / Bush Bohlman** — [UNCERTAIN] not surfaced in mass-timber records externally, though KOR's graph shows **WHM on the Penticton Nexus** mass-timber project — a real Interior data point worth confirming.

## 6. KOR BD angle — where to actually win
KOR is **not** positioned to take trophy towers from Fast+Epp, the Diamond Schmitt+Fast+Epp civic lock, or DIALOG self-engineered work. The realistic, winnable lanes (all enabled or expanded by the 2024 code change):
1. **Mid-market residential mass timber (6–12 storeys)** — non-profit/BC Housing/CMHC developers and First Nations housing where **no SE is committed yet** (the MTDP list shows several with unknown SE). Most accessible entry.
2. **Mass-timber schools outside the VSB/Fast+Epp orbit** — Kelowna, Victoria/Saanich, Langley, Abbotsford, Surrey, Okanagan districts; 2024 code explicitly added schools. *Maps directly onto the SD owners KOR already tracks.*
3. **Sub-metro civic / recreation** — municipalities without a Diamond Schmitt relationship (Kelowna, Kamloops, Chilliwack, Nanaimo, Okanagan).
4. **Newly code-unlocked industrial + care facilities** — essentially zero SE incumbency; sustainability-driven owners.

**Architect relationships to target:** hcma, GBL, Francl, Jack James/Design Build Services, Indigenous-focused practices (Formline, Raven Inside), Arcadis IBI, smaller Okanagan/Island firms.
**Supply-chain play:** build a **Kalesnikoff** design-assist relationship — they're aggressively growing the market and pull SEs/developers in.
**Positioning:** be the **"mass-timber entry partner" for first-time adopters** (design + system selection + code/fire navigation), not the 25th-storey trophy competitor.
