KOR Structural · Standard Details

How it works

One governed set of standard details: 603 of them, approved, numbered, and the same for everybody. This is what it is, what you do with it as a drafter, and what you do as the person who keeps it right.

The idea, in three sentences

1 · ONE SET

One place, not ten jobs

Every standard detail lives in one numbered catalogue and one Revit master template. No hunting through old jobs, no copy of a copy with somebody else's project name still on it.

2 · ONE DECIDER

One person says what's in

A detail is in the master only if the gatekeeper approved it. That is the whole point: what a drafter can place is what the firm has agreed to stand behind.

3 · ONE FIX

Fixed once, for everyone

A detail that is wrong gets corrected once, in the master, by the gatekeeper. Everybody picks it up on their next job. Nobody patches it locally and forgets.

Your first hat — drafting

Start a jobOpen KOR-Standards-Master-R25.rvt from I:\KOR-Standards\template\MASTER\ → Save As into the job → delete the disciplines the job doesn't need. Don't start from an old job.
Use a detailIt's already in your file — you copied the master. Every one is numbered KOR-D-##### on the view. Quote that number whenever you talk about one.
A detail is wrongYou're the gatekeeper now, so it's yours to fix — see over the page. Anyone else tells you, with the number.
You need one that isn't thereDraw it in the job as you would today, and carry on. Making it a standard is a separate, deliberate act — and it is now yours to perform. See over the page.
The Revit toolsThere's a KOR toolbar that pulls a single approved detail straight into an open job, and places the standard parts. It is not on your machine and won't be until you ask for it. Say the word and it takes about ten minutes; your existing tools are untouched either way, and it can be taken off again just as cleanly.

What the words mean

Master — the Revit template every job starts from. Approved details only.
KOR-D-##### — a detail's permanent number. It never changes, and a retired one is never reused.
Approved — the gatekeeper has said it is a KOR standard.
Gatekeeper — one person decides what is in the master. That is now you.
The app — KOR Operations → Standard Details. Where the deciding happens.

Where it lives

The catalogue + the drawings

The master is read-only to everyone but you. Copying it always works.

Your second hat — gatekeeper

What the job actually is

Engineers review the details and tell you what they want changed. You decide, you make the change in Revit, and you publish. Nothing reaches the drafters until you publish. It is a few minutes a week, not a project.

How work reaches you, and what you do with it

An engineer reviewsIn the app they open a whole set as one PDF and mark it up in Bluebeam — the same way they redline a drawing. You get the marked-up PDF back.
A detail is goodIn the app: pick it → Approve. It becomes placeable and goes into the master at the next publish.
A detail is wrongReject it. It drops out of the master and stays out until it's fixed. Nothing is deleted — it keeps its number and its history.
Fixing itYou fix the drawing in Revit, in the standards model — not in a job. That's the one place a detail is ever edited.
Its title is wrongTools ▾ → Edit title / discipline…. Both are recorded with your name and the reason you give.
It should go awayTools ▾ → Retire detail…. It needs a reason — the app won't let you skip it — and it can be restored if you change your mind.
PublishingIn the app: Tools ▾ → Publish to Master…. It rebuilds the master from the approved set and keeps the previous one. That's the moment a change reaches the office.

Adding a brand-new detail

1 · Draw itIn the standards model (AUTHORING), as an ordinary drafting view. Draw it the way you'd draw anything.
2 · Add itIn the app: + Add detail. It lists the drawings in the model that aren't standards yet. Pick yours, type a title, choose discipline and type.
3 · It gets a numberThe app takes the next KOR-D-#####, records the detail against that exact Revit view, and writes the number onto the view for you — so the model and the catalogue can't drift apart.
4 · Approve and publishIt starts unverified: it's in the catalogue but no drafter can place it. Capture its drawing, Approve it, then Publish to Master…. Now everybody has it.

How an engineer gets a new detail in

Through you — there is no "submit a detail" button, and deliberately so. An engineer who wants a new standard sends you a markup, a sketch, or a detail out of a live job, and says what it's for.

You draw it in the standards model, add it, and approve it. That is the whole point of one decider: a detail becomes a KOR standard because a person chose to make it one, not because somebody uploaded it.

What an engineer can do on their own

Find any detail in seconds and open it as a PDF in Bluebeam.
Open a whole set — all the concrete details, say — as one PDF to mark up.
Build their own sheet from approved details and get a PDF of it, for their own use. It is stamped an uncontrolled working copy, so it can never be mistaken for the standard.
Approve or reject, if they're one of the named engineers.

What you need, and what you already have

To decideThe KOR Operations app, with your name on the approver and publisher lists. That's done — it arrives with the next version of the app.
To fix and drawRevit with the standards model. You already have Revit.
To add and publishA small add-in that lets the app drive your Revit for the few seconds an add or a publish takes. It does nothing the rest of the time. Not on your machine yet — it goes on when you say so.
To place detailsThe KOR toolbar, only if and when you want it. Nothing here depends on it.

The one habit

Everything comes from the master. Everything goes back through you.

If a detail is wrong, it gets fixed once and published — not patched in a job. That single habit is what makes the set worth trusting, and it is the whole reason the job exists.
