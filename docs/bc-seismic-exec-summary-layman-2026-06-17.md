## The situation in one paragraph

British Columbia has been spending billions making public buildings — starting with schools — safe in an earthquake. Our Biz Brain software flagged that **KOR is not registered to do this work.** I dug into it. The good news: getting in is mostly a matter of one specific training step that we control, not a complicated bidding process. The honest news: on schools specifically we'd be a newcomer competing against firms that have done dozens of them. This summary explains what the program is, how you get in, and what we should do.

## What is the program?

It's called the **Seismic Mitigation Program (SMP)**. The Province (now through the Ministry of Infrastructure) funds it, and **local school districts actually run the projects**. Engineers and Geoscientists BC (EGBC) is the technical referee. The Province has spent **over $1.9 billion** so far. As of May 2026, **233 schools are done and 247 are still on the to-do list** (498 in total). So roughly half the work is still ahead — a large, published, predictable pipeline.

There is **no single "seismic program" to sign up for** — there are actually **five separate doors**, each run by a different body:

1. **Schools** — the big one above.
2. **Hospitals / health** — run by the health authorities.
3. **Bridges** — run by the Ministry of Transportation. *(Not our type of work — we do buildings, not bridges. We skip this one.)*
4. **City & government buildings, fire halls, emergency centres** — run by cities and the Province. *(This is actually our strongest match.)*
5. **Universities & colleges** — each campus runs its own.

## Can we just sign up?

For **schools**, the key that unlocks everything is **EGBC's "Seismic Retrofit Guidelines" (SRG) training.** By law of the program, only firms that have an engineer trained on the current guidelines are allowed to do this work — and being trained unlocks the official tools and the province-wide database of every school's earthquake risk. **This is a step we can take on our own initiative; it doesn't require winning a bid first.** That's the single most important action.

There's also a surprising point in our favour: on the first stage of a school project — the engineering study — **the structural engineer is the lead consultant** (the "prime"), with the architect working under them. That's the opposite of how most public projects work, where we'd be a sub to the architect. So on these studies, **we can lead.**

For the other doors: **hospitals** have a pre-qualified consultant list run centrally by the PHSA health authority that we apply to join; **cities and the Province** keep pre-qualified engineering rosters (the City of Vancouver refreshed theirs in 2024) that we get onto — these cover the **fire halls and emergency buildings that are exactly KOR's strength.**

## What's the catch for us?

We are a **newcomer on schools.** Two firms (RJC and Fast + Epp) have done many of them and have the track record that school districts look for. So realistically we **train up, then win our first one or two of the smaller study assignments to build a reference**, rather than walking in expecting the big replacement projects. Where we're genuinely strong — earthquake upgrades to important, "must-stay-standing" buildings like fire halls and emergency centres — we should lean in.

## What should we do now?

1. **Get a KOR engineer through EGBC's SRG training** — confirmed: it's a **$689, 7-hour online course** ("Seismic Retrofit Guidelines 2023 Edition") in the EGBC portal. This is the #1 move, and at that price it's a same-day decision.
2. **Sign EGBC's agreement to access the school-risk database.**
3. **Apply to the health authority (PHSA) consultant list and the City of Vancouver / provincial engineering rosters.**
4. **Decide our schools approach** — go after the first study assignments ourselves, or team up with an established firm to earn a first credit.
