## 1. The Mandate — SB 1953 / Alquist Hospital Facilities Seismic Safety Act

**Statute:** California SB 1953 (1994), codified at Health & Safety Code §§130000–130070, known as the Alfred E. Alquist Hospital Facilities Seismic Safety Act. Triggered by the 1994 Northridge earthquake, which red-tagged 24% and yellow-tagged 33% of pre-act hospital buildings. [Confirmed — HCAI Program Overview: https://hcai.ca.gov/facilities/building-safety/seismic-compliance-and-safety/program-overview/]

**Regulator:** HCAI (Department of Health Care Access and Information), successor to OSHPD. Specifically the Seismic Compliance Unit (SCU). Documents are still commonly filed under the "OSHPD" brand. [Confirmed — https://hcai.ca.gov/facilities/building-safety/seismic-compliance-and-safety/hospital-seismic-safety/]

**Scope:** All general acute-care (GAC) hospital buildings in California.

## 2. SPC / NPC Rating System

### Structural Performance Category (SPC)
| Rating | Meaning | Compliance Status |
|--------|---------|------------------|
| SPC-1 | Significant risk of collapse (>1.20% collapse probability) | **PAST DEADLINE: Jan 1, 2020** (extensions possible) |
| SPC-2 | No significant collapse risk but may not be repairable/functional post-quake (≤1.20%) | **HARD DEADLINE: Jan 1, 2030** |
| SPC-3 | Some damage but life-safe post-quake | Compliant through 2030+ |
| SPC-4 | Damage may inhibit services but structure intact | Compliant through 2030+ |
| SPC-4D | Designation for damage-tolerant post-2019 code path | Compliant through 2030+ |
| SPC-5 | Fully operational post-strong ground motion | Compliant through 2030+ |

### Non-Structural Performance Category (NPC)
| Rating | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| NPC-1 to NPC-3 | Progressive anchorage/bracing of comm/power/medical-gas/fire systems; deadlines 2002–2008 (past) |
| NPC-4 | All arch/MEP systems meet seismic standards |
| NPC-5 | Includes 72-hour emergency water, fuel, backup power — **required by Jan 1, 2030** |

**2030 compliance requires ALL GAC buildings on a campus to be: SPC 3, 4, 4D, or 5 AND NPC 5.** A single non-compliant building on a campus means the entire hospital is non-compliant for that building's services. [Confirmed — https://hcai.ca.gov/facilities/building-safety/seismic-compliance-and-safety/seismic-performance-ratings/]

## 3. Deadline Status — How Many Buildings Are Non-Compliant?

**Key numbers (as of 2023–2026):**
- **674 buildings** across **251 licensed hospitals** do not meet the 2030 functionality standard. [Confirmed — California Healthline: https://californiahealthline.org/news/article/california-hospitals-earthquake-retrofit-deadline-extension/]
- **More than half of California's ~410–414 hospitals** have at least one building that likely cannot operate after a major earthquake. [Confirmed — same source]
- **Only 45% of hospitals** statewide are currently on track to meet the 2030 deadline (HCAI figure). [Confirmed — Swinerton/HCAI cited at https://swinerton.com/california-hospitals-face-2030-deadline-seismic-safety-law-driving-major-upgrades/]
- Approximately **two-thirds of hospitals** require major seismic upgrades. [Confirmed — multiple sources including CalMatters: https://calmatters.org/health/2023/01/seismic-safety-hospitals-california/]

**Cost estimates:**
- RAND Corporation (2019, updated): **$34 billion** (retrofit-only) to **$143 billion** (full replacement), equivalent to **$176 billion in 2025 dollars / $199 billion in 2030 dollars**. [Confirmed — https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RR3059.html]
- Roughly **$92 million average per building** at the high end. [Probable — derived from California Healthline data]
- **One-third of California hospitals** are already in some form of financial distress, making compliance especially challenging. [Confirmed — CalMatters]

**Is the non-compliant building list PUBLIC?**  
Yes. HCAI maintains several public data resources:
- **Facility Detail Dashboard** (by individual facility, updated biweekly): posted annual Hospital Services Reports for every non-compliant hospital. [Confirmed — https://hcai.ca.gov/facilities/building-safety/seismic-compliance-and-safety/hospital-seismic-safety/annual-status-reporting/]
- **CHHS Open Data Portal** — "Seismic Compliance Data by County for General Acute Care Hospitals" CSV, updated June 15, 2026, provides SPC/NPC ratings by county. [Confirmed — https://data.chhs.ca.gov/dataset/seismic-compliance-data-by-county-for-general-acute-care-hospitals]
- **"Current SPC/NPC Ratings of Acute Care Hospital Buildings"** downloadable report from HCAI. [Confirmed — https://hcai.ca.gov/facilities/building-safety/seismic-compliance-and-safety/]
- **eService Portal (eSP):** hospitals file annual reports; approved reports are posted publicly. [Confirmed — https://hcai.ca.gov/facilities/building-safety/seismic-compliance-and-safety/hospital-seismic-safety/annual-status-reporting/]

**2026 Compliance Plan deadline:**  
All non-2030-compliant GAC hospitals were required to submit seismic compliance plans to HCAI by **January 1, 2026** (PIN 80). Plans must detail retrofit/replacement solutions with milestone timelines. HCAI reviews within 120 days and may approve, comment, or deny. [Confirmed — https://hcai.ca.gov/document/pin-80-seismic-compliance-plan-and-ab-869-delays-beyond-2030-deadline/]

**AB 869 Extension provisions:**  
Governor Newsom signed legislation allowing hospitals in specific categories to apply for **up to 3-year extensions** beyond 2030:
- 37 critical access hospitals
- 56 small hospitals (<50 beds)
- 59 rural hospitals
- 32 district hospitals (health care district-funded)

These hospitals must submit plans with milestones to qualify for extensions. This does NOT eliminate the work — it extends the timeline for the most financially distressed facilities, creating a pipeline that runs past 2030. [Confirmed — California Healthline]

## 5. What "OSHPD/HCAI Experience" Means in Practice — The Entry Barrier

### Regulatory complexity is the real moat:

**HCAI occupancy classification:** Hospital buildings are HCAI 1 or 1R (most acute care) — the most stringent classification in the California code system, above DSA (Division of the State Architect for schools). Everything goes through HCAI plan check: structural drawings, specifications, material submittals, change orders, RFIs, and field inspections. [Confirmed — https://houseoe.com/hcai-classification-alignment-oshpd-11r-2-3-4-5/]

**SE license required:** California SE (Structural Engineer) license — not merely PE — is required to sign and stamp drawings for public schools and hospitals. John Markulin holds CA SE. [Confirmed — Markulin bio: https://www.korstructural.com/team_member/john-a-markulin/; confirmed SE license requirement: BPELSG]

**ASCE 7 Importance Factor:** Hospitals are Occupancy Category IV / Importance Factor Ip = 1.5 for all nonstructural components — the highest tier. Retrofit projects use ASCE 41 (seismic evaluation and retrofit of existing buildings) as the primary standard; ASCE 41-13 Chapter 17 is explicitly referenced in HCAI guidance. [Confirmed — HCAI seismic FAQs]

**Plan check timeline:** HCAI plan reviews are notoriously long — 6–18+ months for major submittals. Firms that know the reviewers, documentation formats, and pre-submittal coordination process have a material time advantage on every project. This process knowledge is accumulated over years and is not transferable by reading the code. [Confirmed — Degenkolb cites 18-month fast-track as a selling point for Kaiser Oakland]

**Occupied facility constraints:** All retrofits occur in operating acute-care facilities. ICRA (Infection Control Risk Assessment) and ISLM (Interim Life Safety Measures) protocols govern construction zones. SE firms must coordinate structural work around ICU operations, operating rooms, and patient care workflows. This is not standard commercial retrofit practice. [Confirmed — Swinerton article]

**SPC upgrade path complexity:** Moving a building from SPC-2 to SPC-3+ often requires nonlinear PBSD (Performance-Based Seismic Design) analysis under ASCE 41 to demonstrate compliance — the same technical domain as PBSD/ASCE-41 work KOR does. However, the HCAI review process for this work has specific submission requirements and HCAI plan checker expertise is calibrated to firms they know. [Confirmed — HCAI seismic performance ratings; Probable that KOR's PBSD skillset is technically transferable]

**Track record gating:** Hospital owners (especially major systems like Kaiser, Dignity, Sutter) strongly favor firms with documented HCAI project completions. Procurement for large hospital retrofits typically uses qualifications-based selection (QBS), meaning prior OSHPD/HCAI project references are the primary screening criterion. A firm without prior HCAI completions is effectively disqualified from short-listing on major health system work. [Probable — consistent with all published procurement behavior; no explicit HCAI rule found but the QBS pattern is industry-standard]

## 6. Honest Entry Barrier Assessment for KOR

### Hard barriers:

1. **Zero documented HCAI project history.** Markulin's CA SE license is necessary but not sufficient. The SE biography cites "extended health care facilities" generically — no named HCAI 1 projects. [Confirmed — KOR website]

2. **The incumbents have compounding advantages.** Degenkolb's 9-year Swinerton partnership represents a channel lock — not just a relationship. R+C's "decades-long" HCAI relationship is institutional, not personal. These moats are real.

3. **The market is fully contested at the top.** Every major CA health system (Kaiser, Dignity, Sutter, UC, Cedars-Sinai) has SE relationships from prior HCAI projects. Breaking in at the health-system level requires displacing a trusted incumbent — nearly impossible near-term.

4. **Project scale.** Major hospital towers are $50M–$500M+ construction, with SE fees typically in the $1M–$10M range for prime work. These engagements go to firms with multi-decade portfolios of at-scale HCAI work.

5. **Contractor channel is locked.** Swinerton + Degenkolb/Buehler is a vertically integrated delivery partnership. Other GC/CM firms have their own SE relationships. An SE without a GC champion has no inbound channel.

### Soft barriers (potential entry vectors):

1. **Small/rural/district hospitals (the AB 869 cohort).** The 184 hospitals eligible for extensions (critical access + small + rural + district) are disproportionately financially distressed, under-resourced, and NOT served by the major SE incumbents who focus on large health systems. A 9-bed rural hospital in Humboldt County does not have a Degenkolb relationship. These hospitals need low-cost SE partners to help them evaluate retrofit vs. replacement, develop compliance plan milestones, and eventually execute simpler retrofit scopes. [Confirmed — California Healthline, CalMatters]

2. **Subconsultant / peer reviewer role.** HCAI requires an **independent California licensed structural engineer** to review and accept project-specific seismic certification test results. This is a door KOR could potentially walk through: providing independent peer review or third-party seismic assessments as a sub to an HCAI-experienced A/E prime. The structural work is real (ASCE 41/PBSD) and the CA SE license is required. The barrier is finding the prime willing to bring in an unknown sub — which means relationship building first. [Confirmed — HCAI OSP requirements: https://hcai.ca.gov/facilities/building-safety/preapproval-programs/osp/]

3. **PBSD/ASCE-41 analytical depth.** The specific technical work at the heart of SPC-2 → SPC-3/4 upgrades is nonlinear PBSD under ASCE 41 — KOR's core technical identity. Firms that can perform credible NLRHA (nonlinear response history analysis) or nonlinear static (pushover) analyses for HCAI compliance are valuable. The gap is not technical — it's the HCAI process layer on top. [Probable — KOR's PBSD/ASCE-41 identity is documented; HCAI's ASCE 41 requirement is confirmed]

4. **Seismic evaluation / SPC rating assessment (pre-design phase).** Every non-compliant hospital must first understand its current SPC rating and what it would take to reach SPC-3+. This preliminary assessment work (ASCE 41 Tier 1/2/3 screening) is a legitimate SE scope that precedes full project engagement. Smaller hospitals doing this for the first time may not have an incumbent SE. This is an evaluation-first engagement model KOR could pursue. [Probable]

5. **Post-2030 pipeline.** The AB 869 extension cohort creates a pipeline of retrofit/replacement work running to ~2033. After 2030, compliance plans become construction documents. The capacity crunch ("all vying for the same contractor workforce") that affects construction will likely also affect SE capacity — creating potential demand for firms willing to work in this space. [Probable — CalMatters capacity crunch quote]

## 7. Realistic KOR Angle — Summary Assessment

**Verdict: Not viable as a prime SE on large hospital systems near-term. Potentially viable in a narrow subconsultant / small-hospital / evaluation-phase lane, with a 2–3 year runway required.**

### What is NOT realistic:
- Competing for major health system (Kaiser, Dignity, Sutter, UCSF) hospital retrofit prime SE roles. These are locked by Degenkolb, R+C, Saiful Bouquet, and Miyamoto. The relationship and HCAI process depth required would take 5+ years to build from zero. [Confirmed incumbency from multiple sources]
- Winning anything through a general RFP without HCAI project references. QBS is the standard procurement mode; no project references = no short-list. [Probable]

### What might be realistic (2–4 year horizon):

**Option A — Small/Rural Hospital Direct.** Target the ~184 hospitals eligible for AB 869 extensions in underserved regions (Central Valley, Northern CA, Inland Empire, rural SoCal). These facilities have compliance plan deadlines and desperately need affordable SE firms. Entry offer: ASCE 41 Tier 1/2 seismic evaluation + SPC assessment + compliance plan support. No HCAI track record is required for the evaluation phase; track record for permit/construction work would be built over time. This is the most realistic near-term KOR entry. Cost: relatively small projects ($50K–$300K evaluation phase), but genuine foot-in-door.

**Option B — Subconsultant to HCAI-experienced A/E.** Identify a mid-tier architecture or MEP firm active in HCAI healthcare work that lacks in-house structural seismic depth. Offer to provide PBSD/ASCE-41 structural analysis as a sub. This requires relationship development at the A/E level, not the hospital level. A firm like a regional healthcare architecture practice (not Perkins+Will / HKS, which have their own SE relationships, but smaller regional healthcare architects) could be the channel. This is plausible but requires deliberate business development targeting.

**Option C — Independent Peer Review.** HCAI requires an independent CA SE to review seismic certification test data for OSP submittals. This is a boutique service, not a volume play, but it creates HCAI process familiarity and relationships. Not a primary strategy but a legitimacy-builder.

**What KOR needs before pursuing any of these:**
1. Explicit HCAI project listing on at least one completed project — even a small interior renovation at an HCAI-classified facility.
2. Deliberate outreach to small rural hospital associations (California Hospital Association has a small/rural division; CRIHB — California Rural Indian Health Board is another channel).
3. A strategic conversation with one HCAI-experienced prime (Miyamoto or a regional firm, not Degenkolb) about subconsultant opportunities.
