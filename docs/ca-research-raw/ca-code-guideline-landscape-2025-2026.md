## 1. 2025 California Building Code (CBC) — Adoption, ASCE 7-22, Seismic Changes

### Adoption & Effective Date

The 2025 California Building Standards Code (Title 24) was published July 1, 2025 and becomes **mandatory January 1, 2026** for all new permit applications. [Confirmed]  
Source: [ISE Engineers — 2026 CBC Changes](https://iseengineers.com/what-californias-2026-building-code-changes-mean-for-structural-engineering/)

Projects permitted before January 1, 2026 may remain under the 2022 cycle. Local jurisdictions may adopt early. The 2025 CBC is based on the **2024 IBC** (previous cycle was 2021 IBC), representing a full IBC-cycle jump. [Confirmed]  
Source: [MeltPlan — 2025 CBC Major Changes](https://www.meltplan.com/blogs/how-the-2025-california-building-code-cbc-overhauls-everything-from-authority-to-engineering)

### ASCE 7-22 Adoption — Replacing ASCE 7-16

The single largest structural change: ASCE 7-22 replaces ASCE 7-16 as the governing load standard. [Confirmed]  
Source: [ISE Engineers](https://iseengineers.com/what-californias-2026-building-code-changes-mean-for-structural-engineering/); [DGS Chapter 16](https://www.dgs.ca.gov/BSC/Resources/2025-Title-24-California-Code-Changes/2025-Part-2-Chapters-16-and-16A-Structural-Design)

Key seismic changes in ASCE 7-22 as adopted by 2025 CBC:

**Multi-Period Response Spectra (MPRS)**
- ASCE 7-22 introduces a 22-point Multi-Period Design Response Spectrum alongside the legacy Two-Period spectrum [Confirmed]
- 2025 CBC repeals the prior NEHRP supplement approach; MPRS is now directly embedded in ASCE 7-22 [Confirmed]
- Only the Multi-Period spectrum may be used for modal/RHA analysis; either may be used for ELF [Confirmed]
- Source: [SK Ghosh — Two-Period vs Multi-Period](https://www.skghoshassociates.com/blog/is-continued-use-of-the-two-period-design-spectrum-in-the-equivalent-lateral-force-procedure-of-seismic-design-permitted-by-asce-7-22/)

**New Site Classification System (6 → 9 classes)**
- ASCE 7-16 had Site Classes A–F (6 classes); ASCE 7-22 adds BC, CD, and DE, giving **nine site classes** [Confirmed]
- Classification is now based solely on shear-wave velocity Vs30; SPT blow counts and undrained shear strength no longer suffice as a primary basis [Confirmed]
- Default site class changed from "D (assumed)" to "Assumed C, CD, or D" [Confirmed]
- Fa and Fv coefficient tables deleted from CBC in favour of ASCE 7-22 site-specific provisions; old OSHPD table amendments rendered obsolete [Confirmed]
- Source: [DGS 2025 Chapter 16](https://www.dgs.ca.gov/BSC/Resources/2025-Title-24-California-Code-Changes/2025-Part-2-Chapters-16-and-16A-Structural-Design); [SEAU ASCE 7-22 Significant Changes](https://seau.org/images/meeting/022024/seau_2024_asce_7_22_updates.pdf)

**Seismic Hazard Map Updates**
- MCER hazard maps revised; most revisions concentrated in Western US [Probable — multiple indirect citations]
- Sa (spectral acceleration) multiplier for height/area increases now capped at **2× for Groups A, E, H, I, L, R** and **3× for most others** (was 4×); potentially restricts height of some R-occupancy buildings [Confirmed]
- Source: [ISE Engineers](https://iseengineers.com/what-californias-2026-building-code-changes-mean-for-structural-engineering/)

**Vertical Irregularity Restrictions**
- New CA amendment: **vertical irregularities Types 1b, 4a, and 4b prohibited for SDC D and higher** [Confirmed]
- Significant impact on podium and mixed-use designs common in CA multifamily [Probable — engineering inference]
- Source: [DGS Chapter 16 Structural Design](https://www.dgs.ca.gov/BSC/Resources/2025-Title-24-California-Code-Changes/2025-Part-2-Chapters-16-and-16A-Structural-Design)

**Other Structural Provisions**
- Cold-formed steel framing limited to ≤3 stories in SDC D0/D1/D2; must comply with AISI S230 [Confirmed]
- Wood-frame buildings limited to 3 stories above grade (CRC); SDC D2 over 2 stories requires full seismic/wind engineering [Confirmed]
- Light-frame wood overstrength exemption clarified: applies only when structure is braced entirely by wood including diaphragms and shear walls [Confirmed]
- Type IIIB and VB no longer permitted for any height in I-2 occupancies [Confirmed]
- CLT now formally recognized as a structural system within ASCE 7-22 (mass timber seismic systems codified) [Confirmed]
- Source: [ISE Engineers](https://iseengineers.com/what-californias-2026-building-code-changes-mean-for-structural-engineering/); [CBC 2025 search results](https://www.meltplan.com/blogs/how-the-2025-california-building-code-cbc-overhauls-everything-from-authority-to-engineering)

### KOR Relevance
The MPRS and new 9-class site system require updated geotechnical coordination and software workflows. Firms still running ASCE 7-16 calcs will produce non-compliant designs after Jan 1, 2026. KOR's seismic fluency is an advantage in onboarding CA teams faster than generalist newcomers. The vertical irregularity prohibition strengthens KOR's case for pure shear-wall systems on podium buildings, reducing transfer-element complexity.

## 2. Functional Recovery — AB 1329 and Statewide Push

### What is Functional Recovery?

"Functional recovery" means designing buildings so their structural and nonstructural systems can maintain or be restored to support pre-earthquake use within a specified time frame — going beyond life-safety (don't collapse) to operational resilience (reoccupied quickly). [Confirmed]  
Source: [AB 1329 bill text](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202120220AB1329)

### Legislative History: AB 1329

- **AB 1857 / AB 2681 (2018):** First wave of functional recovery bills — vetoed by Governor Brown in September 2018. [Confirmed per Structuremag article]
- **AB 1329 (2021–22, Nazarian):** Reinstated the concept; required CBSC and HCD to develop mandatory building standards for new construction to a functional recovery standard for earthquake loads, during the **2024 triennial cycle**. Standards to become effective 180 days after Title 24 publication. [Confirmed from bill text]
- SEAOC publicly supported AB 1329 and testified in its favor. [Confirmed]  
  Source: [SEAOC — Functional Recovery Bill Moves Ahead](https://www.seaoc.org/news/563515/SEAOC-Functional-Recovery-Bill-Moves-Ahead.htm)
- **Final status of AB 1329:** The bill passed the Assembly (54–19) and Senate in 2021. Whether it was signed by Governor Newsom or vetoed could not be independently confirmed from available sources (multiple searches returned no chaptering confirmation). [Unverified — legislative databases returned conflicting or incomplete records; recommend checking leginfo.legislature.ca.gov directly]

### Current Status (2025–26)

- The **2025 CBC (effective Jan 1, 2026)** does not include a functional recovery standard; the 2024 code cycle did not deliver the AB 1329 mandate on schedule. [Probable — no functional recovery section found in 2025 Title 24 summary materials]
- A **2025 legislative hearing on seismic resilience and functional recovery** was held October 15, 2025 in Sacramento, with UC San Diego researchers and former Seismic Safety Commission director presenting findings intended to inform the **next CBC update cycle**. [Confirmed]  
  Source: [Sheehy Strategy Group — Oct 2025 hearing](https://www.sheehystrategygroup.com/news/2025/10/10/seismic-resilience-and-functional-recovery-proposition)
- NIST has published a technical framework for mapping functional recovery performance objectives to prescriptive seismic design provisions. [Confirmed]  
  Source: [NIST SP 1340](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.1340.pdf)
- Direction of travel is clear: functional recovery will enter the next code cycle (2028 CBC or possibly interim amendment), with debate now on mandatory vs. above-code-voluntary implementation. [Probable]

### What It Would Require

When enacted, functional recovery standards would likely establish:
- Occupancy-class-specific maximum downtime targets (e.g., "hospital restorable within 72 hours", "office within 30 days")
- Prescriptive structural system requirements or performance-based design pathway with quantified drift/damage thresholds
- Nonstructural (MEP, facades, elevators) design to the same recovery benchmark — a major cost driver
- Local jurisdictions can adopt more stringent standards
- CBSC authority initially limited to nonresidential; HCD handles residential occupancies [Confirmed from bill text]

### KOR Opportunity

**Significant differentiation opportunity.** [Probable]  
A firm that can demonstrate performance-based seismic design targeting functional recovery timelines — rather than just code-minimum life-safety — will win institutional owners, REIT developers, and public-sector clients who face post-earthquake liability and occupancy disruption risk. KOR's seismic DNA (Bryson heritage, CA P.Eng via Markulin) positions the firm to become a functional recovery resource for CA architects. Early positioning now (white papers, LinkedIn presence on functional recovery, outreach to SEAOC events) precedes any code mandate.

## 3. Mass Timber — Type IV-A/B/C, CBC Adoption, Pipeline

### Code Framework

The 2021 IBC introduced three new Type IV construction categories enabling tall mass timber; these were adopted into California's 2022 CBC (effective January 2023). The **2025 CBC** aligns with the **2024 IBC**, which preserves the three types with one major upgrade for Type IV-B. [Confirmed]  
Source: [Woodworking Network — CA Allows MT 18 Stories](https://www.woodworkingnetwork.com/news/woodworking-industry-news/california-codes-now-allow-mass-timber-buildings-18-stories); [WoodWorks IBC Status](https://www.woodworks.org/resources/status-of-building-code-allowances-for-tall-mass-timber-in-the-ibc/)

| Type | Max Height | Exposed Mass Timber (2021 IBC) | Exposed (2024 IBC / 2025 CBC) |
|---|---|---|---|
| IV-A | 18 stories | ~0% (fully protected) | ~0% |
| IV-B | 12 stories | 20% ceiling | **100% ceiling/beams** |
| IV-C | 9 stories | ~40% walls, 20% ceiling | ~40% walls |

The jump from 20% to 100% ceiling exposure in Type IV-B under the 2025 CBC is architecturally transformative — it makes mass timber economically viable on projects where biophilic aesthetic is the sales driver (multifamily amenity floors, office, hospitality). [Confirmed]  
Source: [ISE Engineers 2026 CBC](https://iseengineers.com/what-californias-2026-building-code-changes-mean-for-structural-engineering/); [WoodWorks IBC Status](https://www.woodworks.org/resources/status-of-building-code-allowances-for-tall-mass-timber-in-the-ibc/)

ASCE 7-22 in the 2025 CBC also **formally recognizes CLT as a structural system** with codified seismic design provisions — previously designed via alternative means. [Confirmed]

### California Project Pipeline

California is among the **top 7 states** for mass timber construction (others: TX, WA, MA, GA, CO, NY), all with 50+ projects in design or construction as of early 2026. [Confirmed]  
Source: [Mass Timber Performance Index 2025 — IMTC](https://masstimberconference.com/report/content/mass-timber-performance-index-2025/)

Nationally, approximately **2,746 mass timber projects** (multifamily, commercial, institutional) were in progress or completed as of March 2026, with ~1,168 in the design phase. [Confirmed]  
Source: [WoodWorks mapping tool](https://www.woodworks.org/resources/mapping-mass-timber/)

**California landmark projects:**
- **1510 Webster St., Oakland** — 19-story Type IV-A tower, first MT high-rise in the US; first beamless mass plywood panel high-rise in the world. [Confirmed]
- **Habitat, Baldwin Hills (LA)** — 454,000 sq ft mixed-use development, 6-story 250,000 sq ft office + mass timber. Lendlease/Aware Super. [Confirmed]
- **843 N. Spring St., Chinatown LA** — 145,000 sq ft hybrid CLT office/retail; opened August 2024. [Confirmed]
- **42XX, Marina del Rey** — 150,000+ sq ft creative office campus; opened October 2024. [Confirmed]
- **SuperBungalows, Silver Lake** — CLT multifamily complexes (9- and 15-unit). [Confirmed]
- Source: [LA Business Journal — Mass Timber on the Rise](https://labusinessjournal.com/special-reports/special-report-mass-timber-on-the-rise/)

**California manufacturing scaling:**
- **Mosaic Timber** — California's first CLT manufacturing operation, targeting production **mid-2026**. [Confirmed]
- **FABRIC + WRNS Studio** — Launching CA's first large-scale mass timber production facility (announced Nov 2025). [Confirmed]
- A DLT press producing up to 1,000 sq ft/day was commissioned 2025. [Confirmed]
- Source: [Archpaper — FABRIC/WRNS CA Facility](https://www.archpaper.com/2025/11/fabric-wrns-california-mass-timber-facility/); [Passive House Accelerator — CA MT Production](https://passivehouseaccelerator.com/articles/new-mass-timber-production-in-california)

**Santa Monica Mass Timber Accelerator** — city program offering pre-approval pathways for MT projects; applications Oct 2025–Feb 2026, signaling municipal enthusiasm. [Confirmed]  
Source: [City of Santa Monica](https://www.santamonica.gov/santa-monica-mass-timber-accelerator)

**Dominant project types:** Assembly, business, educational, and multifamily accounted for over 80% of new mass timber construction in 2024. [Confirmed]  
Source: [IMTC Performance Index 2025](https://masstimberconference.com/report/content/mass-timber-performance-index-2025/)

### Cost Context

Mass timber front-end costs run ~26% higher than concrete alternatives currently; expected to decline as CA manufacturing capacity (Mosaic, FABRIC) comes online and supply chains mature. [Confirmed]  
Source: [LA Business Journal](https://labusinessjournal.com/special-reports/special-report-mass-timber-on-the-rise/)

WoodWorks provides free structural/technical support for multifamily, institutional, and commercial mass timber projects via the WoodWorks Innovation Network. [Confirmed]  
Source: [WoodWorks WIN](https://www.woodworksinnovationnetwork.org/projects/)

### KOR Opportunity

**This is KOR's clearest CA growth lane.** The 2025 CBC's 100%-exposure IV-B provision arrives just as CA's local CLT manufacturing comes online (Mosaic mid-2026), compressing the cost premium. KOR's woodframe/seismic competency translates directly to MT lateral design. The 9- and 12-story sweet spots (IV-C and IV-B) align with the multifamily pipeline density profile from SB 79 (65–95 ft buildings near transit). KOR should seek to join mass timber project teams in SoCal and the Bay Area now, before the market becomes crowded by established MT-specialist firms from PNW.

## 4. Housing and Density Laws Driving the Multifamily Pipeline

### Overview

A sustained legislative program since 2019 has systematically dismantled local exclusionary zoning. The 2025–26 package accelerates the pipeline particularly for transit-adjacent and infill multifamily — the structural typologies KOR competes in.

### SB 9 (HOME Act, 2021) + SB 450 (2024)

- SB 9 requires ministerial (no discretionary review, no CEQA) approval of duplexes and/or lot splits on single-family parcels. [Confirmed]  
- **SB 450 (2024, effective 2025):** Tightens enforcement — cities must respond within 60 days; denials limited to public health/safety grounds only; no non-uniform standards. [Confirmed]  
- Structural impact: small-scale, but drives demand for R-3/R-2 SE services on ADU-adjacent duplexes. [Probable]  
- Source: [HCD SB 9 Fact Sheet](https://www.hcd.ca.gov/sites/default/files/docs/planning-and-community/sb-9-fact-sheet.pdf)

### SB 10 (2021)

- Allows local governments to upzone parcels near transit or in job-rich areas to allow up to 10 units by ordinance, without environmental review. Purely optional for cities — no state mandate. [Confirmed]  
- Source: [All World Constructions — SB-9/SB-10 Shadowing Heights](https://allworldconstructions.com/build-an-adu/f/sb-9-sb-10-shadowing-heights-new-era-of-urban-density)

### AB 2097 (2022) + AB 2553 (2024)

- AB 2097: Prohibits parking minimums within ½ mile of a major transit stop. [Confirmed]  
- **AB 2553 (effective 2025):** Expands the definition of "major transit stop" to include bus routes at 20-minute headways (was 15 minutes), substantially widening the no-parking zone across LA, San Diego, Sacramento, and Bay Area. [Confirmed]  
- Structural impact: Removal of podium parking drives shift from PT-concrete podium to simpler wood-frame podium configurations or ground-up all-wood designs; reduces structural dead load and simplifies lateral systems. [Probable]  
- Source: [Terner Center — 2025 CA Housing Laws](https://ternercenter.berkeley.edu/blog/california-housing-laws-that-go-into-effect-in-2025/)

### AB 2011 (2022) + AB 2243 (2024)

- AB 2011: Ministerial/CEQA-exempt approval for multifamily projects on commercially zoned land, subject to prevailing wages and affordable housing targets. [Confirmed]  
- **AB 2243 (2024):** Extends to lower-density commercial zones, broader geographic reach. [Confirmed]  
- Structural impact: Unlocks commercially zoned infill parcels as multifamily sites — often irregular lots with soil challenges, an SE-intensive typology. [Probable]  
- Source: [Holland & Knight — 2025 CA Housing Laws](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2024/11/californias-2025-housing-laws-what-you-need-to-know)

### ADU Laws — SB 1211 + SB 1077 + AB 2533 (effective 2025)

- **SB 1211:** Increases allowable detached ADUs on multifamily properties from 2 to **up to 8** (capped at number of primary units); an 8-plex can now add 8 ADU units. [Confirmed]  
- **SB 1077:** Coastal Commission guidance for ADU elements in Local Coastal Plans — unlocks coastal infill. [Confirmed]  
- **AB 2533:** ADU Amnesty Program improvements for unpermitted pre-2020 units. [Confirmed]  
- Structural impact: Detached ADU clusters on apartment lots create SE demand for foundation, lateral, and structural assessments of add-on units. [Probable]  
- Estimated pipeline: 1.5 million additional units potentially enabled statewide. [Unverified — cited by one advocacy source]  
- Source: [Terner Center 2025](https://ternercenter.berkeley.edu/blog/california-housing-laws-that-go-into-effect-in-2025/)

### SB 79 (Wiener, signed October 2025, effective July 1, 2026)

**This is the most structurally significant housing law for KOR.** [Confirmed]  
Source: [Holland & Knight — SB 79 Signed](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/10/california-gov-gavin-newsom-signs-sb-79); [Buchalter — SB 79 TOD](https://www.buchalter.com/insights/high-density-transit-zones-california-authorizes-transit-oriented-development-with-senate-bill-79/)

- Applies in **8 CA counties** (LA, Orange, San Diego, Santa Clara, San Mateo, San Francisco, Alameda, Sacramento) — KOR's four target metros are all covered.
- Tier 1 stops (heavy/commuter rail, 72+ trains/day): up to 75 ft / 120 du/ac within ¼ mile; 65 ft / 100 du/ac at ¼–½ mile.
- Tier 2 stops (light rail / 48–71 trains/day / qualifying BRT): up to 65 ft / 100 du/ac within ¼ mile; 55 ft / 80 du/ac at ¼–½ mile.
- Adjacent-to-stop bonus: +20 ft height, +40 du/ac, +1.0 FAR on top of base tier.
- Projects >85 ft require prevailing wage + skilled/trained workforce — this is the **wood-to-steel structural transition point**.

**KOR's sweet spot under SB 79:** Projects targeting 55–75 ft (5–7 stories) stay in Type V-A or hybrid woodframe territory, maximizing KOR's lateral-design strength and keeping SE fees competitive with local firms. The higher-density projects pushing to 85–95 ft (7–8 stories) with CFS or light-gauge steel create an opening for KOR's CA-licensed SE to certify specialty lateral systems.

### Density Bonus Law (Existing + Updates)

California's density bonus law allows additional units, height, and reduced parking beyond base zoning in exchange for affordable units. Layered on SB 79, density-bonus projects can substantially exceed 75–95 ft base limits, potentially entering high-rise structural typologies. [Confirmed]  
Source: [ArchiDev — CA Density Bonus](https://archidevstudio.com/california-density-bonus-state-housing-laws)

## 5. Other 2025–26 CA Structural/Seismic Regulations Worth Knowing

### 5A. Mandatory Soft-Story Retrofit Cascade

Multiple cities have new or tightened mandatory retrofit ordinances for soft, weak, or open-front (SWOF) buildings, primarily wood-frame multi-unit residential:

- **LA:** Long-running program; Phase 3 compliance deadlines continue rolling through 2025–26. [Confirmed]  
  Source: [LADBS Soft-Story Program](https://dbs.lacity.gov/services/specialized-services/mandatory-retrofit-programs/soft-story-retrofit-program)
- **San Jose:** New mandatory ordinance effective April 2025; screening phase deadline October 1, 2027; design/permit/construction deadlines 2031–2033. [Confirmed]  
  Source: [City of San Jose Soft-Story](https://www.sanjoseca.gov/your-government/departments-offices/planning-building-code-enforcement/building-division/soft-story-retrofit)
- **Burbank:** Ordinance effective January 2025; construction deadline January 2030. [Confirmed]  
  Source: [City of Burbank Soft-Story](https://www.burbankca.gov/web/community-development/soft-story)
- **San Francisco, Oakland, Berkeley:** Established mandatory programs continuing. [Confirmed]  
  Source: [City of Oakland Mandatory Soft-Story](https://www.oaklandca.gov/Business/For-Landlords/Mandatory-Soft-Story-Retrofit-Program)

KOR relevance: Retrofit work on SWOF buildings (typically 2–4 story wood-frame multifamily with tuck-under parking) is directly within KOR's existing woodframe and seismic skill set. This is a volume, not prestige, play — good for market entry and CA P.Eng utilization under Markulin.

### 5B. Hospital Seismic — SB 1953 / 2030 Deadline

California hospitals must meet SPC-4D (collapse-prevention for all acute care beds) by **January 1, 2030** under the Alfred E. Alquist Hospital Facilities Seismic Safety Act (SB 1953). Compliance reports due to HCAI. Facilities that cannot comply face loss of Medi-Cal and Medicare payments. [Confirmed]  
Source: [California Hospital Association — 2030 Seismic Requirements](https://calhospital.org/issues/2030-seismic-requirements/)

KOR relevance: Healthcare seismic retrofit is highly specialized (OSHPD/HCAI oversight, peer review required). Bush Bohlman and major competitors have entrenched relationships. Treat as background context; do not compete directly without established OSHPD track record.

### 5C. Balcony Inspection / SB 326 + SB 721

- **SB 721** (rental properties, 3+ units): Requires visual and invasive inspection of exterior elevated elements (EEEs — balconies, walkways, decks) by January 1, 2025; subsequent inspections every 6 years. [Confirmed]
- **SB 326** (HOA/condo): Same scope; initial deadline January 1, 2025, then every 9 years. [Confirmed]
- Effective January 1, 2026: stricter enforcement, with municipalities authorized to levy fines and lien properties for non-compliance. [Confirmed]  
  Source: [AAGLA — Balcony Deadline Jan 2026](https://members.aagla.org/events/deadline-january-1-2026inspect-your-balconies-and-eees-before-its-too-late/)

KOR relevance: Every vintage multifamily building in SoCal and NorCal now needs periodic SE-certified EEE inspection. High-volume, lower-fee, but strong pipeline entry for building relationships with property managers and HOAs. Immediate opportunity with January 2026 enforcement trigger.

### 5D. CSU Seismic Requirements (September 2025 Revision)

California State University campuses maintain above-CBC seismic standards, revised in **September 2025**. [Confirmed]  
Source: [CSU Seismic Requirements Rev. September 2025](https://www.calstate.edu/csu-system/doing-business-with-the-csu/capital-planning-design-construction/Documents/CSU_Seismic_Requirements.pdf)

KOR relevance: CSU work requires institutional relationships and OSHPD-adjacent peer review familiarity. Background reference only; not a near-term entry point.

### 5E. Post-LA Wildfire Rebuilding (October 2025 Legislation)

Governor Newsom signed legislation in October 2025 to accelerate LA rebuilding after wildfires, including streamlining structural review processes. [Confirmed]  
Source: [Governor CA — LA Rebuilding](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2025/10/10/governor-newsom-signs-bipartisan-legislation-to-boost-ongoing-los-angeles-rebuilding-efforts-strengthen-future-disaster-response-and-recovery/)

KOR relevance: LA wildfire rebuild is a surge-demand event. Primarily wood-frame residential. Firms already licensed in CA (Markulin) can capture volume SE work if local partnerships are in place. Worth monitoring for 2026 activation.
