## Executive Summary

KOR's structural sweet spot — Type III/V woodframe-over-podium multifamily and mass timber (Type IV) — maps onto two distinct CA opportunity tracks with very different timelines and SE competition levels.

- **Woodframe/podium (near-term, high-volume):** The top CA developers (Greystar, Holland, Sares-Regis, Wermers, MBK/Trumark, City Ventures, Western National, Mill Creek) use an incumbent SE ecosystem anchored by Saiful Bouquet (SoCal), KPFF Greater LA, Englekirk/WSP, CKC, and regional boutiques. KOR's entry path is through the architect relationships — KTGY, AO, TCA Architects, Withee Malcolm, Dahlin — who pick or recommend SEs. The SB 79 TOD pipeline (effective July 1, 2026 in 8 CA counties) accelerates mid-rise woodframe at exactly the 5–8-storey Type III/V band. Comslab-enabled hollow-core podiums are the differentiator to foreground.

- **Mass timber (medium-term, emerging):** The market is real but nascent. Only three CA mass timber residential projects are confirmed as of June 2026: 1510 Webster St (oWOW/DCI, completed), McEvoy Apartments (First Community Housing/Fast+Epp, under construction), and 1523 Harrison St (oWOW/DCI, planned 2025 start). The dominant SE pair for CA mass timber is DCI Engineers (Bay Area, oWOW), Fast+Epp (McEvoy San Jose), and Forell/Elsesser (DLT pilot study + UC Berkeley). Glotman's "first CLT in LA" claim is real but for commercial/office only (843 N Spring, 6344 Fountain) — not residential. The residential Type IV market is genuinely open.

### 1D. KOR Differentiators in Woodframe/Podium

1. **Comslab precast hollow-core podium system** — Differentiates the podium from poured concrete; faster, quieter, lighter. No CA SE competitor has publicized this as a standard offering on woodframe-over-podium. [KOR internal knowledge]
2. **ASCE 7-22 / 2025 CBC fluency** — New 9-class site system + MPRS + vertical irregularity prohibitions (Types 1b, 4a, 4b banned in SDC D+) create complexity. Firms still running ASCE 7-16 workflows will produce non-compliant designs after Jan 1, 2026. KOR's seismic fluency from BC practice directly translates.
3. **SB 79 sweet spot** — Projects at 65–85 ft / 5–7 stories are directly in Type III/V territory and in KOR's practiced range. The 85-ft labor threshold creates a natural "build at 84 feet" incentive. Eight CA counties (LA, Alameda, OC, Sacramento, SD, SF, San Mateo, Santa Clara) are in scope — exactly KOR's four target metros.

## Part 3 — Local CLT Supply: Mosaic Timber + Mad River

### 3A. Mosaic Timber (Crescent Mills, Plumas County)

- California's **first domestic CLT production line**. Equipment arrived November 2025 (12 trucks, 200+ tons from Slovenia). Production start: **mid-2026**. [Confirmed] — Sierra Institute, Plumas Sun, Pacific Beach Builder

- **Production capacity:** 60 cubic meters per shift (no annual sq ft figure publicly stated). [Confirmed] — Pacific Beach Builder
- **Feedstock:** Small-diameter California forest thinnings — reduces wildfire risk, creates 50+ jobs. [Confirmed]
- **Cost impact:** Transportation for imported CLT panels (currently from Canada, Pacific Northwest) "can add 15–25% to material expenses." Mosaic Timber eliminates this premium. No specific price/panel data published. [Confirmed — cost savings implied; specific price unconfirmed]
- **Target markets:** Multi-family residential, single-family, ADUs, commercial, educational, adaptive reuse. [Confirmed]
- **Named customers:** None as of June 2026. No committed developer or architect announced. [Confirmed — no customers named]

### 3B. Mad River Mass Timber (MRMT, Humboldt County)

### 3C. FABRIC Mass Timber (NorCal)

### 3D. KOR Implication — Supply Unlocks Economics

- Pre-Mosaic/MRMT, CA mass timber projects sourced from Canada (Nordic Structures used for SuperBungalows on Marathon) or PNW (Freres MPP for oWOW). Freight premium = 15–25%.
- Mosaic mid-2026 start + MRMT operational 2025 = California-manufactured panels available for projects breaking ground late 2026 and beyond.
- **For KOR:** Being fluent in CA local supply chain (panel specs, lead times, fabricator contact) is a differentiator when pitching mass timber structural services to CA developers who are cost-sensitive. The supply unlock is a genuine threshold event — projects that were marginal at Canadian CLT prices may now pencil at 15–25% lower material cost.

## Part 4 — SB 79 TOD Pipeline: Woodframe Opportunity Engine

SB 79 signed October 10, 2025; **effective July 1, 2026**. [Confirmed] — Holland & Knight, Allen Matkins, CA YIMBY

**Scope:** Eight "urban transit counties" — Alameda, Los Angeles, Orange (2026), Sacramento, San Diego, San Francisco, San Mateo, Santa Clara. By-right residential at transit stops; ministerial CEQA exemption; heights 65–95 ft.

**Height/Labor trigger:**
- Projects **≤ 85 ft** = no prevailing wage mandate under SB 79.
- Projects **> 85 ft** = SB 35/SB 423 prevailing wage + skilled and trained workforce requirements.
- The **85-ft band is the structural sweet spot** — 6–7 stories of Type III wood-frame over 1–2-story concrete podium sits right below this threshold. Developers will design to 84 ft to avoid the labor uplift. [Confirmed — legal analysis from Holland & Knight, Buchalter]

**KOR structural read:**
- Type III-B wood-frame over concrete podium at 5–8 stories, in seismically active zones (LA, SD, East Bay, Sacramento), with the new ASCE 7-22 vertical irregularity prohibitions (Types 1b, 4a, 4b banned in SDC D+) creates demand for SEs who can efficiently solve the podium-wood interface at high seismic demand.
- KOR's podium detailing experience (BC precedent) + Comslab hollow-core option = directly applicable. This is not new technology for KOR — it is new volume for CA.
- The **eight target counties are exactly KOR's four target metros** (LA/OC, San Diego, Bay Area, Sacramento). SB 79 is not a theoretical benefit; it matches KOR's declared footprint.

## Part 5 — Strategic Read

### 5A. Is Mass Timber a Genuine KOR Growth Lane?

**Verdict: Yes — but the lane is early-stage and requires patient positioning.**

The evidence:
1. The code is permissive (Type IV-A to 18 stories; 2025 CBC now at full parity with 2024 IBC). [Confirmed]
2. CA's first domestic CLT supply comes online mid-2026, reducing the 15–25% import freight penalty. [Confirmed]
3. Only three confirmed CA mass timber **residential** projects exist (1510 Webster, 1523 Harrison, McEvoy San Jose). The **SoCal residential mass timber market is completely unoccupied** by a dominant SE. [Confirmed]
4. Glotman's CA mass timber work is **commercial/office only** — their "first CLT in LA" claim (843 N Spring, 6344 Fountain) does not translate to residential. They have not done a CA mass timber residential project. [Confirmed]
5. Fast+Epp is the SE on McEvoy Apartments (San Jose, under construction) — their BC-based seismic mass timber pedigree is strong, and they are a direct competitor to KOR in this lane. [Confirmed]
6. DCI Engineers owns the Bay Area MPP/oWOW niche — 1510 Webster (19 stories, complete) + 1523 Harrison (28 stories, planned). They are the dominant mass timber residential SE in NorCal. [Confirmed]
7. The DLT California pilot (Forell/Elsesser + WRNS + Mad River + Team Projects, Oakland, Apr 2026) suggests Forell/Elsesser is positioning for NorCal mass timber residential — not yet built, but they are entering the lane.
8. **The LA/SoCal mass timber residential market has no SE incumbent.** SuperBungalows (9 units, 3 stories) used CLT panels but the SE was not publicly credited. The Habitat project (Lendlease/Johnson Fain) is mass timber mixed-use but the SE is not confirmed.

**Risk:** Mass timber residential in CA is still pre-volume. Projects are developer-specific (oWOW is essentially a self-contained design-build ecosystem; First Community Housing is affordable housing). The high-volume market-rate Type IV-B/C residential pipeline is not yet confirmed — it is directionally coming with supply unlock + code maturity, but likely 2027–2028 before recurring project flow.

**KOR positioning strategy:** Pursue one SoCal mass timber residential project as a credential project (targeting 6–9-story Type IV-B, where Glotman is absent and Fast+Epp/DCI are Bay Area-focused). Partner with a local CA architect who has not yet done mass timber residential (KTGY, Withee Malcolm, Dahlin all qualify). The Santa Monica Mass Timber Accelerator (applications closed Feb 2026; teams announced Mar 2026) is the highest-leverage BD event for 2026 — if KOR is not in the cohort, track which teams were selected and approach them.

## Appendix A — CA Mass Timber Code Summary (KOR Talking Points)

| Construction Type | Max Height (Residential) | Key Requirement | CA Code Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Type IV-C | 9 stories / ~85 ft | Exposed wood allowed; protection per code | Active since July 2022 CBC; confirmed in 2025 CBC |
| Type IV-B | 12 stories / ~120 ft | More exposed wood allowed (100% under 2025 CBC vs. 20% limit previously) | 2025 CBC expands exposure allowance — effective Jan 1, 2026 |
| Type IV-A | 18 stories / ~180 ft | Most restrictive mass timber type (fully encapsulated); point-supported MPP precedent at 1510 Webster (19 stories with variance) | Active since July 2022 CBC; confirmed in 2025 CBC |

**2025 CBC mass timber expansion:** Type IV-B exposed mass timber ceiling allowance increases from 20% to 100% under certain conditions, with greater unprotected undersides of floors allowed. Effective January 1, 2026. [Confirmed] — ISE Engineers, 2025 CBC

**CLT as seismic system:** ASCE 7-22 (adopted in 2025 CBC) formally recognizes CLT wall systems as a seismic lateral system — first time this was codified in US standard. Previously required alternative means/special approval (as Glotman encountered on 6344 Fountain CLT diaphragm). [Confirmed] — ISE Engineers, Structure Magazine
