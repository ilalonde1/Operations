## Section 1: UC / CSU / Community College Capital Programs

### 1.1 UC System

**Pipeline:** UC has 74 active major capital projects (>$20M) per the 2025 Consolidated Capital Report. Student housing is a top strategic priority. Active examples:
- **UCSD Pepper Canyon East District** — ~6,000 beds on 20 acres, design team Steinberg Hart + Studio Gang + Henning Larsen + BIG + HED, pre-design phase, Fall 2030 target. Construction value likely $1B+.
- **UCLA 901 Levering** — 1,130 beds, 19-story electric building; $8.75M planning budget approved; schematic design underway; site work summer 2026, occupancy Fall 2030.
- **UCSB San Benito Student Housing** — 2,140 beds; construction begins June 2026; Fall 2028 opening. (Source: UCSC Heller Student South also approved, 1,300 beds, Fall 2029.)

**How SEs are selected (UC):** UC capital projects are managed by campus Facilities/Design & Construction offices. Procurement follows California Government Code §4525 (Mini-Brooks Act / QBS). Campuses issue project-specific A/E RFPs, evaluate qualifications first, then negotiate fees. There is no UC-systemwide SE pre-qualification pool. SEs are typically sub-consultants to the lead architect, or separately retained for complex structural scopes. For very large projects, UC issues progressive design-build RFPs (e.g., Pepper Canyon West used progressive D-B with Clark Construction). **There is no formal barrier preventing KOR from pursuing UC work**, but competition is intense (Saiful Bouquet, Thornton Tomasetti, Forell/Elsesser, BKF, etc. are established).

Sources: [UC 2025 Consolidated Capital Report](https://capitalprojectsreport.ucop.edu/pdf/2025-ConsolidatedCapitalReport.pdf) | [UCSD current projects](https://plandesignbuild.ucsd.edu/projects/current.html) | [UCLA housing regents approval](https://adminvc.ucla.edu/news-views/summer-2025/uc-regents-approve-planning-budget-student-housing-project) | [UCSC housing approval](https://news.ucsc.edu/2026/05/more-student-housing-on-the-horizon-as-uc-regents-approve-new-housing-complex/)

### 1.2 CSU System

**Pipeline:** CSU's 2026–2031 Five-Year Capital Outlay Plan covers all 23 campuses. Sacramento State is in master planning for a downtown campus including student housing (early 2026). The system's five-year plan is a multi-billion dollar program; the 2024–2029 plan ran to hundreds of millions per year. Priorities include seismic upgrades, housing, and academic renewal.

**How SEs are selected (CSU):** CSU CPDC runs annual architectural prequalification (Nov–Mar window, eligible for the following July–June cycle) — but structural, MEP, and civil engineers are explicitly **NOT** subject to systemwide prequalification. SEs are hired on a per-project basis by campus, either as A/E of record or sub-consultant, under QBS per Govt Code §4525. CSU also uses Task Order Service Agreements (TOSA) for minor capital and feasibility work. **No pre-qual barrier for structural engineers.** Entry requires campus-by-campus relationship building or teaming with a prequalified architect.

Sources: [CSU Architectural Prequalification page](https://www.calstate.edu/csu-system/doing-business-with-the-csu/capital-planning-design-construction/operations-center/Pages/architectural-prequalification.aspx) | [CSU 2026-2031 Five-Year Plan](https://www.calstate.edu/csu-system/doing-business-with-the-csu/capital-planning-design-construction/Documents/2026-2031%20Five-Year%20Plan.pdf) | [Sacramento State Downtown](https://www.csus.edu/experience/capital-campus/)

### 1.3 Community Colleges

**Pipeline:** Proposition 2 (November 2024, passed) authorized $10B in state GO bonds: $8.5B for K-12, **$1.5B for community colleges**, with an explicit seismic retrofitting priority. Additionally, $45B+ in local community college bonds are queued statewide. A proposed 2026 College Health and Safety Bond Act (AB 48) would add more if placed on the November 2026 ballot.

**How SEs are selected:** Community colleges operate similarly to CSU — district-run QBS per project, DSA plan review required (community colleges fall under DSA oversight for new construction). Seismic retrofit work under Prop 2 is particularly relevant to KOR. **No equivalent to the K-12 school "prior DSA school experience" RFQ threshold** has been found in community college procurement (vs. LAUSD's explicit 3-project/3-year requirement). Smaller community college districts may be more accessible than LAUSD/SDUSD.

## Section 2: K-12 — DSA-Regulated School Construction

### 2.1 The DSA Track-Record Barrier

The California Division of the State Architect (DSA) requires plan review and approval for all K-12 public school construction. DSA does not itself license structural engineers — it requires a California-licensed SE or PE (structural) to be the A/E of Record or Design Professional in General Responsible Charge. However, **public school districts impose their own experience requirements through their A/E procurement processes.**

**LAUSD's formal threshold (RFQ R-26015, Sep 2025):**
- Minimum 3 years of experience providing SE services for DSA-approved California K-12 entities
- Minimum 3 DSA-approved school projects submitted as evidence (at least 1 must have DSA-approved design)
- At least 1 licensed California SE with ≥10 years' experience on staff
- For seismic peer review specifically: 3 years' experience with California K-12 or OSHPD/HCAI projects

KOR has **zero** DSA-approved K-12 projects in California. This is a hard procurement barrier for LAUSD and, by industry practice, SDUSD and similar large districts. It cannot be waived. The only path is to first build a DSA track record — which requires winning a first DSA school project, likely through a smaller/rural district or as a sub-consultant to an experienced SE firm.

### 3.1 Scale of the Pipeline

California's affordable housing production pipeline is the largest in the US:
- **CDLAC fall 2025 round:** $2B+ in tax-exempt bond allocations awarded to **108 developments** in a single round — a record.

### 3.3 How Structural Engineers Are Selected in Affordable Housing

**This is the key insight for KOR:** Affordable housing developers in California do **not** run formal government QBS procurement for their structural engineers. The selection process is:

1. **Developer selects architect** (often the same firm across multiple projects — e.g., a developer like Wakeland may use the same 2–3 architecture firms for all their projects)
2. **Architect recommends SE** or developer has a preferred SE from prior projects. It is a **relationship-driven, repeat-engagement model**.
3. **Federal/HUD rules** require competitive procurement for general contractors and certain major consultants, but for structural engineering specifically, informal competitive processes or sole-source engagements are common on private-developer TCAC projects.
4. **TCAC itself does not specify SE qualifications** — it reviews feasibility and financial underwriting, not design team credentials.

Key implication: **KOR's entry path is through the architect, not through the developer directly.** Identify architects who specialize in affordable multifamily (woodframe, Type V/III podium) in San Diego, LA, and Sacramento, and establish KOR as a preferred SE sub. CA-licensed architect firms active in this space include: Studio E, Architects Orange, Togawa Smith Martin, KTGY Architecture + Planning, WHA Architects, MCA Architecture.

### 3.4 Building Type Fit

KOR's woodframe competency is directly relevant. California affordable housing in the 3–6 story range is predominantly:
- **Type V-A woodframe** (3–4 stories stick frame, fire-resistive)
- **Type III-A woodframe over podium** (4–5 stories wood over 1–2 story concrete/PT podium)
- Some **Type I concrete/PT** for 7+ story affordable towers in high-value markets

California Residential Code (2025 edition) allows woodframe up to 3 stories for conventional construction; taller buildings require engineered drawings from a licensed SE, which is standard practice for 4–5 story affordable housing. This is KOR's zone.

## Section 4: Civic / Transit / Mixed Public

### 4.1 LA Metro Joint Development

**Pipeline:** LA Metro has committed to **10,000 homes by 2031** (5,000 income-restricted) via its Joint Development (JD) program. It has identified **20 "10K Sites"** for competitive developer proposals. The flagship project:

**District NoHo (North Hollywood Station):**
- Developer: Trammell Crow Company / High Street Residential (NOHO Development Associates LLC)
- Scale: 1,500 housing units, 500,000 SF office, 60,000 SF retail, public park, new transit center — Metro's largest JD ever
- Affordable: 311 units (60% AMI) in Phase I; Phase I also includes ~570 mixed-income apartments
- Design team: Gensler, HKS, Killefer Flammang Architecture (KFA), Relm Landscape
- **SE not publicly named** — hired through the developer's design team
- Timeline: initial components could begin 2025–2026; full build-out to 2031

**How SEs are selected (Metro JD):** Metro selects a developer via competitive RFP. The developer then independently assembles its design team and procures consultants — **SE selection is entirely developer-controlled**, not Metro-controlled. It follows the same relationship-driven pattern as private affordable housing. Entry requires relationship with the Trammell Crow/High Street design-build team or with the architect firms (Gensler, HKS, KFA).

**Other Metro 10K sites:** Temple/Beaudry Bus Lot is currently in community engagement. Metro's JD portal lists active sites at metro.net/about/joint_dev_pgm.

### 4.2 California Judicial Council / Courthouse Program

**Pipeline:** The Governor's FY2025–26 budget included $118.2M GF for six active courthouse capital projects (Butte, Fresno, Los Angeles, Plumas, San Luis Obispo, Solano Counties). The Judicial Council's Five-Year Infrastructure Plan (FY2026–27) outlines an ongoing multi-year program.

**How SEs are selected:** The Judicial Council runs competitive **IDIQ (Indefinite Delivery/Indefinite Quantity) on-call contracts for structural engineering**. The most recent RFP (RFP-FS-2023-22-MB) closed April 2024 and awarded contracts to **multiple firms simultaneously**. Key features:
- Competitive, qualifications-based (Government Code §4525 / Mini-Brooks Act)
- Multiple firms are selected per cycle (not single-source)
- Scope: structural assessment, analysis, seismic retrofit, new additions, building system upgrades
- IDIQ allows flexible task order assignments across the state's courthouse portfolio
- A new IDIQ cycle is expected around 2026–2027

**KOR entry read:** This is one of the most concrete, near-term opportunities in the civic/institutional lane. KOR's seismic credentials are directly relevant. The next RFP cycle would be an open competition. **Recommended: monitor courts.ca.gov/programs-initiatives/facilities for the next structural SE IDIQ solicitation.**

Sources: [Judicial Council RFP-FS-2023-22](https://www.courts.ca.gov/76385.htm) | [Judicial Council 5-Year FY2026-27](https://courts.ca.gov/system/files/file/5year2627_1.pdf) | [CA Court FY2025-26 budget LAO](https://lao.ca.gov)

## Section 5: Regulatory Framework for SE Procurement in California

California Government Code §4525 et seq. (**"Mini-Brooks Act"**) governs all state and local agency procurement of engineering services. Key provisions:
- Selection must be based on **demonstrated competence and professional qualifications** — price cannot be used as selection criterion until a firm is chosen
- Applies to: state agencies, UC, CSU, K-12 districts, community colleges, courts, ports, cities, counties
- Equivalent to the federal Brooks Act
- Result: All public-sector SE procurement in California is formally QBS — which levels the playing field for qualified firms without long incumbent lock-in, **provided** the agency's specific RFQ does not layer in experience requirements (as LAUSD does for DSA school track record)

Source: [CA Govt Code §4525](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?article=&chapter=10.&division=5.&lawCode=GOV&part=&title=1.) | [QBS in California — Nomos LLP](https://calconstructionlawblog.com/2014/06/09/the-b-s-in-qbs-has-price-crept-into-qualification-based-selection/)

## Section 6: KOR's Four-Lane Priority Read

**Ranking from most to least realistic for near-term entry:**

### Lane 1 — Affordable Woodframe Housing (BEST FIT, pursue now)
KOR's woodframe seismic engineering is the strongest fit in the entire institutional landscape. The pipeline is massive (CDLAC alone awards $2B+ per round; TCAC $500M/year). The procurement is relationship-driven — no RFQ gatekeepers, no DSA track-record requirement, no formal pre-qualification barrier. Entry is via architect partnerships: find 2–3 affordable multifamily architects active in San Diego/LA (KTGY, Architects Orange, Studio E, WHA, MCA) and position as preferred SE. Wakeland (SD), Jamboree (OC), BRIDGE (Bay Area/LA), and Chelsea (SD) are the top developer targets. A single awarded project opens recurring pipeline.

**Action:** Direct outreach to affordable multifamily architects in San Diego + OC + LA within 30 days.

### Lane 2 — Judicial Council Courthouse IDIQ (monitor, bid next cycle)
The Judicial Council's structural engineering IDIQ (multiple firms selected, open competition) is the cleanest public-sector path. Seismic expertise is exactly what they need. No DSA/K-12/HCAI track record required. The last solicitation closed April 2024; expect a new cycle in 2026–2027. A Markulin SE stamp on a California public building project would satisfy license requirements. **Recommended: register on courts.ca.gov solicitation list and bid the next IDIQ.**

### Lane 3 — CSU / Community College Capital (medium-term, build relationships)
No formal SE pre-qualification barrier at CSU. The 2026–2031 five-year plan is multi-billion. Smaller CSU campuses (Stanislaus, Dominguez Hills, Channel Islands) are less competitive than UCLA/UCSD. Community college seismic retrofit (Prop 2 priority) is an even cleaner entry — QBS, seismic focus, smaller contract sizes. Build an architect partner at the campus level. This is a 12–24 month play; worth initiating architect introductions now.

### Lane 4 — K-12 / UC / HCAI / Ports (incumbent-gated or track-record-required — skip near-term)
- **K-12 (LAUSD/SDUSD):** Formal 3-project DSA requirement blocks entry. Skip until 1–2 DSA approvals can be secured via smaller district or sub-consultant role.
- **UC system:** No formal barrier but extremely competitive. Saiful Bouquet, Forell/Elsesser, Thornton Tomasetti dominate. Entry via architect team only; 18–36 month minimum timeline.
- **HCAI/Hospitals:** Hard OSHPD/HCAI track-record barrier — equivalent to DSA but steeper. Separate strategic decision required. See `hcai-hospital-2030-seismic-2026-06-17.md`.
- **Ports:** Incumbent-dominated; no active RFP identified. Lower priority.
