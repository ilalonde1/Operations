## 2. Submarket Narratives

### 2.1 Inland Empire (Riverside–San Bernardino–Ontario–Rancho Cucamonga)
**HIGH volume, LOW structural complexity.**

The Inland Empire is California's most active multifamily construction volume story outside the Bay Area, with 7,014 units delivered in 2025 (best year of the decade) and 7,198 under construction as of January 2026, plus 45,000 in planning/permitting stages. [Source: Yardi Matrix, March 2026 report]

However, the product type is almost entirely 3–4 story wood-frame garden apartment—the low end of KOR's capability envelope. Average asking rent is $2,151/month (down 0.3%), which is insufficient to support mid-rise concrete or steel construction economics. Key performance submarkets are SW Riverside County/Temecula, Rancho Cucamonga, and Ontario/Chino, where Q1 2026 saw 210 of 260 delivered units concentrated.

The Brightline West high-speed rail terminal at Rancho Cucamonga (projected 2028–2029) and Ontario International Airport expansion create a potential transit-oriented development corridor where mid-rise density becomes economically viable. This is a 3–5 year opportunity, not immediate.

Developers visible: Lennar (48 communities in IE), Fairfield Residential (national institutional), Presidio Capital + Fuller Apartment Homes (Temecula/wine country). No SE firms are publicly credited on specific IE multifamily projects. KPFF's Southern CA office would be the likely incumbent on any institutional-grade project.

**Verdict: MONITOR.** Check again when Brightline West entitlements generate TOD mid-rise projects near Rancho Cucamonga station, likely 2027+.

### 2.2 Central Valley (Fresno, Bakersfield, Stockton, Modesto)
**THIN for KOR except Bakersfield long-term.**

Fresno: Construction has collapsed to just 96 units underway (Q4 2025), all affordable or low-rise. Rents at $1,480/unit cannot support structured construction. Downtown large apartment plans have stalled. YM Architects + Breen Engineering are visible on affordable work. No market-rate mid-rise developer ecosystem present. [Source: Colliers Q4 2025 Fresno MF Report]

Bakersfield: The landmark "Park Center at Seven Oaks" — Kern County's largest-ever multifamily development at 520 units by Presidio Capital Partners, Fuller Apartment Homes, and Bolthouse Properties (master-plan land) — broke ground May 2025 with Phase 1 (352 units) preleasing Spring 2026. This is entirely suburban garden product. However, Bakersfield's future HSR station corridor has been rezoned for up to 100 du/acre density — a genuine mid-rise trigger if state-supported development materializes near the station, circa 2030+. [Source: BakersfieldNow, Bolthouse Properties press release, May 2025]

Stockton / Modesto: No significant market-rate pipeline identified in web research. Affordable-only infill at scale below KOR's structural complexity threshold.

**Verdict: SKIP (Fresno, Stockton, Modesto). MONITOR LONG-TERM (Bakersfield HSR corridor, 2029+).**

### 2.3 Central Coast (Santa Barbara, San Luis Obispo, Ventura, Oxnard)
**SEISMIC angle real; new-construction pipeline thin.**

Santa Barbara County: $116M asset sale of La Vista (460 units, largest-ever SB County MF deal) in December 2024 confirms institutional appetite, but this was a re-sale, not new development. Coastal Commission entitlement constraints make new mid-rise construction near-impossible. The seismic retrofit angle (Category D/E, soft-story) is more viable — but KOR is not yet licensed for California seismic retrofit work and this is a specialist niche dominated by local retrofit contractors (ProStruct Engineering, Retrofit1). [Source: Marcus Millichap / IPA, Dec 2024; Colliers Central Coast MF Divide]

San Luis Obispo: ~330 units pipeline, largest projects are Avila Ranch (310 u, 2025) and Reserve at Vinedo (214 u, $97M build-to-rent, Paso Robles). All are 3-story garden product. Cal Poly enrollment supports demand but rent levels (~$1,800–$2,200) do not support concrete/steel mid-rise. Vacancy at 7.4% — elevated. [Source: HartCRE Q2 2024 SLO MF Market Report]

Ventura / Oxnard: The Lugo Mixed-Use Project is in CEQA review (2024–2025). Soft-story retrofit mandate under California SB 1705/equivalent is actively generating SE work in Ventura County. The 2025 CBC update (Title 24, effective Jan 1 2026) requires all new and retrofit work to be stamped by a licensed CA SE. This is a compliance-driven entry point. However, KOR lacks CA licensure at this time.

**Verdict: MONITOR (Ventura/Oxnard seismic retrofit angle if KOR pursues CA SE license). SKIP for new construction pipeline.**

### 2.4 San Diego North County (Carlsbad, Oceanside, San Marcos, Escondido)
**ACTIVE mid-rise pipeline; direct extension of KOR's San Diego work.**

This is the most compelling secondary submarket for near-term KOR BD activity.

Carlsbad: Rincon Homes has a 5-story, 62-foot, 50-unit for-sale condo + 3,700 SF retail project on Carlsbad Village Drive in active application. This is precisely KOR's SD product type — for-sale mid-rise condo, mixed-use podium. Architect not publicly named yet. [Source: North County Pipeline substack]

Oceanside: Level 3 Construction is active GC on multiple affordable and mixed-use projects. "Blocks 5 and 20" is a 373-unit two-building mixed-use project (193 + 180 units) with ~17,400 SF commercial — this is the most structurally significant project in North County, comparable to KOR's existing SD work. Ocean Creek is 295 units in 5 buildings at 4 stories. [Source: CEQA filings, Level 3 Construction press releases]

San Marcos: North City is the largest single project — 3,400 units, 200 acres, $2B total investment, adjacent to CSUSM, currently under construction. This mixed-use urban village is being built in phases; the 224-unit 4-story affordable piece (Rise Carmel LLC / Urban Villages San Marcos) is in CDLAC/CalHFA pipeline. The overall North City master plan is generating continuous mid-rise product flow. [Source: Voice of San Diego, April 2026; CalHFA multifamily approved list]

Structural engineering: KPFF's San Diego office covers the region. No named SE on North County projects specifically. WorkPoint Engineering is named on Frontwave Arena (Oceanside) — a commercial/civic project.

Geographic proximity to KOR's existing San Diego projects means BD cost (travel, relationships) is minimal. The BD lever is the same as core SD: architects and developers doing 4–6 story podium/stick-frame.

**Verdict: PURSUE (Carlsbad + Oceanside Blocks 5+20 as near-term targets; San Marcos North City as medium-term relationship build).**

### 2.5 Sacramento Valley Deepening (Beyond Primary Stream)
**Slower pipeline than peak but real KOR-complexity projects present.**

The Sacramento market construction pipeline has contracted: 3,135 units under construction as of Q1 2026, down 37% year-over-year. However, at $230,869 average price per unit (up 41% y/y), the per-unit economics now support structured mid-rise, unlike the value-constrained periods prior. [Source: NorthMarq Q1 2026 Sacramento MF]

Key developer: SKK Developments (founder Sotiris Kolokotronis; $2.5B lifetime portfolio, 3,000+ apartments across NorCal). SKK's current pipeline includes 1,689 units across 6 sites + the Grand Gateway Master Plan in West Sacramento (332 apartments + 120-key hotel + 30,000 SF retail) — exactly KOR's mixed-use structural complexity level. SKK also developed Q19 Midtown, H16 Apartments, and Eleanor Apartments with Grupe Company as joint-venture partner, indicating a track record with major developers. [Source: SKK Developments website; Multi-Housing News]

The 840 Delta Lane project in West Sacramento (195 units, 5-story market-rate, in planning) is another direct KOR-type target. [Source: NorthMarq Sacramento report]

Architecture firms active: LPAS Architecture + Design (Sacramento + Oakland; multifamily specialist since 1978, university/civic/mixed-use; awards credits include multifamily of the year); Entero Architects (Sacramento County residential); DeSCor Builders (multifamily development + GC).

Structural engineering incumbents: KPFF Northern California (Sacramento hiring active, active pipeline); Degenkolb Engineers (428 J St Sacramento, seismic/structural specialist — KOR's closest analogue in the market).

The BD angle is to approach LPAS and SKK Developments before projects reach structural design phase. KOR should also track the West Sacramento Grand Gateway application timeline (mixed-use hotel + residential = needs SE with multi-use experience, KOR's strength).

**Verdict: PURSUE (MEDIUM-TERM) — SKK/LPAS relationship cultivation; West Sacramento Grand Gateway + 840 Delta Lane as specific near-term targets; lower urgency than primary Sacramento stream.**

## 3. Prioritized Read — Which Secondary Submarkets Matter for KOR

**Pursue now (near-term, 0–12 months):**
San Diego North County (Carlsbad + Oceanside Blocks 5+20) is the highest-priority secondary submarket — it is a direct extension of KOR's existing SD BD work, with 4–5 story mixed-use mid-rise projects in active permitting, architects not yet locked to SE, and minimal incremental BD overhead. Treat as part of the SD stream, not a separate initiative.

**Pursue medium-term (6–18 months):**
Sacramento Valley Deepening (SKK Developments / LPAS relationship; West Sacramento Grand Gateway; 840 Delta Lane). The pipeline has contracted but per-unit economics now support KOR-complexity product. SKK is the clearest named developer target in California outside the primary streams. San Marcos North City ($2B master plan) is a medium-term architecture-relationship play, best approached via the same architects KOR is cultivating in core SD.

**Monitor, do not invest BD time now:**
Inland Empire (watch Rancho Cucamonga/Ontario Metrolink TOD corridor when Brightline West timeline solidifies, 2027+). Central Coast Ventura/Oxnard (seismic retrofit angle relevant only if/when KOR pursues CA SE licensure). Bakersfield HSR corridor (2029+ horizon).

**Skip:**
Fresno, Stockton, Modesto — rent levels and product types are below KOR's structural complexity threshold. Central Valley affordable-only markets will not generate SE fee volume commensurate with BD cost. Santa Barbara and San Luis Obispo new construction pipelines are too constrained by coastal entitlement restrictions.
