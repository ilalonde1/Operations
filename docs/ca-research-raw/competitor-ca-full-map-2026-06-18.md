# California Structural Engineering Competitive Map — KOR Entry Analysis
**Date:** 2026-06-18 | **Purpose:** Full-state competitor landscape for KOR Structural's CA mid-market entry (seismic + woodframe/podium + mid/high-rise concrete)

---

## Executive Summary: KOR's Realistic Competitive Wedges

1. **Seismic retrofit pipeline is massive and open.** LA alone has 13,500+ soft-story buildings under mandatory ordinance; additional ordinances in Santa Monica, West Hollywood, Pasadena, Long Beach, Burbank, and Glendale create a multi-year multi-city retrofit queue that exceeds incumbent capacity. KOR's EGBC seismic heritage is directly credentialed for this work once CA SE license is in hand.
2. **Mid-market woodframe/podium (Type V over Type I) is KPFF/DCI/VCA territory but none have a lock.** These are volume-production deals where price, schedule, and BIM competence matter more than brand. An aggressive new entrant with BC podium depth can win architect-developer pairs who are frustrated with large-firm backlog.
3. **Post-acquisition fog at Englekirk (WSP) is a real opening.** WSP absorbed a 90-person boutique seismic shop in 2021; cultural integration drag is documented (headcount fell from 90 to ~69 by 2024). Architects who valued boutique seismic depth are actively re-evaluating.
4. **NYA is in leadership transition.** Founder Nabih Youssef died July 2024 — the firm continues under John Paul Youssef and a principal team, but institutional client relationships built around the founder's personal reputation are in play for competitors who can offer equivalent seismic credibility.
5. **KOR cannot contest trophy/supertall (TT, JAMA, Brandow & Johnston) or large-campus institutional (Degenkolb, Englekirk/WSP, Saiful Bouquet). The wedge is: 5–25 story concrete and podium residential + mixed-use, seismic retrofit on mid-size multifamily stock, and under-served San Diego / Sacramento markets where KPFF's dominance creates wait-time complaints.**

## Detailed Firm Profiles

### KPFF Consulting Engineers
**HQ:** Seattle, WA | **Founded:** 1960s | **CA Presence:** LA (280 staff, since 1967), San Diego (self-described "largest SE in SD," founded 1994), SF/NorCal (civil), Irvine, Newport Beach | **Firm Total:** ~1,400 across 27+ offices; revenue ~$240–248M (2024)

KPFF is the dominant mid-market SE in California by sheer footprint. The LA office (Greater Los Angeles Structural, operating as GLAS with two locations in DTLA and Irvine) handles everything from healthcare to multifamily residential to mass transit. The San Diego office explicitly claims the largest SE team in San Diego. Their stated competitive strength is communication excellence — "4 Marvin Black Partnering Awards" signals deep embed with institutional architects.

**Architect/Developer Lock:** Carrier Johnson + Culture (San Diego 7th & Market mixed-use with Cisterra Development confirmed), CSU system, general relationship with major SoCal architecture firms. GLAS work includes Providence Cedars-Sinai Medical Center, Destination Crenshaw, and LACCD educational.

**Vulnerability:** Large-firm backlog and overhead pricing; the four separate CA offices have inconsistent responsiveness; multifamily podium is a volume play they handle but it is not their crown jewel. Architects on 50-unit podium projects sometimes feel they are second-tier clients at KPFF.

**KOR Contestable:** Yes, particularly in San Diego and Sacramento where a nimble entrant with competitive pricing and faster turnaround can attract the mid-market architect practices (10–100 unit residential, 5–15 story podium). KPFF's "largest in San Diego" claim is also a vulnerability — it signals premium positioning that KOR can undercut.

### Saiful Bouquet Structural Engineers
**HQ:** Pasadena / Los Angeles, CA | **Founded:** 1997 | **CA Presence:** LA, Pasadena, San Diego | **Size:** ~81 employees; one of five largest pure SE firms in Southern California

Founded with 4 people, grown to 81 through organic growth. Now positioned as a broad multi-sector firm handling 27+ market segments including high-rise, healthcare, higher education, mission critical, hospitality, and seismic. CEO Dr. Saiful Islam has 35+ years of experience; founding Principal Tom Bouquet similarly. The firm won commissions on One Beverly Hills ($3B, Gensler architect), LAX Midfield Satellite Concourse, and 29-story LA City Center hotel-residences.

**Architect/Developer Lock:** Caltech, UCLA, UC Riverside, UC Irvine, Kaiser Permanente, CHOC Children's Hospital, Petersen Automotive Museum, Bloomingdale's, USC Keck Medical (completion 2025). Gensler relationship confirmed on One Beverly Hills. They presented the "Landmark Tower" (34-story high-rise) at SEAOC 2024.

**Vulnerability:** At 81 staff serving 27 sectors, they spread thin. Heavy institutional / healthcare / university focus means multifamily podium is a secondary line, not their identity. Fee structure likely premium. KOR would not contest their healthcare or USC/UC portfolio.

**KOR Contestable:** Partially — on mid-rise residential and mixed-use podium where architects find Saiful Bouquet over-resourced or over-priced for the project type.

### Englekirk / WSP USA Buildings
**HQ:** Los Angeles, CA (absorbed into WSP 2021) | **Founded:** 1983 as Englekirk | **CA Presence:** LA (only) | **Size:** ~69 in CA (was 90 at acquisition); operates as WSP USA Buildings, Inc. dba Englekirk Structural Engineers

WSP acquired Englekirk in October 2021 for its West Coast seismic specialty capability. Englekirk had long been the premier seismic boutique in California — performance-based design, code development participation, CSU system peer review (confirmed via CSU master enabling agreement signed 2024). The 25% headcount reduction since acquisition (90 → ~69) is a material signal.

**Architect/Developer Lock:** CSU system (confirmed master enabling agreement as WSP USA Buildings/Englekirk for systemwide seismic peer review services). Legacy Englekirk relationships with institutional clients across SoCal.

**Vulnerability:** Post-acquisition cultural friction. WSP is a 70,000-person global engineering conglomerate; Englekirk's boutique identity is diluted. Engineers who want to work on seismic code development and be known personally to clients leave for Degenkolb or start their own firms. Procurement overhead of going through WSP's system is a deterrent for small-medium clients. This is the clearest opening for KOR on a seismic credential play — architects who previously called Englekirk now face a corporate middleware layer.

**KOR Contestable:** YES — the seismic boutique gap left by the acquisition is real. KOR's Vancouver seismic heritage (founder Bryson, EGBC seismic pedigree) is directly credible to the SoCal seismic design community. Target the architects and developers who were Englekirk's pre-WSP loyal base.

Revenue data inconsistent (reported $31–100M range) suggesting possible data gap on true size.

**KOR Contestable:** Yes on seismic retrofit mid-size multifamily (LAX ordinance buildings, SF soft-story, SD Housing Commission-type portfolios). NOT on their high-rise new construction with Gensler.

### Degenkolb Engineers
**HQ:** San Francisco, CA | **Founded:** 1956 | **CA Presence:** SF (HQ), Oakland, Sacramento, LA, Orange County, San Diego (6 CA offices) | **Size:** 392 employees (2024), growing at ~4%/year; $41.6M revenue [Probable — older figure]

Degenkolb is the oldest dedicated earthquake engineering firm in the US, founded by Henry J. Degenkolb who helped write seismic codes used globally. The firm is deeply embedded in the civic, healthcare, higher education, and corporate seismic program management space. They do new buildings (performance-based, high-seismic) but their brand and client base is heavily retrofit/evaluation/lifecycle-management. Their corporate seismic programs business (managing portfolios of buildings for tech campuses, healthcare systems) is proprietary recurring revenue.

**Architect/Developer Lock:** UC/CSU system, California government, tech corporate campuses (Cupertino confirmed in portfolio), Oakland office towers, Glendale soft-story multifamily (one confirmed project in portfolio).

**Vulnerability:** Not a multifamily podium volume shop. Fee structure is premium (earthquake engineering specialists command higher rates). Less competitive on commodity residential. New San Jose Mexico office is interesting expansion but does not directly affect KOR.

**KOR Contestable:** On seismic retrofit of mid-size multifamily stock (SF mandatory program, LA soft-story ordinance) — KOR can offer comparable technical quality at lower cost than Degenkolb's premium structure. Not contestable on healthcare/campus/government institutional work.

## Competitive Wedge Analysis: Where KOR Wins in California

**Wedge 1 — Seismic Retrofit Queue (Immediate Revenue)**
The LA soft-story ordinance (13,500 buildings), SF mandatory retrofit program, and 8+ adjacent city ordinances (Santa Monica, West Hollywood, Pasadena, Long Beach, Burbank, Glendale, Downey, Torrance) represent a multi-decade retrofit pipeline. Dominant players (Miyamoto, Degenkolb, Structural Focus) are running near capacity on institutional commissions. Mid-size building owners (10–50 unit apartment buildings) are under-served by boutique firms and over-priced by institutional SEs. KOR's seismic pedigree (EGBC seismic registration, Bryson heritage) directly qualifies for this work. Entry cost: California SE license + local SE references. Revenue potential: $60K–$200K/building at volume.

**Wedge 2 — Mid-Market Woodframe/Podium New Construction (Growth Lane)**
The 5-over-2 podium type (Type V over Type I, 5–8 stories, 50–300 units) is California's dominant new multifamily form in 2024–2026. KPFF handles it at premium pricing with backlog. DCI handles it from a PNW base. VCA handles it at commodity volume pricing with a developer-first orientation. KOR's wedge: architect-first positioning (serve the architect, not the developer), BC podium depth, BIM competence, and pricing positioned between VCA's commodity rate and KPFF's premium. Target: mid-size CA residential architecture firms (KTGY adjacent, Humphreys adjacent, boutique firms not on VCA's 43-client roster).

**Wedge 3 — Post-Acquisition Gap at Englekirk/WSP (Time-Sensitive)**
WSP absorbed Englekirk's 90-person seismic boutique in 2021; 25% of that team is gone. Architects who want boutique seismic expertise on institutional/mixed-use commissions are actively looking. KOR can position as the "Englekirk experience without the WSP overhead" for projects in the $5M–$50M range where Englekirk's institutional credibility mattered but the WSP machine is overkill. This window is 2–4 years before the market re-equilibrates.

**Wedge 4 — San Diego Geographic Opening**
KPFF is self-described as "the largest SE in San Diego" — which is a market-dominance signal, not a vulnerability, but their scale creates a premium-positioning gap. DCI's SD office is their strongest CA footpost but is PNW-origin. Saiful Bouquet's SD office is their secondary market. No boutique-but-serious CA SE firm is aggressively serving SD's mid-market podium residential. SD's downtown (Barrio Logan, Hillcrest, Mission Hills, Point Loma) is a high-density multifamily growth corridor. KOR's first CA office should be San Diego — lower competition intensity than LA, established architecture community, and a base from which to expand north.
