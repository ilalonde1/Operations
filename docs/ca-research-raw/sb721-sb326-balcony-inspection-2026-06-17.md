## 1. Law Summary: Scope, Deadlines, Recurring Cycle

### 1.1 Origins
Both laws trace directly to the June 2015 Library Gardens balcony collapse in Berkeley that killed six students. The failure was a wood-framed balcony with hidden dry rot; inspectors had never checked it. [Confirmed — https://www.bvna.com/california-balcony-inspection-bill-mandate-requires-compliance-january-1-2025sb-721-sb-326]

### 1.2 SB 326 — Condominiums / HOAs (Davis-Stirling)

**Statute:** California Civil Code §5551 (SB 326, signed 2019).

**Who it covers:** Condominium associations and common interest developments (CIDs) governed by the Davis-Stirling Act with **three or more dwelling units**. Covers all exterior elevated elements (EEEs) — balconies, decks, walkways, stairways, landings, railings — that are (a) more than 6 feet above grade, (b) supported by wood or wood-based products, and (c) intended for human occupancy/use. Does NOT apply to planned developments where homeowners own their individual structures outright; targets associations that hold maintenance obligations for exterior elements. [Confirmed — https://mbkchapman.com/misinformation-about-california-hoa-balcony-law-sb-326-fact-sheet/]

**First inspection deadline:** January 1, 2025 (already passed; no extension granted unlike SB 721). [Confirmed — https://baylegal.com/sb-326-for-hoas-condominium-balcony-inspection-requirements-after-january-2026/]

**Recurring cycle:** Every **9 years** from the date of the initial inspection.

**New construction exception:** Buildings receive a 6-year grace period from the certificate of occupancy before the first inspection is due.

**Sampling methodology:** Statistical sampling at **95% confidence / 5% margin of error** — typically means a larger percentage of elements must be inspected than under SB 721's flat 15% floor.

**Who qualifies to inspect:**
- Licensed Structural Engineers (CA PE/SE)
- Licensed Architects
- Licensed Civil Engineers (added by SB 2114 to expand capacity)
- Licensed contractors and certified building inspectors do **NOT** qualify for SB 326.

This is more restrictive than SB 721 and requires a PE-stamped inspection report. The inspector takes full legal and professional liability for the report. [Confirmed — https://vertexeng.com/insights/california-balcony-deck-inspection-faqs/]

**Enforcement:** Civil penalties of **$100–$500/day** per non-complying association; $500/day max = $182,500/year potential exposure. No state agency actively audits HOAs (CA DRE does not enforce Davis-Stirling; local building departments lack jurisdiction over HOA internal inspections). Primary enforcement drivers are: (1) insurance carriers requiring valid SB 326 reports as a condition of renewing master policies, and (2) litigation liability — a non-compliant HOA faces "negligence per se" exposure in any future injury suit. [Confirmed — https://baylegal.com/sb-326-for-hoas-condominium-balcony-inspection-requirements-after-january-2026/]

### 1.3 SB 721 — Rental Apartment Buildings

**Statute:** California Health & Safety Code §17973 (SB 721, signed 2018; amended by AB 2579).

**Who it covers:** Rental apartment buildings (non-HOA) with **three or more multifamily dwelling units**. Same physical scope: EEEs more than 6 feet above grade supported by wood or wood-based products. [Confirmed — https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=201720180SB721]

**First inspection deadline:** Originally January 1, 2025; extended to **January 1, 2026** by Assembly Bill 2579 (signed September 2024) specifically because qualified inspector capacity was inadequate to handle the volume. [Confirmed — https://rimkus.com/article/california-balcony-inspection/]

**Recurring cycle:** Every **6 years** after the initial inspection.

**Sampling methodology:** Minimum **15% of each type of EEE** — a lower statistical floor than SB 326, though the inspector may recommend greater coverage.

**Who qualifies to inspect:**
- Licensed Structural Engineers (CA PE/SE)
- Licensed Civil Engineers
- Licensed Architects
- Licensed B General Contractors or C-5 Framing Contractors with **5+ years experience** constructing multistory wood-frame buildings
- Certified Building Inspectors (jurisdiction-dependent)

This broader pool means contractors with no PE can perform SB 721 inspections, driving down price floor and increasing competition for apartment work relative to condo/HOA work. [Confirmed — https://vertexeng.com/insights/california-balcony-deck-inspection-faqs/]

**Enforcement:** Same $100–$500/day civil penalties, enforced by **local city or county building departments** (not the state), creating geographic variation in enforcement vigor. [Confirmed — https://saundersseismic.com/blog/california-balcony-inspection-law-guide-2/]

**Repair obligation:** Once deficiencies are identified, owners have **120 days** to complete all repairs or face further enforcement.

### 1.4 AB 2579 (2024) Amendment Note
This amendment only extended the SB 721 deadline (apartments); the SB 326 deadline (HOAs/condos) was NOT extended and expired January 1, 2025. Any condo HOA that has not completed inspection is currently over 18 months past the statutory deadline as of June 2026. [Confirmed — https://www.baycitiesconstruction.com/blog/is-the-sb-326-deadline-in-2026-california-hoa-balcony-law-ab-2579-explained]

## 2. Market Size and Compliance Backlog

### 2.1 SB 721 — Rental Apartments

**Buildings in scope:** Approximately **500,000 apartment facilities** in California are subject to SB 721. This figure is cited by multiple industry sources and is consistent with California's housing stock (CA has ~6M renter-occupied households, the largest rental market in the US). [Probable — cited in https://www.authoritypm.com/how-sb-721-is-shaping-the-future-of-property-management-in-california-a-comprehensive-analysis; consistent with Census data showing CA's multifamily stock]

**Inspector capacity (at law's original 2025 deadline):** Only **~200 qualified inspectors** statewide were active at the time of the original deadline, meaning each would need to complete 2,500+ inspections/year to clear the backlog. This capacity crisis is directly why AB 2579 extended the deadline. [Probable — cited in https://slpm.com/new-california-balcony-inspection-law-sb-721/ and https://palmtreeproperties.com/property-management-resources/sb-721-ca-deadline/; multiple sources reference the "200 inspector" figure]

**Compliance rate as of January 2026:** No official state-tracked statistic exists. However, based on the known capacity constraint and the widespread industry commentary about 2-3 month booking backlogs extending even into smaller metros, **a significant fraction of the ~500,000 buildings were non-compliant at the January 1, 2026 deadline**. [Unverified — no authoritative compliance-rate data published; estimated majority non-compliant based on capacity math]

**Recurring demand rhythm:** Every 6 years. If ~500,000 buildings need inspection every 6 years, that's roughly **83,000 building inspections/year** as a steady-state demand, in addition to the initial compliance surge.

### 2.2 SB 326 — Condominiums / HOAs

**HOA communities in California:** Approximately **50,700+ HOA communities** operate statewide, housing ~14.4M residents across ~5M housing units. California leads the nation. [Confirmed — https://lifetimehoa.com/california-hoa-stats/; citing CAE Foundation Statistical Review 2024 and Community Associations Institute data]

**Subset applicable to SB 326:** Not all 50,700 HOAs are SB 326-relevant — planned developments where owners hold their own structure are excluded. Condominium associations (where the HOA owns/maintains exterior elements) are the primary target. No official count of qualifying condominium associations is published; a reasonable working estimate based on the CA condo/cooperative share of the HOA universe is **25,000–35,000 qualifying associations**. [Unverified — derived estimate; no authoritative breakout by CID type found]

**Compliance rate:** Over 18 months past the January 1, 2025 SB 326 deadline with no state enforcement agency actively auditing. A meaningful share of HOAs — particularly small ones — are non-compliant. The primary compliance forcing function is **insurance renewal**: carriers increasingly require a valid SB 326 report before renewing master policies. [Probable — https://baylegal.com/sb-326-for-hoas-condominium-balcony-inspection-requirements-after-january-2026/]

**Recurring demand rhythm:** Every 9 years. Even at 25,000 qualifying associations this is ~2,800 inspections/year steady-state, but the initial compliance catch-up wave is much larger.

### 2.3 Aggregate Market Size Estimate

No published third-party market sizing report was found. Deriving from first principles:

**SB 721 — Initial surge (apartment buildings):**
- ~500,000 buildings x average inspection fee of ~$3,500 (blended: small 3-plex to 100-unit buildings; conservative given many are small 3-6 unit buildings) = **~$1.75B total initial compliance market**
- Many buildings still in compliance backlog as of June 2026; this is a multi-year wave.

**SB 326 — Condominium HOAs:**
- ~30,000 qualifying associations x average inspection fee ~$8,000 (mid-size HOA blend from DrBalcony pricing table) = **~$240M total initial compliance market**
- 9-year recurring cycle = ~$27M/year steady-state

**Combined recurring steady-state (post initial-wave):** Roughly **$300–400M/year** across both cycles at full maturity. This is not a small niche. [Unverified estimate derived from building count assumptions and fee data; treat as order-of-magnitude]

## 5. KOR-Specific Strategic Analysis

### 5.1 Licensing Position
KOR holds a California PE license via John Markulin. Markulin's stamp can sign SB 326 reports (requires licensed SE/Civil Engineer/Architect). SB 721 reports can also be stamped. KOR can legally perform both types of inspection under California law. No residency requirement exists; a CA-licensed engineer at a Vancouver-based firm can perform inspections and stamp reports provided they conduct the physical site visit. [Confirmed — California licensure law makes no distinction between in-state and out-of-state PE holders for performing licensed work; https://www.bpelsg.ca.gov/applicants/seappinst.shtml]

However: **In-person site visits are required.** The statutes mandate direct visual inspection including exploratory openings where needed. Remote inspection or desk review does not satisfy the mandate. KOR would need to either fly Markulin (or another CA-licensed KOR engineer) to the site, or establish a Southern California presence that can handle site visits. [Confirmed — inspection requirement is physical; implied by all statute language reviewed]

### 5.2 Advantages for KOR in This Lane

1. **SB 326 restricts to SEs/Architects/CEs only.** This eliminates contractor-tier competition entirely for HOA/condo work. The pool for SB 326 is materially smaller than for SB 721 — a structural engineer has a genuine qualification advantage.

2. **PE-stamped report = professional liability.** Property owners and their insurers want the defensibility of a PE report. KOR's SE credential (structural engineer designation) arguably reads better than a civil engineer for structural adequacy assessments.

3. **Structural knowledge is directly applicable.** EEE inspections focus on wood-frame structural deterioration, connection failure, waterproofing-to-structure interface, and load path continuity — all core SE competency, not forensic specialty.

4. **KOR already operates in SoCal (SD and LA).** This lowers the marginal travel cost of adding inspection work when already in market for other business development.

5. **Repair design follow-on.** When an inspection identifies structural deficiencies, the building owner needs a repair design and permit drawings. That is pure SE work. An SE performing the inspection is the natural follow-on for repair design — this is the higher-value half of the service.

### 5.3 Disadvantages / Risks

1. **High volume + low margin per inspection.** SE firms doing inspections well report fee ranges of $3,500–$18,000 per building, but a mid-size HOA inspection by one engineer takes 1 full day on-site + 1 day report writing. At a $8,000 fee for a 20-unit building and a $185/hr blended KOR rate, that's ~43 hrs to break even. Labor-intensive relative to structural design work.

2. **Travel cost erodes margin significantly.** Every Vancouver→LA or Vancouver→SD trip adds $700–$1,200 in airfare + hotel + time. At $2,500 for a small HOA inspection, travel alone destroys the margin. Only feasible for larger buildings ($10,000+) or trip-batched multi-property visits.

3. **Market is already commoditized for the volume tier.** DrBalcony, EmpireWorks, and the contractor-tier firms have built scalable, price-competitive models. KOR cannot compete on price in the SB 721 apartment market against firms with local staff on salary. [Confirmed — market competition evident from search results]

4. **The first-wave rush is ending.** SB 326 deadline passed January 2025; SB 721 deadline passed January 2026. The initial compliance surge — the highest-demand, highest-urgency period — is behind us. The market transitions now from a compliance-rush to a steady-state 6/9-year recurring cycle plus laggard catch-up. Entering now means competing for the catch-up tail and recurring wave, not the initial rush. [Confirmed — https://baylegal.com/sb-721-balcony-inspection-deadline-what-california-property-owners-must-know-in-2026/]

5. **Enforcement is soft.** No state agency actively audits HOA compliance with SB 326. Local enforcement of SB 721 is variable. The real enforcement is insurance-driven — but a non-compliant HOA that doesn't want to be found out may simply wait. This means the "500,000 non-compliant buildings" story somewhat overstates immediate demand. [Probable — https://baylegal.com/sb-326-for-hoas-condominium-balcony-inspection-requirements-after-january-2026/]

6. **Repair design is where SE adds most value — but requires a sustained CA client relationship.** One-off inspection-only work doesn't build the relationship that yields the repair engineering follow-on. Without local client management, KOR would be delivering inspection reports and watching a local firm capture the repair design work.

7. **Sole-stamp risk.** With only one CA PE (Markulin), every SB 326 report requires his physical presence and signature. If Markulin is unavailable/leaves, the entire EEE service line stops. This is a concentration risk in a mandated-inspection product where clients need guaranteed recurring service.

## 6. KOR Entry Recommendation

### Honest Summary: **Conditional No** as a standalone lane; **Conditional Yes** as a bundled add-on

**Do not enter as a primary revenue stream.** The EEE inspection market is real and recurring, but it is structured against a small out-of-state firm trying to build standalone volume:
- The first-wave surge is over. Timing is unfavorable.
- The per-inspection economics are poor relative to travel costs at KOR's size.
- The volume market (SB 721 apartments) is too commoditized and contractor-accessible to generate SE-level margins.
- Sole-stamp dependency on Markulin creates operational fragility.
- National players (Rimkus, Bureau Veritas, Intertek) and volume specialists (DrBalcony, EmpireWorks) have already carved out territory with local staff, systems, and marketing reach.

**Do enter selectively as an add-on to existing CA relationships.** When KOR is already engaged with a property owner, developer, or property management company in SoCal (which the BD data shows is happening), offering to add EEE inspection at the relationship level is defensible:
- A developer who KOR is pitching for structural design on new work might also own older HOA-managed buildings needing SB 326 compliance.
- A property manager relationship (e.g., through an architect referral) could generate a multi-property inspection contract across their portfolio — trip-batching multiple sites on one LA or SD visit eliminates the travel-cost problem.
- The repair design follow-on is the real opportunity: KOR performs the inspection, finds issues (which is statistically likely given wood-frame waterproofing failures are endemic), and captures the engineer-of-record repair design contract. That secondary contract is where the SE margin actually is.

**The right entry move if KOR wants to test this:**
1. **Identify 2–3 warm relationships** (architects, property managers, large HOAs in SD/LA) who need SB 326 compliance.
2. **Batch 3+ sites into a single 2-day SoCal trip** to make travel economics work.
3. **Price for the follow-on:** Offer the inspection at a fee that reflects real costs ($5,000–$8,000 for a medium HOA, transparent about travel surcharge) but explicitly positions KOR to do the repair design.
4. **Do not build a dedicated marketing channel** (website, direct mail to HOAs) for this without a local partner or staffed presence. The volume model requires local scheduling infrastructure that KOR doesn't have.

**If KOR ever opens a SoCal staffed office:** The calculus flips. A local SE doing EEE inspections with local travel is a legitimate recurring-revenue line — the 6/9-year cycle is genuinely valuable as a predictable pipeline, especially if KOR captures the repair design work. At that point, SB 326 (HOA/condo, SE-only) is the target, not SB 721 (commoditized by contractors).
