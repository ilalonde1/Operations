## 3. Strengths and Vulnerabilities

### Strengths

**S1 — Incumbent on the Holland/MVE stack.** Nelson is the named SE on the $290M Phase 1 Gateway Crossing delivery. Holland and MVE have now invested two years of CA/coordination with Nelson on this team. Replacing an SE mid-masterplan has a real switching cost. [Confirmed]

**S2 — Principal-led delivery, both principals licensed SE/CA.** Nelson and Nielsen personally run every project. For a developer paying $290M, having principals in the room is exactly what they want. This mirrors KOR's own value proposition. [Confirmed — principal participation policy from firm website]

**S3 — Deep podium and high-density residential expertise.** Twenty-plus years of podium, concrete, and wood-frame multifamily. Not a generalist; this is their core typology. The ACI award on Brand+Wilson and the Orange/Wilson wood-frame podium confirm technical depth. [Confirmed]

**S4 — Seismic capability.** Thomas Nielsen holds an advanced degree in seismic dynamic analysis (non-linear modeling). For 7-story SDC D/E podium in the South Bay, this is a gating credential. [Confirmed — bio from About Us]

**S5 — Southern California architect network.** Deep in the SoCal AEC ecosystem (Irvine/OC). MVE is also based in Irvine — same geography, likely long relationship pre-dating Gateway Crossing. [Probable — co-located, award-winning collaboration]

### Vulnerabilities

**V1 — Very small firm (11 people) handling a $500M+ masterplan.** Gateway Crossing full build-out is 1,565 units + hotel across 4 buildings. With 11 total staff (not all engineers), concurrent delivery of Phase 2 + hotel + NorCal projects + their SoCal base book creates serious capacity risk. [Probable — size from ZoomInfo, project scale from SFYIMBY]

**V2 — NorCal is not their home market.** Nelson is Irvine-based. The Santa Clara site is 400+ miles away. Site visits, city department relationships, NorCal inspector relationships, and local code nuances (SJ/SC building departments vs. LA/OC) are not their native ground. This is partly why FBA Inc. (Hayward-based NorCal specialist) was co-credited on Phase 1 — Holland/MVE needed local Bay Area coverage. [Probable — inferred from FBA co-credit and geographic base]

**V3 — No public NorCal office or local hire visible.** Unlike MVE, which opened a San Francisco studio specifically for the NorCal push, there is no evidence Nelson has opened a Bay Area presence. Their "multi-office" claim on the website is not backed by any named second location. [Probable — no public secondary office address found]

**V4 — Two-principal bottleneck.** With only two named principals and 11 staff, there is a real concentration risk. If Nelson or Nielsen is pulled by one major project (e.g., Phase 2 Gateway Crossing), the other Holland/MVE seats (Stevens Creek, 2200 Calle de Luna, Irvine Company) may be delayed or require a co-SE. The FBA co-SE pattern on Phase 1 may indicate this is already happening. [Probable — structural inference from size and scope]

**V5 — Fees likely SoCal-benchmarked, not cost-competitive vs. BC labor rates.** A two-principal Irvine firm carries California overhead: liability insurance, union-adjacent labor costs, SoCal market fees. Structural fees for a 500-unit podium typically run $800–$1,500/unit in California, i.e., $400K–$750K. KOR's Vancouver-dollar-adjusted labor can undercut this at equivalent margin. [Probable — fee benchmarks from Palisade Engineering blog; currency differential is structural]

**V6 — No visible high-rise experience above 8–10 stories.** All confirmed projects are 4–8 stories. If any future Holland/MVE NorCal tower grows beyond Type III/V wood-over-concrete into a true high-rise concrete or steel core tower, Nelson may not be the natural lead. [Probable — portfolio inference]

**V7 — Transparent capacity signal already visible.** The fact that FBA Inc. was brought in as a co-SE on Phase 1 (not just civil) is unusual for a principal-led boutique. It suggests Holland or MVE requested local NorCal SE coverage that Nelson alone could not provide end-to-end. This is an opening. [Probable — co-SE role is atypical for a named project SE]

## 4. KOR Competitive Positioning to Win Against Nelson

### The Target Opportunity
KOR's immediate target is open SE seats on the following confirmed Holland/MVE NorCal pipeline:
1. **Stevens Creek Blvd, San Jose** — 540 units, 8-story podium, TCA Architects, approved Dec 2025, moving to construction permitting. SE not publicly named.
2. **2200 Calle de Luna, Santa Clara** — 301 units, 6-story wood-frame over podium, Holland/NASH JV, preliminary plans filed June 2025. SE not public.
3. **Gateway Crossing Phases 2–4** — ~840 units + 225-key hotel. Nelson is the incumbent, but these are separate contracts from Phase 1.
4. **Irvine Company — 2518 Mission College Blvd, Santa Clara** — 1,792 units, 5 five-story buildings, approved March 2025. SE not named; MVE likely as architect based on prior relationship.

### KOR's Differentiators vs. Nelson

**D1 — CA-licensed SE principal, available now.** John Markulin holds a California S.E. license (confirmed via his bio). KOR can stamp California structural documents without a local co-SE. Nelson needs FBA to backstop NorCal; KOR does not. This directly addresses Holland/MVE's demonstrated pain point. [Confirmed — Markulin bio on korstructural.com]

**D2 — Proven high-volume wood-frame-over-podium throughput.** KOR runs a dedicated wood-frame department in Vancouver and has executed hundreds of 4–8 story podium buildings in BC and Alberta under seismic design loads comparable to or exceeding California SDC D. The San Diego portfolio (10 confirmed projects including The Grande twin towers) demonstrates established CA market delivery. [Confirmed — korstructural.com projects page; Markulin bio]

**D3 — Vancouver-dollar cost advantage on fees.** KOR's structural engineering labor is priced in Canadian dollars and paid at BC market rates, which are meaningfully below Southern California all-in staff costs even after exchange adjustment. On a $750K SE fee for a 500-unit building, KOR can price 10–20% below a comparable Irvine boutique at equivalent margin — a real number to a cost-conscious developer. [Probable — BC/CA wage differential is structural; exact advantage requires fee comparison in a specific proposal]

**D4 — Comparable principal-led model, larger bench.** KOR has 40+ professionals (vs. Nelson's 11) including multiple CA-licensed engineers. KOR can offer principal-led engagement with deeper staff capacity for concurrent phases — the exact capacity risk Nelson faces on a four-building masterplan. [Confirmed — korstructural.com firm description; ZoomInfo Nelson headcount]

**D5 — NorCal is KOR's strategic growth market; Nelson's is not.** Holland/MVE need an SE who is invested in the Bay Area market, not an Irvine firm doing NorCal as an extension of a SoCal relationship. KOR is actively targeting NorCal as its US beachhead. A developer wants an SE who will be there for the long pull, not one who flies in from Orange County. [Unverified as a claim to Holland/MVE — but structurally sound as a positioning argument]

**D6 — Seismic depth from BC practice.** Vancouver is one of the highest seismic hazard zones in North America (NBCC Zone 6). KOR routinely designs for ground motions comparable to the worst-case Bay Area scenarios, in a jurisdiction that mandates peer review on all Part 3 buildings. This is a seismic pedigree argument — KOR engineers have more seismic design reps per year than most SoCal podium firms. [Probable — NBCC seismic zone is confirmed high; direct comparison to Nelson's seismic volume is inferred]

### Positioning Play — The Entry Script

The play is not to attack Nelson directly. It is to position KOR as the "dedicated NorCal SE partner" for the Holland/MVE stack, offering:

- Markulin as principal-in-charge with a CA SE stamp
- Dedicated capacity for the NorCal pipeline (not shared with a SoCal backlog)
- Fees benchmarked below SoCal market (BC labor advantage)
- Local-first approach (willing to invest in Bay Area presence/relationships)
- Seismic depth from 25 years of BC high-hazard practice

**Entry point:** Stevens Creek and 2200 Calle de Luna are both pre-permit, no SE publicly locked. TCA Architects (Stevens Creek) is a separate relationship from MVE; this may be an opening where Nelson is not automatically assumed. Approach TCA Architects first — they are not inside the existing Nelson/MVE relationship.

**Incumbent displacement (Gateway Crossing Phases 2–4):** Do not attack Phase 1. Instead, position KOR as capable of freeing Nelson from the NorCal burden — "KOR can take the Bay Area phases; Nelson can focus on your SoCal pipeline." Frame it as capacity allocation, not competition.

### Risks and Honest Caveats

- Nelson has two full years of relationship equity with Holland Partner Group and MVE on a $290M delivery. That is a real moat. Developers do not switch SEs mid-masterplan without cause.
- FBA Inc. is already inside the NorCal SE relationship. FBA is the more natural absorber of future phases if Nelson needs relief, not an outside firm.
- KOR's San Diego portfolio is residential/high-rise (The Grande is 40-story concrete), not podium multifamily. Showing wood-over-podium CA references specifically would strengthen the pitch.
- KOR has no publicly visible Bay Area project history. The first NorCal win will be hardest; the second will cascade.
