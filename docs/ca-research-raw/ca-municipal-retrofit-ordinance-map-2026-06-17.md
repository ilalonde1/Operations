# California Municipal Mandatory Seismic Retrofit Ordinance Map
**Research date:** 2026-06-17
**Purpose:** KOR Structural owner-direct retrofit SE revenue mapping — identifying jurisdictions where mandatory retrofit programs bypass the architect-prime gate and reward seismic SE depth (PBSD/ASCE-41).

---

## Executive Summary

California has **20+ municipalities** with mandatory or near-mandatory seismic retrofit programs. The lane is real and large: ~15,000+ buildings across Southern California alone still require structural engineering work under active ordinances. The highest-value owner-direct opportunities are the non-ductile concrete (NDC) programs — these buildings require licensed SEs (not architects), the fee per engagement is $50K–$500K+, and programs in LA, West Hollywood, and Santa Monica have active compliance deadlines in 2026–2028. The soft-story lane is dominated by smaller fees ($5K–$25K per building) and is partly saturated in mature programs (SF, Oakland, Beverly Hills all past final deadline) but remains open in the newer wave (San Jose, Culver City, Torrance, San Leandro — all in early compliance). A second wave of NDC programs is forming: SF just launched a voluntary-with-mandatory-horizon program (May 2025), LA County introduced a new high-rise ordinance (April 2025), and Sacramento/San Diego remain uncommitted but politically exposed.

## SE Fee Economics

### Non-Ductile Concrete Buildings (NDC)
The NDC lane is where KOR's PBSD/ASCE-41 depth is most differentiated. These are multi-story concrete commercial/residential buildings built pre-1977.

- **Structural assessment (Tier 1 checklist):** $10K–$50K depending on building size and drawing availability [Confirmed — nonductileretrofit.com FAQ]
- **Full ASCE-41 seismic evaluation + report:** $30K–$150K for a mid-size 5–10 story building
- **Retrofit design (full CDs):** $75K–$500K+ for a 10–20 story concrete frame; construction cost basis $50–$100/SF of building area [Confirmed — WJE/SGH data]
- **SE fee as % of construction:** Typically 8–15% of hard cost
- **Construction cost for typical 8-story NDC building (50,000 SF):** $3M–$5M → **SE fee: $240K–$750K per project**
- **Volume in LA alone:** ~1,500 buildings; most are in the 3–25 story range; many are still in the 10-year permit window (due ~2025–2028 for orders issued 2015–2018)

### Soft-Story Buildings (SS)
Lower fee per engagement but high volume and faster turnaround:
- **Screening report (PE-stamped):** $1,500–$5,000 flat per building [Confirmed — h2seismicpro.com]
- **Full SE design + stamped plans:** $5K–$25K for typical 8–24 unit building [Confirmed — various]
- **Total retrofit construction cost:** $60K–$200K per building (SS)
- **SE fee as % of construction:** 8–12%

## Statewide Legislative Movement

### Current State (2026)
**No statewide mandatory retrofit law for soft-story or NDC buildings exists.** California has historically delegated this to municipalities.

Key findings:
1. **AB 2181 (2013–14)** — authorized (not required) cities to adopt NDC retrofit ordinances. This enabled LA, WeHo, Santa Monica programs. Did not mandate statewide action. [Confirmed — leginfo.legislature.ca.gov]
2. **CA URM Law (1986)** — required cities to inventory URM buildings and create loss-reduction programs. Most cities adopted notification-only, not mandatory retrofit. [Confirmed — CA SSC]
3. **No pending 2025–2026 statewide bill identified** for mandatory soft-story or NDC retrofit across all jurisdictions. Searches for AB/SB 2025–2026 statewide mandate returned no hits beyond city-specific programs. [Probable — search coverage may be incomplete]
4. **LA County new ordinance (Apr 2025)** — Introduced mandatory NDC high-rise retrofit for unincorporated LA County. If adopted, adds coverage beyond city limits. [Confirmed — LA County press release]
5. **San Francisco concrete program (May 2025)** — Board of Supervisors passed a screening+voluntary program for NDC/RWFD. Explicitly stated voluntary retrofits get a 20-year exemption from any future mandatory requirement — implying mandatory is expected. [Confirmed — SF onesanfrancisco.org]
6. **Political pressure post-Northridge + Turkey/Syria 2023 earthquakes** — renewed public interest, but statewide mandate remains politically contested due to cost burden on property owners.

**Verdict:** A statewide mandate is not imminent, but the direction of travel is clear. The San Jose ordinance (2025) and San Leandro ordinance (2024) are the newest wave — expect more Bay Area cities to follow in 2026–2028. SF's "voluntary today, mandatory tomorrow" framing for NDC is the likely template for a 2027–2030 statewide push.

## KOR Positioning Notes (Owner-Direct SE Lane)

1. **NDC is the sweet spot for PBSD/ASCE-41 depth.** Soft-story is primarily prescriptive (ASCE 7 lateral + prescriptive CEBC Appendix A4). NDC requires nonlinear analysis, ASCE 41 performance objectives, and peer review — exactly where KOR's seismic credentials differentiate from generalist firms.

2. **The owner-direct relationship is structural.** Under LA, WeHo, Santa Monica, and San Jose ordinances, the building owner receives the Order to Comply directly from the city. The owner's first call is typically to an SE for the evaluation, not to an architect. This bypasses the prime-consultant gate.

3. **SE-as-prime on NDC.** For NDC retrofit projects, the SE is typically the prime professional of record (not the architect). The architect may coordinate, but the structural engineer leads the project — fundamentally different from new-construction work where KOR is a sub.

4. **John Markulin's CA license is the gate.** All CA SE work requires a CA-licensed engineer. Markulin's licensure makes KOR eligible to sign and stamp CA work; this is not a generic resource, it's a specific credential asset to be deployed strategically.

5. **Competitor landscape:** Major incumbents in CA NDC work include Degenkolb, KPFF, SGH, WJE, ZFA. They focus on large high-rises and institutional. The mid-market (4–12 story concrete frame, $300K–$5M construction value) is less covered. KOR's entry point should be this mid-tier with PBSD depth as the differentiator.

6. **Fee compression risk in soft-story.** The soft-story screening/design market has commoditized in mature cities (SF, Oakland, LA). $1,500 PE-stamped screening reports from high-volume shops squeeze margins. KOR should avoid the commoditized screening tier and focus on buildings that failed screening and need full ASCE-41 evaluation + design — or on NDC work entirely.
