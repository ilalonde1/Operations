What I'm building, in one paragraph

A tool that reads our own structural drawing sets — the PDF stick files we already issue — and produces an ETABS model: columns, walls, slabs, grid lines and storeys, with no Revit and no re-drawing. Today an engineer rebuilds that model by hand. When Andrea first saw the output: "if I have that, it's going to take me like no time to rebuild it." The long-term value is the reissue workflow — the architect sends an update, the tool says what changed. No AEC firm I know of has this; the commercial CSiXRevit route never worked for us, and this one is made of our own drafting conventions.

Where it stands — measured, not estimated

ARCHIVE

253 of 296 build

Every current stick file in our archive runs through the tool on every change, in under an hour. 253 (85%) produce a model from the PDF alone; the 43 that don't say why in a ledger — 17 are copies of another job's file, 17 have no plan the reader can type, 8 name no storey.

ACCURACY

59% of columns within 100 mm

Against the engineers' own ETABS models on 51 jobs — and most of those models are older than the drawings, so the true figure is higher. The bigger gap is slabs: present on 41% of storeys.

RIGOUR

Nothing lives in anyone's head

Every rule the reader applies is code with a test, or a row in our standards database (86 rows). Adversarially audited four times by an independent AI reviewer; the last audit's eleven findings were fixed the same day.

What "usable" means, in the engineers' own words

We have the verticals everywhere; slab outlines on 41% of storeys — that gap is the next two weeks.
What their answers already settledFootings aren't modelled; basement and retaining walls are; beams aren't (headers are); each level shown once; one file per building — each now a rule the tool obeys; nothing is re-asked.
