How a detail lives

1 · FIND

Find it

Type a number or a word in search and press Enter, or click a discipline chip. Click the row — the real drawing appears on the right. Click it to zoom.

2 · REVIEW

Take it to Bluebeam

Open PDF opens the one you picked. Open list as PDF opens everything in the list as one file, a page per detail. Mark up there. No Revit needed.

3 · DECIDE

Approve or reject

Approve puts it in the drafters' palette. Reject keeps it out until it's fixed — nothing is deleted. Fixes are made in Revit by the gatekeeper, then published.

Cheat sheet — I want to…

Find a detailSearch box → number or title → Enter, or a chip (Concrete Steel General Wood Frame) or All types ▾. Click the row to see the drafters' actual view; click it to zoom.
Open one in BluebeamSelect it → Open PDF. It opens in your default PDF viewer, ready to mark up.
Review a whole setFilter the list (say Concrete) → Open list as PDF. One file: an index, then a page per detail. Built from the live store when you click — no package to keep track of.
Say it's goodApprove — it becomes placeable and shows up in the drafters' Revit palette.
Say it's notReject — it stays out of the master until the gatekeeper fixes it in Revit. Put what's wrong on your Bluebeam markup.
Reclassify itChange Type ▾ → Typical detail · Custom detail · Note / schedule. Saves the instant you pick (you'll see ✓ Saved).
Build my own sheetTools ▾ → Compose Sheet… → Add details, drag them into place, name it → Create PDF sheet. Yours to keep, stamped as a working copy.
Change the masterGatekeeper only: Save to master in the composer (the sheet number is assigned for you) and Tools ▾ → Publish to Master….

What everything means

Details — one drawing: a footing, a connection, a section. Numbered KOR-D-#####.
Sheets tab — general notes and schedules: a page, not a picture. Same buttons.
Parts tab — what drafters drop into Revit from Quick Insert.
Typical / Custom / Note — on most jobs; job-specific; a note or schedule.
Master — the Revit template every job starts from. Approved details only.
Gatekeeper — the one person who edits in Revit and publishes the master.

Status key

Approved in the drafters' palette
Pending not reviewed yet
On hold vetted, but its size variants differ — not placeable until split
Rejected out of the master until fixed

Approve, Reject and Change Type need approver access. Publishing needs the gatekeeper.

The one habit

Find it here, not in an old job.

Mark up in Bluebeam. Decide in the app. The fix happens in Revit, once, by the gatekeeper — and every drafter gets it on the next job.
